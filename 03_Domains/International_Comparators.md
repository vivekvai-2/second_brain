# **International Comparators**

**Type:** Domain **Status:** Active — promoted from stub 2026-08-02 **Last Updated:** 2026-08-02

> **Promotion note.** This domain was a 174-word stub created during the 2026-07-31 link-hygiene audit, which asked whether it should be folded into [[03_Domains/AI_Governance]] or [[03_Domains/Systemic_Regulatory_Governance]] on the grounds that only two nodes used it. That question is now answered: **keep it separate.** Twenty `International_*` corpus nodes plus two fsQCA registries already sit beneath it, and it performs an analytical function neither parent domain performs — it holds the comparative baseline against which Indian instruments are diagnosed. Folding it into AI_Governance would bury the comparative layer inside the substantive one.

---

## **Core Synthesis**

The comparative layer of the PRIS corpus does two jobs that are usually conflated and should not be. It supplies **architectural archetypes** — the distinct structural forms AI regulation takes across jurisdictions — against which India's fragmented multi-regulator arrangement can be characterised rather than merely described. And it supplies **convergence evidence** — instances where independent regulators arrive at identical design choices without citation chains, which is the strongest available support for treating regulatory design as structurally determined rather than idiosyncratic.

The domain's most consequential internal finding is a mismatch between its two halves. The corpus-node base is overwhelmingly **Western and multilateral-financial**: of twenty `International_*` nodes, roughly eleven originate from BIS, FSB, IOSCO, the Bank of England, or MAS, and the remainder skew EU, US, and Australia. The fsQCA case base is the opposite — the twenty-case shortlist at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/fsQCA_Case_Registry]] contains almost no G7 jurisdiction, consisting instead of UAE, Singapore, India, Brazil, Indonesia, Chile, Nigeria, Philippines, Peru, Egypt, Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, Colombia, Kenya, South Africa, Mexico, Argentina, Ghana and Bangladesh. **The jurisdictions the corpus knows most deeply are not the jurisdictions the thesis proposes to compare.** This is a live methodological exposure and the domain's first-order open question.

---

## **Scope and Boundary**

The domain covers regulatory instruments, supervisory frameworks, and standard-setting outputs issued outside India whose analytical value lies in comparison rather than in their own jurisdictional content. It includes binding statutes (EU AI Act), administrative measures (China's CAC Interim Measures), executive instruments (US Executive Orders), national strategies and plans (Australia), multilateral normative instruments (OECD, UNESCO, G20), and financial-sector supervisory output (BIS, FSB, IOSCO, Bank of England, MAS).

It excludes substantive AI-governance analysis that does not turn on cross-jurisdictional comparison — that belongs in [[03_Domains/AI_Governance]] — and excludes structural analysis of the Indian regulatory system itself, which belongs in [[03_Domains/Systemic_Regulatory_Governance]]. Where a node does both, it is tagged to both, as [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_22_EU_AI_Act_Conformity_Assessment_Notified_Bodies]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_21_China_CAC_GenAI_Interim_Measures_2023]] already are.

## **Key Debates**

### **Three architectural archetypes, not a maturity spectrum**

The corpus supports a typological rather than ordinal reading of comparative AI regulation. **Horizontal binding statute** — the EU AI Act, risk-tiered, cross-sectoral, with a conformity-assessment and notified-body apparatus (International_22). **Administrative interim measures** — China's CAC framework, service-provider-facing, issued at speed and revisable, trading legal durability for regulatory tempo (International_21). **Fragmented executive and soft law** — the US pattern across [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_5]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_6]], and the Australian plan-plus-tool pattern across [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_16_Australia_National_AI_Plan_2025]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_20_Australia_DTA_AI_Impact_Assessment_Tool]], where governance is carried by strategy documents and assessment instruments rather than enforceable rules.

Treating these as points on a maturity line — the implicit assumption in most policy commentary — obscures that they represent different answers to a prior question about where regulatory authority should sit. India's arrangement is not an immature version of any of them; it is a fourth type, in which sectoral regulators each build a vertical stack and no horizontal layer exists. This typological claim is the domain's principal contribution to [[02_Projects/P4_Doctrinal_IJLIT]].

### **Convergence without citation**

The corpus contains a documented instance of three independent regulators reaching identical accountability architecture without any citation chain: IOSCO's capital-markets AI report, SEBI's Reg. 16C sole-responsibility provision, and RBI's digital-lending accountability retention all landed in 2025 on the principle that the regulated entity bears non-delegable accountability for AI outputs regardless of vendor origin — recorded at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_19_IOSCO_AI_Capital_Markets_CR_2025]] as the strongest three-way [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Signature/Institutional_Coherence]] finding in the corpus. Whether this reflects genuine institutional isomorphism (DiMaggio & Powell 1983, per [[02_Projects/Shared_Anchors/DiMaggio_Powell_1983]]) or convergent response to a shared technical constraint is unresolved and is a legitimate theoretical question.

### **The financial-sector overweight**

Because roughly half the international corpus originates from financial supervisory bodies, comparative claims drawn from it carry an unexamined sectoral prior. Financial regulators share a supervisory grammar — model risk, third-party risk, operational resilience, incident reporting — that is not representative of how AI is governed in health, education, or public administration. Any claim of the form "international practice converges on X" that rests on BIS, FSB, IOSCO and MAS sources is really a claim about *financial* international practice. This is a boundary condition that should be stated explicitly in P1 and P4 rather than discovered by a reviewer.

### **Whether comparative material can bear normative weight**

The corpus's comparative content is predominantly descriptive-institutional. Using it to argue what India *should* do requires a transplant argument the corpus does not currently supply — see the regulatory-theory anchor at [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F095_Drahos_Regulatory_Theory_Foundations_Applications_2017]]. The domain should be used to characterise India's position, not to recommend a foreign model, until that argument is built.

## **Empirical Base**

Twenty `International_*` corpus nodes plus two fsQCA companion registries, spanning 2021–2026.

**By issuing-body type.** Multilateral financial and standard-setting bodies dominate — BIS across [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_2]], [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_7]], [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_8]], [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_9]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_13]]; FSB across [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_11]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_15]]; plus IOSCO, the Bank of England at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_10]], and MAS at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_3]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_4]]. Normative multilateral instruments are represented by [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_1]] (OECD), [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_18_UNESCO_Recommendation_Ethics_AI_2021]], and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_17_G20_South_Africa_Leaders_Declaration_2025]]. National instruments cover the EU, China, the US and Australia.

**By fsQCA readiness.** The twenty-case registry scores each jurisdiction on Legal, Enforcement, Coverage and Time (0–2 each, 0–8 total). The distribution is compressed: one Advanced case (Vietnam, 7), a large Intermediate cluster scoring 5–6, and four Emerging cases (South Africa 1, Bangladesh 1, Ghana 2, Mexico 3). **Compression at the top is a design problem** — with a single Advanced case, configurational analysis of what produces advanced maturity has almost no variance to work with. OQ-09 (case selection, overdue since 31 May 2026 per [[00_MOC/Research_Dashboard]]) should be resolved with this in view.

## **Dominant Theoretical Frames**

**Institutional isomorphism** — DiMaggio and Powell (1983), held at [[02_Projects/Shared_Anchors/DiMaggio_Powell_1983]], supplies the mechanism for convergence-without-citation and distinguishes coercive, mimetic and normative pathways, which the corpus can in principle discriminate between.

**Regulatory theory and regime complexity** — Drahos (2017) and Turk (2020), at [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F095_Drahos_Regulatory_Theory_Foundations_Applications_2017]] and [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Turk_Overlapping_Legal_Rules_Financial_Regulation_2020]], frame overlapping international and national rule systems as a structural condition rather than a defect.

**Configurational comparative method** — Ragin (2008) and Fiss (2011), at [[02_Projects/Shared_Anchors/Ragin_2008]] and [[02_Projects/Shared_Anchors/Fiss_2011]], supply the logic by which cross-jurisdictional comparison yields equifinal pathways rather than single best models.

**Comparative institutional analysis of AI ethics** — Jobin, Ienca and Vayena (2019) and Correa and Kluge (2023), at [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019]] and [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F121_Correa_Kluge_Worldwide_AI_Ethics_200_Guidelines_2023]], establish the precedent for large-N comparative treatment of governance instruments.

## **Open Research Questions**

**OQ-IC-01 (blocking).** Can the fsQCA case base be reconciled with the corpus-node base? The thesis proposes to compare twenty largely Global-South jurisdictions using a corpus built predominantly from Western and multilateral-financial sources. Either the case base narrows toward jurisdictions the corpus actually covers, or corpus intake extends to the shortlisted jurisdictions. This decision is prior to OQ-09 and should be taken with it.

**OQ-IC-02.** Does the three-archetype typology survive contact with the full twenty-case registry, or do the Global-South strategy-led jurisdictions constitute a fourth archetype distinct from the US/Australia soft-law pattern?

**OQ-IC-03.** Is the 2025 IOSCO/SEBI/RBI accountability convergence coercive, mimetic, or normative isomorphism? Discriminating between these requires evidence on the drafting process that the corpus does not yet hold.

**OQ-IC-04.** How does [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Standard/Retention_Horizon_Divergence]] present internationally? The EU's five- and ten-year horizons are the only non-Indian figures currently coded; whether other jurisdictions show comparable internal divergence is untested.
