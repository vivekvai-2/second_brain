# **Institutional Coherence**

**Type:** Signature Construct**Status:** Active**Last Updated:** 2026-07-14 (Cross-Institutional Comparison + Batch 15 continuation additions)

---

## **Definition (Researcher’s Formulation)**

Institutional Coherence is a property of regulatory architecture defined as the degree to which AI governance instruments within a jurisdiction are internally consistent, mutually referencing, and non-contradictory in their allocation of obligations, enforcement responsibilities, and accountability structures. The construct operates at the level of governance systems rather than organisations, firms, or individual regulators. Unlike organisational or policy coherence, Institutional Coherence concerns the structural design of regulatory instruments and the relationships among them, rather than the alignment of organisational activities or policy objectives.

## **Dimensions**

1. **Cross-referencing** — Governance instruments explicitly cite, defer to, or acknowledge related instruments operating within the same regulatory domain.
2. **Jurisdictional clarity** — Enforcement responsibilities and regulatory authority are allocated clearly, without significant overlap, ambiguity, or governance gaps.
3. **Obligation consistency** — Comparable governance requirements employ consistent terminology, scope definitions, thresholds, and compliance expectations across instruments.
4. **Coordination mechanisms** — Formal arrangements exist to facilitate regulatory coordination, including joint circulars, unified governance codes, inter-agency bodies, or equivalent mechanisms.

## **Refined Distinction (2026-07-14): Horizontal Infrastructure Coherence vs. Vertical Substantive Incoherence**

Analysis of the Indian regulatory space in [[Cross_Institutional_Mandate_Comparison]] §9 and §13 reveals that Institutional Coherence is not a binary state and can exist at one layer of a regulatory architecture while being absent at another:

- **Horizontal Infrastructure Coherence** — coherence achieved at the shared technical or operational substrate that underpins multiple regulators (e.g., [[CERT_IN]]'s standardisation of the cyber-auditor market via the empanelment scheme, which SEBI, MeitY, and other regulators rely on). This form of coherence emerges from technical standardisation rather than substantive rule alignment.
- **Vertical Substantive Incoherence** — the parallel, non-aligned rule-making at the sectoral layer that sits atop the shared infrastructure (e.g., RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and MeitY each issuing binding AI/ML mandates without mutual cross-reference, even where they share CERT-In's audit infrastructure).

The Indian case demonstrates that horizontal infrastructure coherence at the audit layer does not translate into vertical substantive coherence at the rule-making layer. This distinction refines fsQCA calibration: jurisdictions with high horizontal infrastructure coherence but low vertical substantive coherence occupy an intermediate SR position (approximately 0.33–0.50), distinct from jurisdictions where both layers are absent (0.00) or both are present (1.00).

## **Role in fsQCA Model**

Institutional Coherence corresponds to the Systemic Regulatory (SR) condition within the approved four-condition fsQCA model comprising EA × SR × RL × SI. The construct is calibrated using four anchor points: 0.00 where no coherence indicators are present, 0.33 where only partial cross-referencing exists, 0.67 where cross-referencing and jurisdictional clarity are present, and 1.00 where all four coherence dimensions are observable. Within the fsQCA design, Institutional Coherence is treated as a causal condition whose configurational relationship with legitimate AI governance outcomes remains to be established empirically. Whether the construct functions as a necessary condition, a sufficient condition, or a component of multiple sufficient configurations remains explicitly open pending completion of the analysis.

## **Empirical Application**

Institutional Coherence serves as a unifying construct across multiple components of the research programme. In P2, the records-rules asymmetry identified within India’s Digital Public Infrastructure governance architecture is interpreted as evidence of reduced coherence in citizen-facing governance arrangements, where participants are heavily represented as records but weakly represented as rule-bearing actors. In P4, the construct functions as a normative benchmark against which the Indian BFSI AI governance architecture is evaluated, with the absence of cross-references between RBI and SEBI instruments indicating a significant coherence deficit. Within the fsQCA chapter, Institutional Coherence is operationalised as a calibrated causal condition across ten jurisdictional cases, enabling comparative analysis of how regulatory architecture contributes to legitimate governance outcomes.

## **Distinction From Adjacent Constructs**

|**Construct**|**Defining Feature**|**Key Difference From Institutional Coherence**|
|---|---|---|
|Policy Coherence|Alignment of policy goals across government departments|Institutional Coherence is regulatory-instrument-level, not policy-goal-level; it concerns obligation structure, not intent|
|Regulatory Convergence|Regulators producing equivalent outcomes through independent action|Convergence is an outcome; Institutional Coherence is a structural property that enables or prevents coordination|
|Coordination|Active regulatory collaboration mechanisms|Coordination is one dimension of Institutional Coherence, not a synonym; a system can have coordination mechanisms without achieving coherence|

## **Theoretical Positioning**

Institutional Coherence engages directly with Institutional Grammar 2.0 by identifying a structural property that can be assessed through ADICO-based analysis of governance instruments. The presence or absence of cross-references between coded institutional statements provides a direct empirical indicator of coherence within a regulatory architecture. The construct also draws on regulatory governance theory, particularly scholarship concerned with jurisdictional boundary design and the allocation of regulatory authority. It complements regime complexity theory by providing a domestic-level analytical construct for evaluating interactions between governance instruments within a single jurisdiction. Across the PRIS research programme, Institutional Coherence functions as the normative benchmark against which [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] and [[Governance_Debt]] are interpreted as structural deficits in regulatory design.

**Theoretical-foundations note (added 2026-07-15):** [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F095_Drahos_Regulatory_Theory_Foundations_Applications_2017|F095]] (Drahos, ed., *Regulatory Theory: Foundations and Applications*, ANU Press, 2017) supplies the general regulatory-theory vocabulary underlying this construct — specifically **nodal governance** (Wood & Shearing's "organisational sites where attempts are made to intentionally shape the flow of events") as the theoretical basis for describing India's multiple independent AI-governance nodes (RBI, SEBI, MeitY, CERT-In, NCAIC, judiciary), and **meta-regulation** ("regulating self-regulation") as the theoretical frame for NCAIC's proposed coordinating role over the other regulators. This is a theory-grounding reference, not an empirical "Instance in Corpus" — see F095's own scope note for why it is not listed in the section below.

**Theoretical-foundations note (added 2026-07-16):** [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F122_Scott_Institutional_Theory_Research_Program_2004|F122]] (Scott, W. R., *Institutional Theory: Contributing to a Theoretical Research Program*, 2004) supplies the foundational sociological-institutionalist vocabulary this construct draws on. Scott's **Pillars framework** (regulative/normative/cultural-cognitive elements) situates Institutional_Coherence's four dimensions closest to the regulative pillar, while the construct's recurring "form vs. substance" finding (F094, F103, F121) can be read through Scott's **decoupling** concept (Meyer & Rowan 1977; Tolbert & Zucker 1996) — formal compliance structures adopted for legitimacy ("rationalized myths") diverging from structures that generate genuine operational action. DiMaggio & Powell's (1983) coercive/normative/mimetic isomorphism taxonomy offers a mechanism-level explanation for *why* several Regulatory_Parallelism instances converge without coordination (see that concept's parallel note). This is a theory-grounding reference, not an empirical "Instance in Corpus" — consistent with F095's precedent.

- (Batch 14 — EU Digital Decade TPRM coherence challenge / India regulatory fragmentation as inverse) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F045_PwC_TPRM_Digital_Geopolitical_Era_2025]] — PwC Netherlands TPRM in the Digital and Geopolitical Era (November 2025): "Good TPRM → Great TPRM" framework reveals a multi-instrument coherence challenge in the EU — DORA (January 2025), NIS2 (October 2024), DSA (February 2024), and AI Act (2024/2026) each impose TPRM-adjacent obligations on the same entities without an integrated compliance architecture. PwC's "Great TPRM" recommendation — integrated multi-framework compliance across all four instruments — is an enterprise-level institutional coherence mechanism. India's TPRM coherence problem is the inverse: no equivalent multi-instrument architecture exists (BFSI-specific obligations in RBI_2 IT Outsourcing, RBI FREE-AI, SEBI AI circulars, and MeitY DPDP rules are distributed, unintegrated, and without a coordination framework), producing regulatory fragmentation without the EU's underlying common market coherence architecture or DORA-equivalent resolution mechanism. For fsQCA SR calibration: EU = high SR on multi-instrument TPRM coherence dimension; India = low SR on same dimension, with the additional disadvantage that India's fragmentation occurs without the common market architecture the EU has available for coherence-mechanism design.
- (Batch 15 — RACE architecture as coherence technical enabler) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Butler_Brooks_Operational_Risk_Paradigm_Change_2024]] (F049) — indirect relevance: the proposed Risk Accounting Calculation Engine (RACE) and AI-enabled Enterprise Data Fabric address the data fragmentation problem that prevents G-SIB banks from achieving BCBS 239 compliance. This architecture acts as a technical enabler for substantive 3LoD execution and multi-instrument coherence at the enterprise execution layer.
- (Batch 15 — Intra-organisational framework limitation) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Papagiannidis_Mikalef_Conboy_RAI_Governance_Framework_2025]] (F050) — conceptual limitation: the paper's framework is restricted to intra-organisational governance practices (structural, procedural, and relational) and does not address how firms navigate coherence across conflicting or uncoordinated regulatory mandates from different authorities. This conceptual gap establishes the multi-regulator coherence problem as a genuine research contribution of P3.
- (Batch 15 continuation — Cross-border AIIRS interoperability as IC failure mode) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/RAND_GovAI_AI_Incident_Reporting_Institutional_Design]] (F051) — Wei/Anderljung/Shevlane/Siegmann: cross-border AI incidents require interoperable national AIIRS. ICAO aviation framework cited as gold standard for cross-border incident management. IC condition failure mode: where national AIIRS systems use incompatible definitions, taxonomies, or reporting formats, cross-border learning is suppressed. India's absence of any AIIRS means IC on this dimension = 0; even a nationally coherent architecture would face interoperability failure with EU AI Act notification format and China GPAI reporting scheme.
- (Batch 15 continuation — Layer-separated governance requires IC coordination) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Paladin_GI_AI_Tech_Stack_Primer]] (F052) — Walden & Lynch: layer-separated governance bodies (export controls regulator vs AI safety regulator vs sector regulator) require coordination mechanisms to avoid contradictory interventions. Absent coordination, layer-aware regulation can generate contradictory obligations — model licence requires open evaluation, sector deployment requires closed operation. IC dimension failure mode is contradiction between layer-specific instruments. India: Layer 1 (MeitY compute policy) + Layer 3 (sectoral regulators for BFSI/health) without Layer 2 body = partial layer coverage with IC undefined.
- (Batch 15 continuation — Multi-framework integration as IC test in Australia) [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_20_Australia_DTA_AI_Impact_Assessment_Tool]] — DTA AIIA Tool integrates with Privacy Act 1988 DPIA, Cyber Security Framework, and Commonwealth Procurement Framework. IC question: whether Australian government agencies navigate these three distinct frameworks coherently, or whether AIIA compliance produces DPIA/procurement contradictions. The AIIA design explicitly attempts multi-framework IC — a positive Institutional Coherence design instance at government-as-deployer layer. India comparator: no equivalent multi-framework integration attempt for government AI systems; DPDP DPIA (draft rules) + IT Act obligations + sectoral rules operate in parallel without integration architecture.
- (Batch 15 continuation — Four-way accountability-attribution convergence, extends Batch 12 three-way) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Cross_Institutional_Mandate_Comparison]] §6.4 documents that DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 13(3) SDF algorithmic due diligence + [[07_Institutions/Corpus/SEBI/SEBI_14]] Reg 16C + [[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_7]] LSP due diligence + [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_19_IOSCO_AI_Capital_Markets_CR_2025]] IOSCO non-delegable accountability principle constitute a **four-way convergence** on unconditional attribution of liability to the regulated entity for automated/algorithmic outputs. Extends the Batch 12 IOSCO+SEBI_14+RBI_7 three-way convergence by adding DPDP (horizontal data authority) as a fourth converging instrument. The strongest independent-convergence signal in the corpus; conjunction of global standard-setter + horizontal data authority + two sectoral regulators arriving at identical accountability architecture without cross-reference or coordination = maximum evidence of convergent regulatory design.
- (Batch 15 continuation — Horizontal infrastructure vs vertical substantive coherence) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Cross_Institutional_Mandate_Comparison]] §9 and §13 introduces the analytical distinction between horizontal infrastructure coherence (CERT-In empanelment scheme standardises the cyber-auditor market for all downstream regulators) and vertical substantive incoherence (RBI, SEBI, MeitY, IRDAI maintain parallel non-aligned rules atop that shared infrastructure). Refines the Institutional Coherence construct into a two-layer analytical model applicable to fsQCA SR calibration.
- (2026-07-16 — Independent governance-theory derivation of coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F103_Johns_Compton_Data_Jurisdictions_Algorithmic_Regulation_2019|F103]] — Johns & Compton (*Regulation & Governance*, 2022) argue that formal cross-jurisdictional standard-setting (naming the WEF's "public-private data collaboration" model) has limited effect if the underlying data formats/infrastructures encode divergent normative commitments — "those instruments may have little effect." Arrived at via governance theory rather than empirical survey, this independently reaches the same coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance distinction [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]] documents empirically — a second, methodologically independent route to the identical conclusion.
- (2026-07-16 — P2 records-rules asymmetry sharpened by a named, cited construct) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F107_Dechamps_Simonofski_Burnay_Citizen_Centricity_Typology_2025|F107]] — Dechamps, Simonofski & Burnay (*Government Information Quarterly* 42, 2025) find that "User and Governance understandings [of citizen-centricity] are necessary but not sufficient premises for Design and End-result understandings" — a DPI-general system can score well on End-result citizen-centricity (efficient transactional service delivery) while remaining structurally weak on User/Governance-level citizen-centricity (citizens as participating, rule-shaping actors). Independently sharpens P2's existing records-rules asymmetry finding (citizens heavily represented as records/end-result-recipients, weakly represented as rule-bearing/Governance-level actors) with a peer-reviewed, empirically-derived construct rather than an ad hoc observation.
- (2026-07-16 — Second corroborating source, larger sample) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F108_Tai_Open_Government_Research_Decade_SLR_2021|F108]] — Tai's 189-study PRISMA systematic review of open government research (2008–2019) finds that 75% of empirical studies focus on the technology/transparency ("vision") dimension versus only 12% on participation and 4% on collaboration ("voice") — a second, independently-sampled, quantified corroboration of the same "vision without voice" asymmetry F107 documents, this time at nearly 4x the sample size.
- (2026-07-16 — Positive counter-example: asymmetry is a design choice, not structural inevitability) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F110_Ansari_Barati_Martin_OGD_Visualization_Review_2021|F110]] — the Cape Town "Day Zero" water-crisis case (Van Belle & Hlabano 2019): a multi-channel OGD visualization strategy (public Day Zero broadcast + suburb-level usage stats + household-level map visualization + crowdsourced real-time reporting) drove a 50% reduction in citizen water consumption within a month — the corpus's clearest quantified instance of citizens activated as participatory, behaviour-changing actors rather than passive record-subjects. A deliberate positive counterfactual to the F107/F108 asymmetry finding: closing the records-rules gap is achievable and consequential when systems are designed to do so.
- (2026-07-16 — Theoretical vocabulary for the whole cluster + second-order finding) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F111_Dekker_Bekkers_Government_Responsiveness_Virtual_Public_Sphere_2015|F111]] — Dekker & Bekkers supply the named theoretical apparatus (Fraser's 1992 strong/weak publics, via Miège's 2010 three-tier elite/mainstream/societal public-sphere model) for the citizens-as-records-vs-rule-bearing-actors distinction F107/F108/F110 document only empirically: the "societal sphere" (weak public, "private, unorganized citizens remote from decision-making centers") is precisely the records position; the "elite sphere" (strong public, linked to decision-making) is precisely the rule-bearing-actor position. Also supplies a second-order finding: across 39 studies, institutional factors (76.3% of explanatory focus) dominate over participation-channel characteristics (34.2%) in explaining actual government responsiveness — meaning closing the records-rules gap requires institutional redesign, not merely adding participation channels.
- (2026-07-16 — Obligation-consistency: administrative practice can exceed formal legal obligation) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F113_Kempeneer_Pirannejad_Wolswinkel_OGD_Legal_Perspective_AI_SLR_2023|F113]] — a replicated Dutch field experiment finds that Freedom of Information requests triggered proactive disclosure beyond what the law strictly required ("concordance" vs. "strict compliance") — evidence that the gap between legal text and administrative reality this construct's obligation-consistency dimension tracks can run toward *over*-implementation, not only the under-implementation pattern the corpus's Indian regulatory-instrument evidence typically documents.
- (2026-07-16 — Third independent convergent "participation ≠ value" finding) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F114_Karkin_Cezar_Public_Value_EParticipation_Synthesis_2024|F114]] — Karkin & Cezar's three-form e-participation taxonomy (managerial one-way / consultative two-way / multi-way participatory, Reddick 2011) extends the cluster's participation-depth vocabulary; their central finding — "e-participation projects have significant potential to create public value *conditional upon* endogenous and exogenous variables" — is a third independent convergent data point (alongside F107's necessity finding and F111's institutional-factor-dominance finding) that participation infrastructure alone does not guarantee governance value without institutional/contextual conditions being met.
- (2026-07-16 — Extended to vulnerable populations specifically) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F116_Liu_Zhou_Liang_Digital_Inclusion_Vulnerable_Groups_2025|F116]] — Liu, Zhou & Liang's Public Service Ecosystem goal-action framework (policy/infrastructure/channel/design/data inclusion) and "warm experts" (informal family/friend digital-access intermediaries) construct extend the records-rules asymmetry specifically to vulnerable populations, who plausibly generate the most "records" (biometric/enrolment data) while having the least Governance-level participation capacity — directly resonant with observed informal-intermediary patterns in India's actual DPI usage (Aadhaar/UPI assisted transactions).
- (2026-07-16 — Normative complication, not corroboration) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F117_Weigl_Roth_Amard_Zavolokina_Public_Values_UserCentricity_Collide_2024|F117]] — Weigl, Roth, Amard & Zavolokina identify four structural value conflicts user-centricity generates: user focus-representation, user focus-pluralism, user involvement-accountability, and user involvement-inclusiveness. The eighth node in the P2 records-rules asymmetry cluster, and the first to complicate rather than corroborate or extend the cluster's implicit "more participation closes the gap" framing — user involvement can itself conflict with representation, pluralism, official accountability, and (via resource/literacy barriers) inclusiveness. Any P2 recommendation to expand citizen voice should explicitly address which of these four conflicts it risks triggering.
- (2026-07-16 — Sharpest coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance evidence in the corpus, direct F094 successor) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F121_Correa_Kluge_Worldwide_AI_Ethics_200_Guidelines_2023|F121]] — Corrêa et al. (*Patterns*, Cell Press, 2023), reviewing 200 AI ethics guidelines (2.4x [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]]'s 84-document sample), name and document the specific mechanisms by which formally convergent principles fail to constrain practice under a "Hidden costs and divergences" analysis: divergent operational definitions of the same principle-label across issuers (e.g., "fairness" as demographic parity vs. equalised odds vs. individual fairness, with no cross-document reconciliation), absent enforcement mechanisms for the 98% soft-law majority of the corpus, and selective principle adoption (issuers cite convergent labels while omitting the specific operational commitments — audit rights, redress, liability allocation — that would make them enforceable). Where F094 documents "substantive divergence in interpretation" and F103 reaches the same conclusion independently via governance theory, F121 is the first corpus node to make the *mechanism* of the coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance gap, not merely its existence, the explicit object of analysis. Also supplies a four-dimension document-classification taxonomy (Nature/Form of regulation/Normative strength/Impact scope) directly reusable for a future PRIS corpus-wide meta-analysis.
- (2026-07-16 — Layer-conflation as a sharper coherence-gap diagnostic) [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F124_Viljanen_Parviainen_AI_Regulatory_Strata_2022|F124]] — Viljanen & Parviainen's five-layer AI law stratigraphy (data rules / application-specific AI rules / general AI rules / application-specific non-AI rules / general non-AI rules), demonstrated through an exceptionally granular Finnish hiring-AI case study citing thirteen-plus independently sourced statutes, offers a sharper coherence-gap diagnostic than simple cross-referencing presence/absence: whether each governance instrument operates at its *correct* layer. Re-readable against India's own stack (MeitY general-AI guidelines / sectoral application-specific mandates / DPDP data-layer rules / general non-discrimination law) as a candidate refinement to fsQCA SR calibration — a genuinely new analytical axis (layer-correctness) distinct from the corpus's existing cross-regulator and intra-jurisdictional-layering (International_21/22, F106) parallelism variants.

## **Committee-Level Coherence Does Not Produce Output-Level Coherence — China (2026-08-04)**

[[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F139_ConcordiaAI_State_of_AI_Safety_China_2026]] contains both a strong positive coherence instance and its own refutation, which makes it unusually useful for specifying what the construct actually measures.

**Coherence by design.** TC260 established a dedicated AI Safety Working Group (WG9) in March 2026 whose leadership deliberately spans four institutions: chaired by Shanghai AI Lab's director, with vice-chairs from CNCERT (incident response, under CAC), CAICT (research institute under MIIT), the MPS Third Research Institute (security testing), and CNITSEC (operates the national vulnerability database). This is cross-institutional composition as an explicit design choice — structurally the same move as IRDAI's WG-AI, which [[03_Domains/Insurance_AI_Governance]] records as the corpus's strongest positive coherence instance.

**Parallelism in practice, simultaneously.** The same report documents TC260/WG9, MIIT/TC1/WG8 and SAC/TC28/SC42 **all drafting overlapping agent-security standards**, and states plainly that coordination between them is uncertain. Separately, the MIIT ethics-review regime and the CAC algorithm registry impose review obligations on the same systems with cross-recognition unresolved — see [[Assurance_Reuse_Gap]].

**The specification this forces.** Coherence at the level of *who sits on the committee* is not the same property as coherence at the level of *what mandates get issued*. China scores high on the first and low on the second, at the same time, within the same standards system. If the construct is operationalised through committee composition or personnel overlap — the readiest available indicator — it will misclassify this case.

**Consequence for [[fsQCA_Thesis_Chapter]]:** the coherence condition needs an explicit operationalisation decision recorded before coding — composition-based, output-based, or a composite. These will not track each other. The IRDAI WG-AI instance and this Chinese instance both look like high coherence on composition and are untested on output.

⚠ Note this also bears on [[Regulatory_Parallelism]]: parallelism occurring inside a unitary state with centralised standards coordination means it is a property of mandate architecture rather than of federal or multi-regulator political structure.
