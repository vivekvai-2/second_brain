## Key Analytical Findings — Batch 12 (5 Primary)

**1. UNESCO Recommendation (International_18) — normative anchor retroactively closes citation chain**  
The vault previously had no node for the UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021), despite it being the founding document of the global AI ethics architecture and being cited implicitly or explicitly in multiple existing nodes (F034 explicitly; G20/EU AI Act implicitly). International_18 now anchors the normative baseline layer: UNESCO (2021) → EU AI Act (2024) → IOSCO CR/01/2025 (2025) → national AI strategies. The gap between UNESCO's near-universal 193-state adoption and its zero direct enforceability is the corpus's sharpest evidence that principle-adoption does not predict regulatory capacity — a core fsQCA hypothesis.

**2. Strongest three-way Institutional_Coherence finding in the corpus**  
IOSCO CR/01/2025 (International_19) + SEBI_14 (February 2025) + RBI_7 (May 2025) independently converge on identical "non-delegable accountability" design for AI outputs in capital markets and BFSI — without documented cross-reference or citation chain. Global standard-setter, India securities regulator, and India banking regulator reached the same accountability architecture in the same 12-month window. This is the strongest three-way Institutional_Coherence signal in the PRIS corpus, codeable as a coherence-without-coordination case for P3/P4.

**3. "Principles-and-existing-law" fsQCA cluster — consolidated with three new members**  
International_16 (Australia) + F031 (Japan) join India (NITI_7/MeitY_2) and the US (International_5) as the "principles-and-existing-law" configuration cluster: no dedicated AI regulator; existing sectoral law applied to AI; explicit rejection of mandatory rules (Australia: "principles before rules"; Japan: framework law, duty to cooperate only; India: advisory guidelines; US pre-January 2025: EO approach). This cluster now has four named jurisdictions with primary-source documentation. Contrasting outliers remain EU AI Act (hard rules, risk classification) and Singapore MAS AIRG (International_3 — binding guidelines).

**4. International relations / global governance theoretical frame — P1 SLR gap closed**  
F032 (Maas, *Architectures of Global AI Governance*, OUP 2025) + F033 (Oxford Blueprint) together supply the global governance theoretical frame that P1's SLR has been missing. Maas's regime complex theory explains why Regulatory_Parallelism persists at the international level (no hierarchical governance authority). Oxford Blueprint's "bridge powers" and compute concentration data (India ≈ 5% of global AI compute) contextualise India's sovereign AI framing (NITI_8) and the structural constraints on India's governance ambitions. These two Format B notes are the highest-value P1 theoretical additions since the vault's inception.

**5. Legal-to-technical verification gap — Governance_Debt operationalised (F034)**  
Buscemi et al. (2025) construct a structured mapping from EU AI Act legal requirements → international standards (ISO/IEC 42001/23894, NIST AI RMF) → technically verifiable activities, producing 11 trustworthy AI verification macro-categories. These 11 categories map almost exactly onto RBI_FREE_AI_2025's Seven Sutras from independent derivation — confirming convergent technical compliance architectures across India and EU. The gap between India's stated obligation (Seven Sutras) and the absence of a standardised technical verification framework is the PRIS "Governance_Debt" operationalised at the system level. F034 is the first corpus entry to bridge this gap with a proposed verification methodology.
