## Key Analytical Findings — Batch 11

**1. Regulatory_Parallelism — Five-sector silo completion**  
India's AI governance fragmentation is now documented across five sectors: BFSI (RBI/SEBI), telecom (TRAI), healthcare ethics (ICMR), healthcare strategy (MoHFW), sub-national state (TN/Telangana/Karnataka). MoHFW_1 confirms SAHI was drafted without cross-reference to DPDP, ICMR, MeitY, RBI, or TRAI instruments.

**2. Intra-RBI deontic bifurcation**  
RBI_7 (Digital Lending Directions, May 2025) uses binding SHALL obligations; RBI_FREE_AI_2025 (November 2025) uses aspirational "should" throughout. Two simultaneous RBI instruments, same compliance period, different deontic weights — practitioners face regulatory uncertainty. The bifurcation is now a codeable finding for P3/P4.

**3. SEBI–RBI Regulatory_Parallelism gap precisely bounded**  
SEBI_17 (January 4, 2019) = start date; RBI_FREE_AI_2025 (November 2025) = end date of the six-year gap. The gap is now bounded at both ends with primary-source precision. January 4, 2019 is also the earliest point at which SEBI had binding AI-governance obligations while RBI had none.

**4. International_13 — Central banks as AI deployers**  
BIS/IMF frame central banks as AI deployers (not just regulators), with a 10-action governance framework. Action A9 (adapt 3LoD, don't create parallel structures) is the corpus's most explicit prescriptive anti-parallelism recommendation. RBI's gap across A1/A4/A9/A10 places it at Maturity Level 2 against this framework — the EME central bank gap is now quantifiable across 10 dimensions for P3/fsQCA.

**5. Three underdeveloped-capacity variants documented**  
Batch 11 closes a typological gap: (a) architecture-without-enforcement (ICMR_1 — ethics framework, no sanction); (b) aspirational-without-operative (MoHFW_1 — strategy, no instrument; TRAI_2 — recommendation, not enacted); (c) capacity-without-deployment (International_15 — DAKSH FIRE-compatible, no mandate). Three distinct governance-debt patterns at the capacity layer.

**6. Accountability architecture — three design patterns complete**  
Batch 11 closes the accountability typology: (a) sole-responsibility (SEBI_14 Reg.16C); (b) cross-functional committee + 3LoD (International_13 A1–A2); (c) threshold-triggered (TRAI_1 5-flag KYC trigger). Combined with temporal accountability (International_15 FIRE phased reporting), the corpus now has a four-dimension accountability design map for P3/P4.

**7. Transparency arc — typology complete**  
SEBI_17 (January 4, 2019) = earliest regulator-facing AI transparency; RBI_7 RU5 = corpus's strongest India-side user-facing transparency; International_12 HCJP = EU statutory consumer right to AI explanation. Three transparency types documented: regulator-facing inventory, user-facing disclosure, statutory consumer right. India remains at second type; EU benchmark is third type.
