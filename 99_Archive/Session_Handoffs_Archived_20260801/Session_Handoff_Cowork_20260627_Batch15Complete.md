## Key Analytical Contributions — Batch 15

### New Gaps and Mechanisms Documented

1. **Seventh Governance_Debt variant** (F049 Butler & Brooks): "espoused-theory vs theory-in-use divergence" at the enterprise execution layer. The 3LoD model creates governance debt by displacing accountability from the first line (business managers) toward a bureaucratic second line (risk function), creating moral hazard, producing data silos that prevent early warning, and requiring backward-looking capital modelling that cannot price forward-looking risk.

2. **Eighth Governance_Debt variant** (F050 Papagiannidis et al.): "principles-adoption-without-operationalisation" as governance debt at the organizational level. The "principles-to-practice gap" — where RAI principles exist as policy artefacts but are systematically deprioritised during actual AI project implementation — matches the espoused-versus-actual gap at the project layer.

3. **Second-order Regulatory_Parallelism mitigation** (F049): An integrated enterprise data fabric and RACE (Risk Accounting Calculation Engine) architecture enables coherent compliance across parallel silos; a firm that integrates risk data across the enterprise is better positioned to produce coherent, consistent regulatory reporting to multiple overlapping regulators simultaneously.

4. **Multi-regulatory complexity gap** (F050): The scoping review's focus on intra-organisational governance highlights the multi-regulatory complexity gap for P3; in real-world BFSI environments like India, firms must navigate structural/procedural/relational requirements under multiple simultaneous, uncoordinated regulatory mandates (RBI + SEBI + IRDAI + MeitY).

5. **Deep operational risk governance incapacity** (F049): ~80% of European banks not modelling their op-risk capital allocation constitutes direct empirical evidence of deep governance incapacity in the operational risk layer, showing that capacity deficits are institutional rather than merely technical.

6. **Responsible AI literacy as capacity prerequisite** (F050): Frames competency-building explicitly as a capacity prerequisite for effective RAI governance, showing that skills/competencies across levels are structurally constitutive of the RAI implementation problem.
