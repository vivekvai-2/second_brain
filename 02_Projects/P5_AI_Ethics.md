## 3. Contribution Summary

P5 develops a **three-phase responsibility architecture** for agentic administrative systems — public-sector AI that selects, sequences, and executes rights-affecting actions (welfare delivery, identity authentication, debt generation, enforcement).

**Three phases:**

| Phase | Function |
|---|---|
| Ex-ante authorisation | Mandate alignment audit before deployment — does the proxy the system optimises for preserve the statutory purpose it was authorised to serve? |
| In-action supervision | Moral friction — structured human checkpoints at rights-affecting decision nodes, designed to prevent complete automation of delegated public action |
| Ex-post accountability and redress | Responsibility traceability to named human authorities; administrative redress architecture |

**Three concepts introduced:**

- **Administrative goal drift** — binary pathology in which proxy optimisation at system level interacts with Mertonian goal displacement at operator level
- **Institutional answerability** — accountability to named human authorities for proxy-to-mandate translations; stronger than compliance accountability
- **Responsibility traceability** — the mechanism holding all three phases together

**Empirical grounding:** Two anticipatory cases — Aadhaar-linked welfare delivery (India) and Robodebt (Australia). Deterministic systems used precisely because they isolate institutional pathology from stochastic machine error.

**Positioning:** Sits between the AI governance framework literature (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, OECD AI Principles) and public administration accountability theory (Bovens 2007, Lipsky 1980) — in the space both conversations jointly underspecify.
