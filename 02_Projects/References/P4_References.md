## PRIMARY THEORETICAL ANCHORS

### Agency Theory — Primary Frame

#### Schuett, J. (2023) — P67 in corpus

**Full citation:** Schuett, J. (2023). Three lines of defense against risks from AI. *AI and Ethics*, *4*, 1501–1516.  
**Zotero key:** `schuett_2023_p67`  
**Role:** Theoretical anchor — primary  
**Claim:** The Three Lines of Defence model requires structural redesign — not modification — to accommodate AI risk governance, because first-line business units cannot own AI risks they do not control.  
**Where used:** §3 Agency Theory frame; §6 Contradiction C01 resolution (P73's fundamental redesign is correct; P67's modification argument is insufficient for Indian multi-regulator context)  
**Note:** C01 in the paper takes an explicit position — Schuett's modification approach is rejected in favour of fundamental redesign.

#### Eisenberg, M. et al. (2025) — P73 in corpus

#### IIA (2020) — P86 in corpus

**Full citation:** Institute of Internal Auditors. (2020). *The IIA's three lines model: An update of the three lines of defense*. Institute of Internal Auditors.  
**Zotero key:** `iia_2020_p86`  
**Role:** Definitional source — 3LoD canonical reference  
**Claim:** The Three Lines model allocates risk ownership, oversight, and assurance across first line (business), second line (risk/compliance), and third line (internal audit) — the model assumes a single coherent mandate environment.  
**Where used:** §2 (doctrinal mapping — how current 3LoD allocates AI risk in BFSI); §3 Agency Theory frame

---

#### Davies, G., & Zhivitskaya, M. (2018) — P34 in corpus

**Full citation:** Davies, G., & Zhivitskaya, M. (2018). Three lines of defence: Two logics of financial regulation. *European Business Organization Law Review*, *19*(1), 55–90.  
**Zotero key:** `davies_zhivitskaya_2018_p34`  
**Role:** Theoretical anchor — 3LoD critique  
**Claim:** The Three Lines model embeds two logics of financial regulation (prudential and conduct) that operate in tension — a tension that becomes structurally visible when two regulators with different logics govern the same entity.  
**Where used:** §3 Agency Theory frame; §4 Fragmentation diagnosis (RBI lending prudence vs SEBI market conduct as divergent institutional logics)

### Institutional Theory — Supporting Frame

#### DiMaggio, P. J., & Powell, W. W. (1983)

**Full citation:** DiMaggio, P. J., & Powell, W. W. (1983). The iron cage revisited: Institutional isomorphism and collective rationality in organizational fields. *American Sociological Review*, *48*(2), 147–160.  
**Zotero key:** `dimaggio_powell_1983`  
**Role:** Theoretical anchor — isomorphic pressures  
**Claim:** Isomorphic pressures (coercive, mimetic, normative) drive organisations within an institutional field toward similarity — but pressures operate within each regulatory silo, not across regulatory fields.  
**Where used:** §3 Institutional Theory frame (explains why RBI and SEBI do not coordinate — divergent institutional logics create different isomorphic pressures within each silo)  
**Shared anchor:** [[DiMaggio_Powell_1983]]

### Regulatory Governance Theory — Supporting Frame

#### Scott, C. (2001/2002)

**Full citation:** Scott, C. (2001). Analysing regulatory space: Fragmented resources and institutional design. *Public Law*, Summer 2001, 283–305.  
**Zotero key:** `scott_2001_regulatory_space`  
**Role:** Theoretical anchor — regulatory space and fragmentation  
**Claim:** Regulatory space is fragmented when multiple actors hold resources necessary for effective regulation — no single actor commands the full set of tools needed to govern a domain coherently.  
**Where used:** §3 Regulatory Governance Theory frame; §4 Fragmentation diagnosis (RBI-SEBI fragmentation as regulatory space fragmentation)
