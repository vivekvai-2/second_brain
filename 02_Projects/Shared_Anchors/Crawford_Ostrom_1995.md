# Crawford & Ostrom (1995)

**Full citation:** Crawford, S. E. S., & Ostrom, E. (1995). A grammar of institutions. *American Political Science Review*, *89*(3), 582–600.  
**Zotero key:** `crawford_ostrom_1995`  
**Journal:** American Political Science Review

---

## Core Argument

Institutional statements — the rules, norms, and strategies that govern collective action — can be parsed into five syntactic components: Attributes (who the statement applies to), Deontic (the level of obligation — must/should/may), aIm (the required or permitted action or outcome), Conditions (the circumstances under which the statement applies), and Or-else (the consequences of non-compliance). This ADICO grammar provides a systematic basis for comparing institutional arrangements across contexts.

---

## Why This Work Matters to PRIS

Crawford & Ostrom (1995) is the canonical source for the entire IG 2.0 coding methodology that underlies P2, P3, and the fsQCA chapter. Every ADICO-coded institutional statement in every paper traces to this source. It is not cited prominently in each paper but it is the methodological foundation that makes the empirical work possible.

## Relationship to Frantz & Siddiki (2022)

Crawford & Ostrom (1995) is the original grammar. Frantz & Siddiki (2022) is IG 2.0 — the refined version with explicit Rules/Norms/Shared Strategies classification and updated ADICO coding protocols. In practice, PRIS uses Frantz & Siddiki (2022) as the working specification. Crawford & Ostrom (1995) is cited for intellectual lineage.

Both must be cited together in any paper using IG 2.0 coding.
