# EU AI Act (2024)

**Full citation:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on Artificial Intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act) [2024] OJ L.  
**OSCOLA citation (for P4):** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on artificial intelligence [2024] OJ L (EU AI Act).  
**APA citation (for P5/fsQCA):** European Parliament and the Council of the European Union. (2024). *Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act)*. Official Journal of the European Union.  
**Zotero key:** `eu_ai_act_2024`

---

## Core Argument

The EU AI Act is the world's first comprehensive binding AI regulation, organised around a risk-tiered architecture: prohibited applications (outright banned), high-risk systems (conformity assessment, documentation, human oversight), limited-risk (transparency obligations), and minimal-risk (unregulated). A separate horizontal track governs General-Purpose AI (GPAI) models. The Act's defining property is its cross-sectoral, risk-tiered, binding architecture — obligations scale with risk level regardless of sector.

**Key dates:**
- February 2025: Prohibition and AI literacy obligations enter force
- August 2025: GPAI obligations apply
- 2026–2027: High-risk system obligations phase in

## Why This Work Matters to PRIS

The EU AI Act is the **canonical RL (Regulatory Logic) benchmark** in the fsQCA model — the highest-RL case against which all other frameworks are calibrated. In P4, it is the international comparator that makes visible what India lacks: a cross-sectoral binding framework with calibrated obligation intensity. In P5, it is one of four governance frameworks whose institutional answerability gap P5's architecture fills.
