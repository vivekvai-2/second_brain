# Kallinikos, Aaltonen & Marton (2013)

**Full citation:** Kallinikos, J., Aaltonen, A., & Marton, A. (2013). The ambivalent ontology of digital artefacts. *MIS Quarterly*, *37*(2), 357–370.  
**Zotero key:** `kallinikos_aaltonen_marton_2013`  
**Journal:** MIS Quarterly (AIS Senior Scholars' Basket — ABDC-A*)

---

## Core Argument

Digital artefacts are ontologically ambivalent — they exist in a state between objects and processes, between stable form and dynamic re-inscription. They possess four distinctive properties that distinguish them from physical artefacts:

1. **Editability** — the content of a digital artefact can be modified without destroying the artefact itself
2. **Interactivity** — digital artefacts respond to input and trigger action in ways physical objects cannot
3. **Openness** — digital artefacts are designed to connect with and be extended by other artefacts
4. **Distributedness** — digital artefacts exist simultaneously across multiple instantiations and contexts

These properties determine how digital artefacts distribute agency, accountability, and authority across the sociotechnical systems in which they are embedded.

---

## Why This Work Matters to PRIS

Kallinikos et al. (2013) provides the **IS theoretical grammar** for analysing why governance architectures have the distributional properties they do. In P2, the argument that the rule architecture of DPI governance instruments can be analysed *as a digital artefact* depends on demonstrating that the rule architecture possesses the four Kallinikos properties. This is what transforms an empirical finding (18 Rules, 0 rights-bearing) into an IS theoretical contribution.

## Relationship to Alaimo & Kallinikos (2022)

Alaimo & Kallinikos (2022) extends the 2013 work into the governance domain — showing how the properties of data objects determine how they distribute agency and accountability. P2 uses both:
- Kallinikos et al. (2013): the four properties as analytical grammar
- Alaimo & Kallinikos (2022): the governance application — how these properties shape accountability distribution

Both cited in §2; Kallinikos et al. (2013) also in §5 for the four-property analysis.
