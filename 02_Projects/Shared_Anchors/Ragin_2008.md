# Ragin (2008)

**Full citation:** Ragin, C. C. (2008). *Redesigning social inquiry: Fuzzy sets and beyond*. University of Chicago Press.  
**Zotero key:** `ragin_2008`  
**Publisher:** University of Chicago Press

---

## Core Argument

Fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis extends Boolean QCA by allowing partial set membership — cases can be more or less fully members of a set (scored 0.0–1.0) rather than fully in or fully out. This enables causal analysis at the intersection of qualitative case knowledge and set-theoretic logic. The core causal concept is the INUS condition: an element that is Insufficient but Non-redundant in an Unnecessary but Sufficient configuration. Three solution types are produced — complex (no simplifying assumptions), parsimonious (maximises simplification), and intermediate (theoretically guided simplification) — and Ragin (2008) recommends the intermediate solution for interpretation.

---

## Why This Work Matters to PRIS

Ragin (2008) is the **methodological foundation** for the entire fsQCA thesis chapter. Every design decision in the chapter — calibration scheme, consistency threshold, solution types, INUS interpretation, limited diversity handling — traces to this source. It is also the theoretical bridge between P1 (SLR-derived typology) and the fsQCA chapter (causal configurations): the Fiss (2011) typology-to-configuration logic presupposes the Ragin (2008) methodology.

## Relationship to Fiss (2011)

Ragin (2008) provides the *method*; Fiss (2011) provides the *theoretical bridge*. The Fiss typology-configuration logic says: build the typology (P1) using SLR; validate the configurations (fsQCA chapter) using fsQCA. Without Ragin (2008), the fsQCA chapter has no methodological foundation. Without Fiss (2011), the relationship between P1 and the fsQCA chapter is unexplained.
