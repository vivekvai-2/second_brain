# Singapore AI Governance — MAS FEAT/Veritas and IMDA Model Framework/AI Verify

**Type:** Framework **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-08-02 (created — 51 referencing notes, no node)

---

## Core Synthesis

Singapore is cited across this corpus as a primary international comparator, and in [[P4_Doctrinal_IJLIT]] it is one of three benchmarks informing the Three-Level Governance Alignment Model. The sourcing pass that created this node establishes something that must now discipline how the comparison is drawn: **Singapore has no binding AI instrument of any kind.** No standalone AI statute, no AI-specific Notice or Directive, no designated AI regulator, no statutory definition of AI. Every instrument in the stack — FEAT, Veritas, the Model AI Governance Frameworks, AI Verify — is advisory or voluntary. Confirmed explicitly across three independent legal trackers, not inferred from omission.

**What Singapore has instead is the most developed *assessment methodology* infrastructure in the corpus.** Veritas supplies published assessment methodologies for each of the four FEAT principles with open-source toolkits; AI Verify supplies a technical testing framework operationalising eleven principles. That is the dimension on which Singapore leads, and it is the dimension P4 should be benchmarking against — not regulatory bindingness, where India is ahead.

## Binding Status — confirmed absence

Three mutually corroborating sources: CMS AI Regulation Scanner (current 16 Feb 2026) — "There is currently no dedicated AI law in force in Singapore"; White & Case Global Regulatory Tracker (current 3 Mar 2026) — "there are no specific laws, statutory rules, or regulations in Singapore that directly regulate AI," and no designated AI regulator; and a comparative compliance table marking Singapore "Binding? No" against South Korea's Framework Act on AI (in force 22 Jan 2026) and Vietnam's Law 134/2025 (effective 1 Mar 2026).

AI obligations arise only indirectly, through statutes that are binding but not AI-specific: PDPA 2012, Computer Misuse Act 1993, Copyright Act 2021 (computational data analysis exception), Online Criminal Harms Act 2023, Health Products Act 2007 (AI-embedded medical devices).

**The only quasi-binding route is indirect:** MAS's FEAT Principles and AI MRM paper sit within supervisory expectations for regulated FIs — *de facto* compulsion without *de jure* obligation. PDPC's Advisory Guidelines on AI Recommendation and Decision Systems carry interpretive weight under PDPA s.49 while remaining, in the regulator's own words, "not legally binding."

**This is a textbook [[Deontic_Bifurcation]] case of a kind not yet recorded on that note** — an entire national regime operating on supervisory expectation rather than deontic obligation. Not mandatory-core-plus-advisory-annexure, and not designed-versus-emergent, but *bindingness generated by supervisory relationship rather than by instrument text*. Worth coding as a fourth type.

## ⚠ Consequence for P4 — the benchmark framing needs disciplining

[[P4_Doctrinal_IJLIT]] currently frames the international benchmarks as comparators "for what India is missing" (§140) and "for what India lacks" (§252). On the Singapore comparison **that framing is wrong in one direction and right in another, and the paper must separate them**:

| Dimension | Who leads | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Binding force** | **India** | RBI issues binding Master Directions; SEBI issues binding circulars. MAS issues Information Papers. India is *ahead*, not behind |
| **Assessment methodology** | **Singapore, decisively** | Veritas publishes per-principle assessment methodologies with open-source toolkits; AI Verify runs technical tests. India has no equivalent for any principle |
| **Coordination** | Neither cleanly | Singapore has one financial regulator, so the parallelism problem does not arise — this makes it a **poor comparator for coordination** and the UK FCA-PRA MoU carries that load alone |

[[Doctrinal_Analysis]] already frames Singapore's contribution correctly as "operationalisation" — that framing is sound and should be preserved and made explicit. The risk is the *paper's* looser "what India lacks" phrasing, which a reviewer familiar with MAS will read as claiming India lacks binding rules Singapore has. It does not.

**Cumulative point across this and the ISO/IEC 42001 pass.** Two of P4's three benchmarks are weaker than the corpus previously assumed: the EU AI Act has **zero cited harmonised standards**, so Article 40 presumption of conformity attaches to nothing ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_33_CEN_CENELEC_JTC21_Harmonised_Standards_2026]]); and Singapore has **no binding AI instrument at all**. The India-lags framing keeps collapsing on inspection. The defensible thesis is not that India lags a mature international standard but that **no mature international standard yet exists** — which is a stronger, more original, and more defensible claim, and one that repositions P4's redesign proposal as filling a genuine void rather than transplanting a working model.

## Measurement Value — Veritas and AI Verify as EA-principle instruments

This is the most directly reusable material from this pass, and it bears on [[fsQCA_Thesis_Chapter]] decision D4.

**Veritas Documents 3A/3B/3C** are published **assessment methodologies** for Fairness, for Ethics and Accountability, and for Transparency respectively — regime-and-system-level assessment procedures, not user-perception scales. The EA-principle concept notes created earlier today recorded that the available instruments (SCS, XAI Trust Scale, CAS) measure *received explanation quality from a human subject* and cannot be applied to a governance regime without category error. **Veritas is the missing instrument class**: it assesses the deploying institution's process. It should be evaluated as a third calibration option for D4 alongside "declare the metric family" and "calibrate on specification."

**AI Verify makes an empirical claim about testability that corroborates a PRIS finding.** Its framework operationalises eleven principles but runs **technical tests on only three — Fairness, Explainability, Robustness** — with the remaining eight assessed by documented process checks. Human Agency and Oversight is a process-check principle. That is independent, practitioner-side confirmation of the conclusion recorded at [[Human_Oversight]] from the academic literature: human oversight is the least technically measurable of the EA constituents. Two independent routes to the same finding is worth stating in the methodology chapter.

## fsQCA Calibration

Singapore is a strong candidate case precisely because it inverts the usual pattern: **near-zero on Regulatory Logic (RL)** — no binding instrument, no statutory definition, no designated regulator — while scoring **high on Structural Readiness (SR)** through Veritas methodologies, AI Verify testing infrastructure, and an institutionalised assessment ecosystem. Cases where RL and SR diverge sharply are the ones that carry configurational information, and this is the second such case identified today (see [[DEPA]], where the same divergence appears for different reasons). If both enter the 10-case set, the design gains real leverage on whether SR can substitute for RL in producing legitimate governance outcomes — which is close to the thesis's central question.

## Key Tensions & Gaps

- **Veritas ended at Phase 3 (June 2023) and has not been extended.** No Phase 4. The most developed AI assessment methodology in the corpus has been static for three years while GenAI and agentic systems moved underneath it — the AI MRM paper (Dec 2024) and the Agentic AI Model Framework (2026) address the gap from outside the Veritas structure rather than by extending it.
- **Confirmed literature gap:** no peer-reviewed study performs a direct comparison of Singapore + India FREE-AI + EU AI Act + UK regulator-led model as co-equal comparators. The strongest sources are Hilliard et al. (*Royal Society Open Science*, 2026) on soft-law positioning, and a Government of India PIB comparative annex. **P4's comparative contribution is filling a genuine void, not duplicating existing work** — this should be stated in the contribution framing.
- ⚠ An unverified secondary claim (LinkedIn commentary, Jan 2026) says the 2024 AI MRM paper is "now fueling 2025 formal guidelines." **No MAS Notice, Guideline or binding circular formalising AI MRM was located.** Do not repeat this claim without direct verification against MAS's circular index.

## Future Research / Reuse Opportunities

- **"No mature benchmark exists"** is the strongest reframing available to P4 and follows directly from combining this pass with the ISO 42001 pass. Rewrite the benchmark section around it.
- Veritas Documents 3A/3B/3C are a ready-made structure for a practitioner assessment product — the [[Governance_Diagnostic_Report_Template]] and [[BFSI_AI_Governance_Handbook]] can be built against a published, credible, open-source methodology rather than an invented one.
- The AI Verify "three testable, eight process-check" split is a clean carousel and a genuinely useful practitioner point: most AI principles are not technically testable, and governance that pretends otherwise is theatre.
- Singapore-as-RL-low/SR-high is the sharpest single case for arguing that assessment infrastructure and binding force are separable governance dimensions — which is close to the thesis's core claim.
