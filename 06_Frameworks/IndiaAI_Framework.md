# IndiaAI Framework

**Type:** Framework **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-06-12

---

## Core Synthesis

The IndiaAI Framework represents India's primary cross-sectoral AI governance instrument at the national level — an advisory, non-binding document issued under MeitY's mandate that articulates India's self-regulatory, sectoral-blueprint approach as a deliberate alternative to the EU's prescriptive binding model and the US's fragmented sector-specific enforcement. Within PRIS, it occupies a dual role: as an fsQCA case representing India's national-level regulatory logic (advisory, principles-aligned, sectoral-delegating), and as the Indian comparator in P4's doctrinal fragmentation analysis — the cross-sectoral instrument that should, in design intent, coordinate what RBI and SEBI have not. Its analytical significance lies precisely in the gap between its coordination aspiration and its non-binding status: it names the governance problem that PRIS's three signature constructs (Regulatory Parallelism, Governance Debt, Institutional Coherence) diagnose at the sectoral level.

## Framework Identity

- **Issuing body:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), Government of India; operationalised through the IndiaAI Mission
- **Instrument type:** Advisory guidelines / national AI strategy — non-binding
- **Jurisdiction:** India (national, cross-sectoral)
- **Binding status:** Non-binding; sectoral regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI) retain independent rulemaking authority within their domains
- **Key dates:**
    - 2023: IndiaAI Mission announced under Digital India framework
    - November 2025: MeitY AI Governance Guidelines issued — Seven Sutras for responsible AI, graded action plan _(verify exact document title and gazette reference via Perplexity)_
    - 2025–2026: Sectoral blueprints for BFSI, healthcare, transport, policing under development

## Structural Overview

The IndiaAI Framework operates on a **self-regulation plus sectoral delegation** architecture. At the national level, it articulates cross-sectoral principles — formulated as "Seven Sutras" in the November 2025 MeitY guidelines — that map onto the international vocabulary of the OECD Principles and UNESCO Recommendation: trustworthiness, fairness, transparency, accountability, safety, privacy, and inclusion. These are aspirational by design; the framework explicitly declines to impose binding obligations at the national level.

The operative governance mechanism is **sectoral delegation**: the framework envisions sector-specific regulators (RBI for BFSI, IRDAI for insurance, and so on) translating national principles into domain-specific obligations. This architecture has two structural consequences. First, it makes India's national AI governance framework dependent on the coherence and coordination capacity of its sectoral regulators — precisely the dimension that P3 and P4 diagnose as failing. Second, it means that the IndiaAI Framework's own binding force is effectively zero at the enterprise level; what enterprises face are sectoral instruments (RBI FREE-AI, SEBI Regulation 16C) that may or may not reflect the national principles.

A third-party model audit ecosystem and risk-based sectoral blueprints are under development as of mid-2026 — their completion would materially alter the framework's RL score and operational significance.

## Regulatory Position & Comparative Significance

Within the PRIS framework set, IndiaAI occupies the position of **national advisory baseline for India** — the direct Indian counterpart to OECD Principles at the international level. Its defining comparative property is the gap between its aspirational scope (cross-sectoral, principles-aligned) and its operational reach (non-binding, sectoral-delegating). Against the EU AI Act, it illustrates the structural difference between coordination by binding risk-tiering and coordination by aspirational principle. Against RBI FREE-AI and the SEBI AI Circular, it shows that India's sectoral binding instruments developed largely independently of the national framework — a structural manifestation of Regulatory Parallelism at the meta-governance level. For P4, IndiaAI is the instrument that _should_ have prevented the RBI-SEBI coordination gap but, by design, could not.

## Coordination Pair Classification

Not applicable in the P3 sense — IndiaAI is not a party to the RBI/SEBI/CERT-In coordination-pair matrix analysed in P3. However, it is analytically relevant as the meta-governance instrument that sits above the P3 corpus: the absence of any RBI or SEBI cross-reference to IndiaAI guidelines in the 18 P3 binding instruments is itself a coordination-pair finding — zero upward linkage from sectoral to national.

## Key Tensions & Gaps

The structural tension is between coordination aspiration and coordination capacity: IndiaAI aspires to cross-sectoral coherence but has no mechanism to compel sectoral regulators to coordinate with each other or with the national framework. This gap is not a drafting failure — it reflects India's constitutional and regulatory architecture, where sectoral regulators have statutory independence. The practical consequence is that IndiaAI provides legitimacy cover for the absence of binding cross-regulator coordination without resolving it. A secondary tension is the "blueprint lag": the sectoral risk blueprints (BFSI, healthcare, etc.) that would operationalise the framework are under development while RBI and SEBI have already issued binding instruments — creating a sequencing inversion where sectoral obligations precede the national framework they are supposed to implement.
