## DJ-07 · 2026-06-19 · PRIS Workflow Discipline Protocol (WDP) Establishment

**Context:** The risk of ontology inflation (over-creation of notes, aliases, and weak pathways) was identified as a critical threat to the compounding value of the PRIS vault.

**Decision:** Establish and formalise the PRIS [[Workflow_Discipline_Protocol]] (WDP) as a binding operational framework. It codifies three core disciplines: Link discipline (mandatory contextualization and bidirectional linking), Concept discipline (the "Three-Instance" Gate for new concept notes), and Close-out discipline (the 15-minute exit buffer and mandatory handoff generation). It also enforces the "Ruthless Utility" doctrine requiring every note to map to one of the Five Output Pockets (Research, Consulting, Teaching, Thought Leadership, Knowledge Products).

**Rationale:** To prevent the vault from degrading into an unnavigable "document dump," operational guidelines must be hardcoded into the co-working and solo workflows. The "Three-Instance" Gate protects the primacy of signature constructs, while the Close-out buffer ensures that sessions always leave behind high-yield, indexed assets.

**Constraints:** Standalone concept notes cannot be created unless they meet the Entry Criteria. New files must be indexed immediately. Every session must end with a structured exit pass.

**Review trigger:** Post-P2 submission (September 2026) or during the next major ontology review.

## DJ-08 · 2026-06-19 · PRIS Workflow Discipline Protocol (WDP) v2.0 Evolution

**Context:** The operational overhead of WDP v1.0 was deemed too rigid for quick micro-sessions, while the ingestion of new corpus documents required a more explicit "search-first" doctrine to prevent duplicate file creation.

**Decision:** Rewrite and upgrade `00_MOC/Workflow_Discipline_Protocol.md` to Version 2.0. This evolution: (1) establishes the **"Update Before Create" (UBC)** doctrine as a core operational loop requirement, supported by a Search-First Ingestion Decision Tree; and (2) implements a **Tiered Close-out Architecture** (L1 Micro, L2 Standard, and L3 Deep Close-outs) with scaled time buffers (3, 15, and 30 minutes) and specific required deliverables.

**Rationale:** Standardizing a "Search-First" process ensures that agents check for existing notes and merge or stage concepts inline before creating files, directly preventing vault clutter. A tiered close-out architecture matches administrative overhead to session complexity, making the protocol lighter for small edits while maintaining maximum rigor for deep batch-processing sessions.

**Constraints:** Standalone concept creation remains bound by the "Three-Instance" Gate. All new files must still be indexed, but the close-out buffer time and deliverables scale dynamically.

**Review trigger:** Post-P2 submission (September 2026) or during the next major ontology review.

## DJ-11 · 2026-08-01 · Session_Handoff Consolidation — 31 Files to 1 Rolling File

**Context:** A housekeeping-burden discussion identified `00_MOC/Session_Handoffs/` as the single largest source of vault clutter: 31 files (~230KB), several literally covering the same batch (three separate files for "Batch 15" alone: Start, Complete, Continuation_Complete). A full read-through of all 31 files, cross-checked against this Decision Journal and `Corpus_Index_MOC.md`, found that the large majority (26 of 31) were fully redundant — their numbering-state tables, VERIFY flag lists, and "Instances in Corpus" indexing items are all now duplicated in `Corpus_Index_MOC.md` and the concept files themselves. Five files, however, carried content that existed nowhere else in the vault: a standing duplicate-handling protocol change (2026-07-16, never logged here), two live citation-integrity bugs in `P2_References.md`, the Records_Rules_Asymmetry fabrication-audit downgrade (signature → candidate) with its unresolved remediation options, two open research-management questions (P2 second coder, fsQCA case-list lock), and a five-item "Vivek-only" verification punch list requiring the user's own project artefacts.

**Decision:** (1) Extracted the genuinely unique content from the five non-redundant files into a single rolling handoff, `00_MOC/Session_Handoffs/Session_Handoff_Current.md`. (2) Archived all 31 original files, unedited, to `99_Archive/Session_Handoffs_Archived_20260801/` — archived, not deleted, since the vault carries no git history and archiving is the only recovery path if the extraction pass missed something. (3) Updated the two process files that instructed future sessions to create a new dated handoff (`PRIS_Cowork_Context.md` §9 and Quick Reference; `PRIS_Corpus_Intake_Prompts.md` Session Completion Checklist) to instead specify: overwrite `Session_Handoff_Current.md` at session close, copying the outgoing version to the same archive folder first for recovery purposes.

**Rationale:** The 31-file pattern was solving a real problem (session-to-session continuity across tools that don't share memory — Cowork, ChatGPT, NotebookLM all read this file to resume work) at an unbounded, compounding cost: every session added a new file rather than updating one, and several files were near-duplicates of each other by construction (a batch's "Complete" note is, by definition, the same information as the next batch's "Start" note). A single rolling file, overwritten each session and archived before each overwrite, preserves the cross-tool continuity function at a fixed cost instead of a growing one, while the archive-before-overwrite step keeps a recovery path given the absence of version control.

**Constraints:** `Session_Handoff_Current.md` must be kept current, not historical — prune resolved items at each update rather than letting it re-accumulate into a second version of the same problem. The five extracted "still open" items (protocol change, two citation bugs, Records_Rules_Asymmetry remediation, two research-management questions, five-item verification punch list) remain genuinely unresolved and should not be considered closed by virtue of this consolidation — they are carried forward in `Session_Handoff_Current.md`, not resolved by it.

**Review trigger:** If `Session_Handoff_Current.md` is ever found stale (not updated for several sessions) or re-accumulates unresolved items past a reasonable length, revisit whether the single-file model is holding.
