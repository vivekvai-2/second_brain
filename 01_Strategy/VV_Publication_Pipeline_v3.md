## 3. Drafting Discipline Protocol (DDP) — Non-Negotiable

> **Context:** The GIQ desk rejection (14 Apr 2026) diagnosed a narrative architecture failure, not a substantive failure. Editor-in-Chief Panos Panagiotopoulos stated: *"issues of clarity and contribution … difficult to follow and presented in small sections with insufficient connection to an overall narrative … no opportunity to outline the significance and contribution."*
>
> **This means:** The same failure mode will recur in every paper unless drafting discipline changes. Substance is fine. Structure is broken.

### 3.1 The Five Non-Negotiable Rules

**Rule 1 — Narrative Spine Before Drafting**

Before writing any section of any paper, produce a one-paragraph narrative spine:

> *"This paper argues **X**, because **Y**, demonstrated through **Z**, contributing **W** to the field of [domain]."*

The spine lives in the paper's Tier-3 state file (`P{n}_State.md` Section 6). Every section, every paragraph, every citation must serve the spine. If a section does not serve the spine, it does not belong in the paper.

**Rule 2 — Contribution-First Placement**

The contribution claim must appear in **Section 1 (Introduction)**, not after the framework, not in the conclusion, not buried in the abstract. The reader must know what the paper contributes before they finish the first 500 words. The GIQ rejection specifically cited: *"no opportunity to outline the significance and contribution"* after the framework. The fix is front-loading.

**Rule 3 — Thematic Aggregation Over Fragmentation**

Target **3–5 substantive sections per major chapter**, not 8–12 fragmented ones. Each section should be at least 600–1000 words with a clear thematic anchor. If a section is under 400 words, it is either a subsection of a larger block or it does not belong.

Panagiotopoulos specifically cited: *"presented in small sections with insufficient connection to an overall narrative."* The fix is aggregation.

**Rule 4 — Forward-Referencing Transitions**

Every section must end with a sentence that signals what the next section adds to the argument. No orphan sections. No section that could be moved elsewhere without disruption. The transitions are the connective tissue Panagiotopoulos found missing.

**Rule 5 — Whole-Paper Coherence Pass + Narrative Reader Review**

Before submission:

(a) **Whole-paper coherence pass** — Read the entire paper in one sitting with zero focus on substance. Only check: does the argument flow? Does each section follow from the previous? Can the contribution be restated in one sentence from memory after reading?

(b) **Narrative reader review** — Send the paper to one person who reads only for narrative flow, not technical content. The test: after reading, can they restate the paper's contribution in one sentence without looking back? If no, restructure.

(c) **AI cadence elimination pass** — Run the `ai-cadence-elimination` skill on the **whole paper**, not section-by-section. Section-level passes preserve fragmentation; whole-paper passes force narrative coherence.

### 3.2 Drafting Discipline Checklist (Per Paper)

Every paper must clear this checklist before submission. The checklist lives in the Tier-3 state file; this is the master version.

| # | Check | Status Location |
|---|---|---|
| DDP-01 | Narrative spine written and locked before drafting started | `P{n}_State.md` Section 6 |
| DDP-02 | Contribution claim appears in Section 1, not after the framework | Manuscript Section 1 |
| DDP-03 | 3–5 substantive sections per major chapter, no fragmented small blocks | Manuscript TOC |
| DDP-04 | Every section ends with a forward-referencing transition | Manuscript review |
| DDP-05 | Whole-paper coherence pass completed | `P{n}_State.md` Section 12 (Session Log) |
| DDP-06 | Narrative reader review completed by a non-technical reader | `P{n}_State.md` Section 10 (Conversations Log) |
| DDP-07 | AI cadence elimination pass run on whole paper | `P{n}_State.md` Section 12 |
| DDP-08 | Contribution restatable in one sentence after reading the full paper | Narrative reader confirms |
| DDP-09 | Pre-submission read-through by advisor (Prof Panigrahi) | `P{n}_State.md` Section 10 |
| DDP-10 | 48-hour cooling period between "finished" and "submitted" | Discipline rule |

### 3.3 What Changed From Pre-GIQ Drafting Practice

| Old Habit | New Rule (Post-GIQ) |
|---|---|
| Draft section-by-section, stitch together at the end | Draft from a locked narrative spine; every section serves the spine |
| Contribution claim emerges after framework is presented | Contribution claim is front-loaded in Section 1 |
| 8–12 small thematic sections per chapter | 3–5 substantive sections per chapter |
| AI cadence elimination run per section | AI cadence elimination run on whole paper only |
| Self-review only | Narrative reader review + advisor read-through mandatory |
| Submit as soon as drafted | 48-hour cooling period between "finished" and "submitted" |

## 10. Drafting Discipline Application Checklist

**A fresh Claude working on any paper must walk through this checklist before producing any draft text.**

### Pre-Drafting

- [ ] Has the narrative spine been written and locked in the paper's state file Section 6?
- [ ] Has the contribution claim been sharpened using `contribution-framing` skill?
- [ ] Has the theoretical scaffold been identified using `identifying-theoretical-scaffolding` skill?
- [ ] Has the target journal's theoretical conventions been surveyed (3–5 recent papers)?
- [ ] Is the target word count and section structure locked?

### During Drafting

- [ ] Does every section serve the narrative spine?
- [ ] Is the contribution claim placed in Section 1, not after the framework?
- [ ] Are sections 600–1000 words minimum, aggregated thematically?
- [ ] Does every section end with a forward-referencing transition?
- [ ] Is every citation substantive — does it advance the argument, not just decorate it?

### Pre-Submission

- [ ] Whole-paper coherence pass completed (read in one sitting, substance ignored)
- [ ] Narrative reader review completed (non-technical reader)
- [ ] Advisor read-through completed
- [ ] `ai-cadence-elimination` skill run on WHOLE paper (not section-by-section)
- [ ] Contribution restatable in one sentence after reading full paper
- [ ] 48-hour cooling period observed between "finished" and "submitted"
- [ ] Submission checklist matches target journal's author guidelines
