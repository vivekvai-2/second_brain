# **DPDP Playbook**

**Type:** Knowledge Product  
**Status:** Planned — activates October 2026  
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-12  
**Audience:** BFSI compliance leaders, DPOs, privacy counsel, CISOs

---

## **Product Description**

The DPDP Playbook is a practitioner-focused implementation guide designed to translate the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and associated Rules into operational governance practices for organisations deploying AI systems. The product is intended to bridge the gap between statutory obligations and day-to-day implementation by providing structured compliance pathways, governance artefacts, and accountability frameworks. Particular emphasis is placed on BFSI organisations operating within overlapping DPDP, RBI, and AI governance requirements. The playbook is designed as an operational companion to the AI Governance Wiki and as a practical advisory asset for privacy, compliance, and governance leaders.

## **Planned Structure**

### **Chapter 1 — DPDP Governance Architecture**

Establishes the governance foundations of the DPDP framework, organisational accountability structures, and the relationship between horizontal privacy obligations and sector-specific regulatory requirements.

### **Chapter 2 — DPIA Framework for AI Systems**

Provides a structured approach for conducting Data Protection Impact Assessments covering AI training, inference, model deployment, and ongoing operational monitoring.

### **Chapter 3 — Data Protection Officer Governance**

Defines the role, authority, reporting lines, escalation responsibilities, and board engagement requirements for Data Protection Officers operating within AI-enabled organisations.

### **Chapter 4 — Consent Architecture and DEPA Alignment**

Examines informed, granular, and revocable consent requirements together with consent-management patterns emerging from DEPA-aligned ecosystems.

### **Chapter 5 — Data Minimisation and Retention Controls**

Provides governance approaches for managing personal data throughout the AI lifecycle, including collection, training, inference, retention, and disposal.

### **Chapter 6 — Breach Response and Incident Governance**

Develops an operational response framework covering escalation pathways, governance responsibilities, documentation requirements, and Data Protection Board notification obligations.

### **Chapter 7 — Data Localisation and Cross-Border Data Governance**

Explains the interaction between DPDP cross-border transfer provisions and stricter sector-specific localisation requirements applicable to BFSI entities.

### **Chapter 8 — Auditability, Logging, and Board Oversight**

Focuses on governance evidence, audit log requirements, accountability documentation, and mechanisms for ensuring board ownership of privacy risk decisions and DPIA outcomes.

## **Activation Schedule**

The playbook activates following submission of P2 to the JSIS Special Issue in October 2026. Initial development during Q4 2026 will focus on finalising the architecture and drafting Chapters 1 through 3. Publication is targeted for Q2 2027 to coincide with the broader thesis completion window and expansion of consulting activities. Future updates will be triggered by Data Protection Board notifications relating to breach reporting timelines, significant MeitY guidance, amendments to DPDP implementation requirements, or new RBI instruments affecting AI-enabled data governance.

## **Strategic Value**

The DPDP Playbook converts statutory privacy obligations into operational governance practices at a time when organisations are struggling to implement DPDP requirements alongside rapidly expanding AI deployments. The product operationalises governance insights emerging from P2 and P4 while providing a practical bridge between academic research and enterprise implementation. As a practitioner-focused asset, it serves as a foundation for workshops, advisory engagements, compliance diagnostics, and recurring privacy-governance consulting services.
