## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Core regulatory-philosophy contrast: the EU applies a "rights-based precautionary paradigm" (GDPR's minimal-rights framework supplemented by the risk-based, technology-focused AI Act) versus India's "innovation-led techno-legal utilitarianism" embodied in the DPDP Act 2023's high-consent/Consent Manager architecture — a directly citable jurisprudential-philosophy dichotomy for fsQCA condition-coding (feeds S5/S3).
2. Identifies a structurally significant divergence: the DPDP Act grants the Indian government extensive national-security/law-enforcement exemptions that "privatize... an apparatus of state surveillance," shifting sovereign-access risk onto private data fiduciaries — a strong empirical anchor for [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] analyses of data-sovereignty vs privacy tension (feeds S5/S2).
3. Highlights the absence in the DPDP Act of an EU-AI-Act/GDPR-Article-22-equivalent explicit right against solely automated decision-making — a precise legal-textual gap directly relevant to ADM accountability debates (feeds S5).
4. For MNEs, recommends a "globally integrated governance model founded on risk classification" (EU AI Act style) layered with India-specific compliance modules addressing mandatory data retention and sovereign-access risk — a practical dual-track compliance framework reusable for consulting deliverables (feeds S2/S3).
5. Frames algorithmic opacity/black-box problems as arising from both deliberate IP/trade-secret obscurity AND inherent mathematical complexity — a two-pronged taxonomy of explainability barriers useful for distinguishing technical vs strategic transparency gaps (feeds S5/S6).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse
