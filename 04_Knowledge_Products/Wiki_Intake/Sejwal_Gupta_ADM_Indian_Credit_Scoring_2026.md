## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Documents RBI's **Digital Lending Directions (May 2025)** — a regulatory development not previously captured in the corpus — which mandate consent-notice specificity (in any of 22 scheduled Indian languages), prohibit mechanised credit-limit increases without borrower-initiated request, and require "assessorial check" before automated credit decisions (feeds S2/S6).
2. Core diagnostic gap: the DPDP Act 2023 governs *data input* (consent, minimisation) but "may not fully address the imperative for explainability or the mitigation of proxy variables that lead to unfair lending outcomes" — i.e., India's privacy law addresses collection but not algorithmic *output* accountability — a precise articulation of a regulatory-coverage gap directly useful for [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] analysis (feeds S5/S2).
3. Identifies a structural enforcement paradox: the data principal's "Right to Correction or Erasure" becomes "practically moot" in high-risk ADM where "the input data's precise contribution to a credit decision cannot be traced to the individual" — black-box opacity defeats a nominally-granted data right (feeds S5/S6).
4. Quantifies the scale/urgency: India's digital lending market carries "outstanding debt of loans of more than half a trillion dollars," growing at "over 25 percent annually" (as of late 2025) — a strong scale-of-stakes statistic for credit-scoring AI governance arguments (feeds S2). ⚠ VERIFY — figure attributed to a footnoted source not independently confirmed in this extract.
5. Concrete policy recommendations directly comparable to EU/US/China regimes: mandatory independent algorithmic-bias audits, a "Right to Explanation" for adverse credit decisions, and improved inter-regulator coordination (DPDP Board ↔ RBI) — a reusable three-point reform checklist for practitioner briefings (feeds S2/S6).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

- The "input controls vs. output accountability" gap framing is a sharp, reusable explainer for why DPDP-Act compliance alone does not satisfy AI-governance expectations in lending — strong AEGIS_OS positioning angle (compliance ≠ governance).
- The three-point reform checklist (bias audits / right to explanation / regulator coordination) is directly usable as a practitioner-facing recommendations slide.
