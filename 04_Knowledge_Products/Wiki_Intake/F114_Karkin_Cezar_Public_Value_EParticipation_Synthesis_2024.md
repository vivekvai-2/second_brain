## Key Findings and Framework

### Three forms of e-participation (Reddick, 2011) — extends the cluster's participation-depth vocabulary

**Managerial** (one-way interaction — government informs), **consultative** (two-way interaction — government solicits input), **multi-way/participatory** (citizens co-design and co-implement). A third independently-derived participation-depth taxonomy in the P2 cluster, alongside F107's User/Governance/Design/End-result typology and F111's elite/mainstream/societal public-sphere model — managerial e-participation maps onto the "societal sphere"/End-result-only position; multi-way participation maps onto the "elite sphere"/Governance-level position.

### Public Value (PV) — core construct and its conditionality

PV is "the creation of added value beyond the provision of benefits or contributions that government actions and discourses bring to society" (Moore, 1995; Witesman, 2021) — multifaceted, spanning social, cultural, environmental, and economic dimensions. The review's central finding: "e-participation projects have significant potential to create PV **conditional upon** endogenous and exogenous variables" — PV generation is not automatic from the mere existence of a participation channel. This is the cluster's **third independent convergent finding** on the same theme F111 documents (institutional factors dominate responsiveness) and F107 documents (User/Governance necessary-but-insufficient for Design/End-result): participation infrastructure alone does not guarantee governance value; institutional and contextual conditions determine the outcome.

### Endogenous/exogenous variable taxonomy (Table 3) — directly reusable

**Endogenous:** political/leadership, cultural, organisational/institutional, technological, normative/instrumental values, business-style redesign. **Exogenous:** networked governance, perceptions/expectations/acceptance/adoption level, paradigms, novel tools/methods, normative/instrumental values. A directly reusable structure for analysing what drives (or blocks) public-value generation from India's DPI-adjacent citizen-participation channels — most of which (Aadhaar, UPI) are transaction-processing/managerial rather than consultative or multi-way in Reddick's sense.

### Explicit citable call for AI-in-e-participation governance research

The paper's conclusion states: "Future research should concentrate on the potential advantages and risks involved in using artificial intelligence (AI) in e-participation, the design and implementation of AI-powered e-participation platforms, and the impact of AI on PV creation," and further calls for research on "the ethical and legal ramifications of using AI in e-participation" and "best practices for ensuring such initiatives uphold democratic values and respect citizens' rights and privacy." A directly citable, published (2024) gap-identification for positioning PRIS's own AI-governance research (P1, AI Governance Wiki) as addressing an explicitly flagged open question in the e-government/public-value literature, not merely applying AI-governance frameworks to a pre-existing debate.
