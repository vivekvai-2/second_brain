## Key Findings and Framework

### Five AI tensions (Section 4.2.4) — directly citable for AI Governance Wiki S1

1. **Automation versus augmentation** — whether AI replaces or assists human decision-makers.
2. **Nudging versus autonomy** — AI-driven behavioural influence versus preserving individual/citizen autonomy.
3. **Data accessibility versus security and privacy** — open-data/interoperability goals versus data-protection obligations.
4. **Predictive accuracy versus discrimination, biases, citizen rights** — model performance versus fairness/rights protection.
5. **Predictive accuracy versus transparency and accountability versus gaming the system** — a three-way trade-off: more transparent/accountable systems can become more "gameable" by users seeking to manipulate predictable decision rules.

Distinguished as either **"true dilemmas"** (values inherently contradictory) or **"dilemmas in practice"** (arising from technology/resource limitations) (Whittlestone et al., 2019) — a useful analytical distinction for classifying which of PRIS's own documented AI-governance tensions are structural versus merely current-state-technical.

### "Algorithmic bureaucracy" — direct cross-link to F116

Henman's (2019) concept: AI transforms street-level bureaucrats into **"system-level bureaucrats"** — extending Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy theory to describe how AI reshapes (rather than simply replaces) the discretion-exercising role of frontline public administrators. Directly complements [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F116_Liu_Zhou_Liang_Digital_Inclusion_Vulnerable_Groups_2025|F116]]'s "automated systems... in lieu of a civil servant" definition and its street-level-intermediary framing, and further grounds [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Sejwal_Gupta_ADM_Indian_Credit_Scoring_2026]]'s ADM analysis in a named, citable theoretical lineage (Lipsky → Henman → algorithmic/system-level bureaucracy).

### TOE-for-AI — extends F109's general diffusion framework to AI specifically

Applies the Technology-Organisation-Environment framework (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990) specifically to AI adoption, adding a fourth cross-cutting theme, **absorptive capacity** (internal antecedents: mental models/structures; external antecedents: environmental conditions/knowledge characteristics — Lane, Koka & Pathak, 2006). Environmental pressures are further split into **vertical pressures** (policy signals, government mandates) and **horizontal pressures** (intergovernmental competition, citizen demands, industry pressure, media scrutiny) — directly useful for analysing what is driving India's AI-in-public-administration initiatives (IndiaAI Mission, NITI Aayog Responsible AI) along the same vertical/horizontal-pressure axes already used elsewhere in the corpus's TOE-derived analysis ([[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F109_Zhang_Xu_Xiao_Diffusion_Egovernment_Literature_Review_2014|F109]]).

### Named comparative national AI initiatives

Canada's digital-first directives (2021), UK's GovTech fund under the AI Sector Deal (2019), US's National AI Initiative (2021), UAE's National AI Strategy 2031 (2021), EU's GDPR (as an environmental/regulatory pressure on AI adoption) — a compact comparator list for positioning India's own national AI initiatives internationally.

### Field-maturity indicators

85% of the 73 reviewed publications are from 2019 onward (field is very recent); only 10% employ quantitative methods (58% are conceptual/literature reviews) — a citable maturity/evidence-rigor gap consistent with the corpus's recurring rhetoric-vs-evidence-gap thread (F108, F110, F117).
