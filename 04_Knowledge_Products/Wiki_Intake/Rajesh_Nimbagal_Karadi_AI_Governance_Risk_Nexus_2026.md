## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Proposes a three-dimensional conceptual framework — AI operating models, governance maturity, risk management integration — as the institutional "nexus" shaping AI efficiency outcomes (feeds S6/S1).
2. Private and foreign banks allocate ~12–18% of IT budgets to AI vs 6–8% for public-sector banks (2020–2024 proxy data), with >50% process AI-enablement vs <30% for PSBs (feeds S2).
3. Functional skew: public-sector banks concentrate AI in low-decision-risk functions (70% chatbot use) while credit appraisal/treasury AI use stays below 30%; private/foreign banks exceed 65–72% in these higher-risk functions (feeds S6).
4. Core claim — the "governance–risk nexus" moderates the AI–efficiency relationship: AI investment improves performance only when paired with mature oversight; without it, efficiency gains are inconsistent despite comparable spend (feeds S6/S1).
5. Reframes AI scaling in banking as an organisational/institutional transformation challenge rather than a purely technological upgrade — directly echoes [[Governance_Capacity]] framing (feeds S1).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

- The public/private/foreign-bank AI-spend and functional-distribution tables (Tables 1–2) are directly reusable as a benchmarking slide for AEGIS_OS positioning — illustrates the "governance maturity gap" Indian PSBs face relative to private/foreign peers.
- The "governance–risk nexus" framing is a useful practitioner shorthand for explaining to BFSI boards why AI investment alone does not de-risk operations.
