## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Silent, unpriced AI agent exposure** (feeds S6/S7): Frontier AI agent risk currently sits largely as "silent coverage" inside existing cyber, professional, and general liability policies rather than being explicitly priced, even as enterprise frontier-AI spend grew over 300% in 2025. Only around one in five surveyed businesses report a mature governance model for autonomous agents.

2. **Eight-component AI insurance stack** (feeds S6): The report proposes a reusable implementation stack — incident data collection, accumulation-risk/CAT modelling, standard setting, contract design, risk selection, pricing, ongoing monitoring, and claims management — structurally analogous to a governance-maturity framework and directly portable to non-insurance AI governance program design.

3. **Foundation-model concentration creates correlated-loss risk** (feeds S6/S7): Over 80% of AI agent deployments depend on just three foundation model providers, creating systemic correlated-loss exposure structurally similar to third-party/vendor concentration risk patterns already tracked in BFSI AI governance.

4. **Historical-precedent argument for industry coordination** (feeds S6): The report argues affirmative AI coverage at scale requires industry-wide coordination analogous to Underwriters Laboratories (founded 1894) and the medical-malpractice Closed Claims Project — a transferable "how regulated industries build shared safety infrastructure" narrative.

5. **Catastrophic tail risk needs purpose-built institutions** (feeds S6): For catastrophic/systemic frontier-AI risk (CBRN, critical-infrastructure collapse, loss-of-control scenarios), the report argues conventional insurance is insufficient and calls for purpose-built institutions — a dedicated mutual for frontier AI developers, catastrophe bonds, bespoke liability regimes, and government backstops.
