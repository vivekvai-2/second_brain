## Key Findings and Framework

### Computationally Intensive Theory Construction (CITC) — full protocol (Episode 37)

Berente, Seidel & Safadi (2019) / Miranda et al. (2022): a five-step protocol for building theory from large-scale computational processes (ML, agent-based simulation, NLP, network analysis) — (1) start with a substantive IS phenomenon justifying computational investigation; (2) be transparent about the computational process and its limitations; (3) ground theoretical interpretation of patterns in prior IS theory; (4) preregister exploratory patterns where possible to distinguish CITC from HARKing (Hypothesising After the Results Are Known); (5) anticipate reviewer resistance and cite the Miranda et al. (2022) primer directly. **This extends F097's brief, unelaborated mention of the same Berente-Seidel-Safadi (2019) citation** (Episode 5, "Method-ism") with the actual actionable methodology — noted here rather than retroactively edited into F097, per standing practice of not rewriting closed nodes. Distinct from fsQCA (a configurational, not computational-discovery, method); not added to `08_Methods/fsQCA.md`, since CITC is a different method family and no documented PRIS methods gap currently calls for it — flagged here as a candidate future method note only if a computational-theory-building need arises.

### Grounded Theory — three variants and a citation-hygiene discipline (Episode 28, "Naughty Grounded Theory")

Distinguishes Glaserian GT (Glaser & Strauss 1967 — "blank slate," emergence-first), Straussian GT (Strauss & Corbin 1998 — systematic axial coding), and Constructivist GT (Charmaz 2006 — researcher as co-constructor), explicitly cautioned against conflation with the unrelated Gioia methodology. Advisory: "do not cite GT in a paper unless you have genuinely used constant comparison from the start; theoretical sampling — not convenience sampling — drives GT's rigour." A citation-hygiene discipline directly transferable to any future PRIS qualitative-coding work (NVivo-based coding is already part of Vivek's stated methodological toolkit) — a caution against loosely invoking "grounded theory" language for what is actually template-based thematic coding.

### The Berente Rules for Starting Collaborations (Episode 33 context, collaboration-management thread)

Four heuristics: (1) only start a collaboration if it solves a research problem you could not solve alone; (2) agree on the publication venue *before* data collection; (3) have the "awkward conversation" about authorship and contribution at the start, not the end; (4) protect collaborative energy with regular meeting rhythms even during low-output phases. Directly extends F097's CRediT/gift-authorship discipline (Episode 8) with front-loaded collaboration-design advice — the two together form a complete authorship-and-collaboration governance pair.

### Hostile Q&A — a three-mode response framework, plus resilience architecture (Episode 34, "When Your Audience Is Full of Jerks"; Episode 35, "Resilience Is Key")

Three response modes for adversarial conference questions: engage-and-rebut, deflect-and-acknowledge, or transcend. Distinguishes productive cognitive conflict (challenging ideas, Jehn 1997) from destructive affective conflict (attacking the person). Advisory: identify one's own "resilience architecture early — before the first major rejection" rather than improvising under pressure, and distinguish productive envy (motivation) from corrosive envy (paralysis). **Directly relevant to PhD defence and conference-presentation preparation** given the Q3–Q4 2027 defence window in [[01_Strategy/VV_Publication_Pipeline_v3]] — a practical framework worth revisiting closer to the defence date.

### Engaged Scholarship and the four-element relevance framework (Episode 39, "Engagement, Relevance, and Beautiful Artifacts")

Van de Ven (2007)'s Engaged Scholarship thesis — research done *with* practice, not merely *about* it — paired with Rosemann's four-element framework for design-science relevance: problem embeddedness, artifact co-design, applicability check, and aesthetic standard ("making the artifact beautiful"). **Directly maps onto Vivek's own practitioner-researcher positioning** (active consulting inbounds AEGIS_OS/PrivacyWeave alongside the academic pipeline) — a citable theoretical anchor for framing consulting-academic integration as a deliberate scholarly method (engaged scholarship) rather than a career-management compromise.

### Journal-fit discipline (Episode 40, "Journals Are Like Newspapers Except That They're Not")

Advisory: match the manuscript to the target journal's theory orientation, method preferences, and review culture; read recent editors' comments/editorials from the target journal before submission to calibrate framing. A direct, actionable addition to the Drafting Discipline Protocol's pre-submission checklist, reinforcing (from a different episode than F097/F098) the same narrative-calibration discipline the GIQ rejection exposed as missing.

### Pragmatism as throughline (Episode 32, "Pragmatism, Baby!")

Confirms pragmatism (Peirce/James/Dewey) as the hosts' explicit, named philosophical home — consistent with the implicit pragmatist framing already noted in F097 (Episode 1) — now given a dedicated episode. Noted for continuity; no new PRIS action required.
