## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Provides a technique-by-technique regulatory mapping table (Table 1: LLMs, GANs/VAEs, Reinforcement Learning) cross-referencing each AI technique against India-specific use cases AND India-specific regulatory constraints — e.g., LLMs face DPDP Act 2023 data-residency requirements and "hallucinations in regulatory advice" risk; RL-based algo trading falls under SEBI's Black Box algo approval/Research Analyst registration regime; GANs/VAEs face "RBI restrictions on synthetic financial data" — a directly reusable taxonomy linking AI architecture choice to applicable Indian regulator (feeds S2/S4).
2. Frames India's Digital Public Infrastructure (UPI, Aadhaar, India Stack — billions of transactions, >1bn Aadhaar enrolments) as both the enabling substrate AND the distinct risk surface for GenAI in finance — directly extends P2's DPI-governance research focus into a GenAI-specific application (feeds S2).
3. Identifies multilingual/Hinglish/vernacular NLP capability as a structural precondition for inclusive GenAI deployment in India — >98% of India's ~900m projected (2025) internet users consume content in an Indic language, per Kantar-IAMAI 2024 — a citable inclusion-equity data point (feeds S2/S4).
4. Applies Actor-Network Theory (ANT) to map GenAI adoption as a socio-technical "translation" process involving human and non-human actors (regulators, models, infrastructure, users) — offers a theoretical scaffold alternative/complement to institutional theory for analysing AI governance diffusion in BFSI (feeds S1/theoretical scaffolding candidates).
5. Recommends concrete technical governance thresholds (e.g., BLEU score >0.3 acceptable / >0.5 recommended for production GenAI deployment) plus ongoing qualitative audits — a rare instance in the corpus of quantified, auditable AI-performance governance benchmarks (feeds S4/S6).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse
