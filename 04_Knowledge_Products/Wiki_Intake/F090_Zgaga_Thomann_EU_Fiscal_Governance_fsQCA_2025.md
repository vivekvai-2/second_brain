### Definitions (Density/Restrictiveness Framework)

- **Literal compliance (LIT):** member states transpose the same number of rules (unchanged density) and the same stringency (unchanged restrictiveness) as the original EU rule.
- **Customized compliance (~LIT):** either density or restrictiveness (or both) is changed during transposition.
- **Density:** whether the national rule has more/fewer elements (instrument, definition, scope, restrictions, requirements, conditions, exemptions) than the EU rule.
- **Restrictiveness/intensity:** whether the national rule is more/less stringent than the EU rule.
- **Policy fit / goodness of fit (FIT):** compatibility of the European model with domestic arrangements; **policy misfit** = fundamental clash between EU legislative goals and domestic policy legacies.
- **Flexibility (FLEX) / rigid rule (~FLEX):** whether a rule permits multiple practical compliance pathways.

This density/restrictiveness decomposition is structurally similar to PRIS's own analytical vocabulary for comparing regulatory instruments across regulators (e.g., how RBI vs. SEBI AI circulars differ in scope/stringency) — **a candidate analytical lens, not evidence**, noted for awareness only since the paper contains no AI or India content.

### Headline Finding

*"When the EU gives member states both the carrot and the stick, then they comply literally even in an area of core state powers."* External pressure (Excessive Deficit Procedure threat, market refinancing costs, high deficit) combined with internal fit/flexibility conditions jointly determine literal vs. customized compliance — a clean equifinal (multiple-paths) result with very high solution consistency (up to 1.0), useful as a worked example of unusually clean fsQCA results for methods-section citation.
