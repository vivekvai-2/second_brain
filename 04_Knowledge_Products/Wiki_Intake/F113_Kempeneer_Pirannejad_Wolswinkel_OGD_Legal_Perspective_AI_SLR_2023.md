## Key Findings and Framework

### Three-dimension OGD-law taxonomy — direct P2 relevance

OGD law is analysed along three dimensions: **topics** (access to information / re-use of information / concurring legal interests such as privacy, copyright, national security); **sources** (legislation/statutory law / policy rules/soft law / case-law); **levels** (international / national / local-decentral). A directly reusable structure for mapping India's DPI legal architecture: DPDP Act 2025 and sectoral regulations = legislation; MeitY/RBI/SEBI circulars = soft law/policy rules; an emerging (thin) case-law layer. The topics dimension's third category — "concurring legal interests" — maps precisely onto the DPDP-vs-DLT tension P4 already documents (TRAI_1's spam-detection DLT mandate vs. MeitY_4's DPDP erasure right, [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] §Batch 15 continuation).

### "Strict compliance" vs. "concordance" — direct Institutional_Coherence relevance

A replicated Dutch field experiment found that Freedom of Information requests actually **triggered proactive disclosure beyond what the law strictly required** — the paper terms this "concordance" (behaving in ways that move beyond the law) as distinct from "strict compliance" (doing exactly as the law asks). A citable empirical instance that administrative practice can exceed formal legal obligation — directly relevant to Institutional_Coherence's obligation-consistency dimension, since it demonstrates that the gap between legal text and administrative reality can run in either direction (under- or over-compliance), not only toward under-implementation as the corpus's Indian regulatory-instrument evidence typically documents.

### AI-assisted screening methodology — P1 methods precedent

The review used **ASReview**, an open-source active-learning tool, to triage 2,598 title/abstract records after an initial 30-record "prior knowledge" training set (15 relevant, 15 irrelevant). The stopping criterion (top 100 relevant records) was reached after reviewing only **522 records — approximately 20% of the full candidate pool** — a roughly 80% reduction in manual screening burden relative to reviewing all 2,598 records individually. A concrete, quantified efficiency precedent for P1's own SLR reactivation, complementary to F096's search-*strategy* gap-fill: F096 addresses how to find candidate studies; F113 addresses how to triage a large candidate pool efficiently once found. Flagged as a candidate addition to [[08_Methods/SLR_Protocol]]'s documented search-methodology gap.

### Descriptive statistics — positioning context

56 of 105 included articles categorised under public administration, 49 under law — the legal dimension of OGD is nearly as well-studied as the public-administration dimension, useful positioning context if P2 needs to justify a legally-inflected DPI governance analysis. Geographic distribution again shows the same imbalance F108 documents: Europe (49) and Americas (28) dominate; Asia (6) and Africa (2) are marginal — reinforcing the case for India-focused DPI legal research as filling a documented gap.
