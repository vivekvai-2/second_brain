## Key Findings and Framework

### Data objects — the paper's central construct

Defined as "objects made of data structured and aggregated under a specific template," which "rather than representing existing entities... construct new entities out of data produced in dispersed digital environments." A sharper, more precisely specified companion construct to Kallinikos, Aaltonen & Marton's (2013) four properties (editability, interactivity, openness, distributedness) — this paper treats data objects as the unit of analysis for field structuration specifically, rather than as a general ontological category.

### Fields, structuration, and object-oriented practices

Draws on DiMaggio & Powell (1983)'s field definition and Fligstein & McAdam (2012)'s strategic action field, but argues that in datafied fields, structuration ("processes of reproduction and stabilisation of structures arising from... interaction patterns") occurs through **object-oriented practices** — coordinated human action organised around maintaining, repairing, and adjusting data objects and the protocols that define them (RTB protocol, OpenRTB JSON/protobuf, AdCOM) — rather than through the discursive/normative structuration processes classical institutional theory emphasises. Central finding: "the space left to organizing is a space of adjusting and repairing" — practices informants themselves called "bug fixing," "repairing," "model tweaking."

### Worked case — programmatic advertising exchange (24-month ethnography)

A single-case ethnographic study (TD, pseudonym, London-headquartered demand-side platform/trading desk; 20 semi-structured interviews, 50 internal documents, 35 IAB documents, 55 external documents, 12 US patent applications) of the real-time bidding (RTB) protocol governing programmatic ad exchange. Documents the ad-impression object (Figure 3) as a fully specified, protocol-governed data object — a bid request "needs to contain at least one ad impression object to trigger the exchange," processed "under 100 milliseconds," with GDPR compliance embedded directly in the object specification ("Implementer should ensure compliance with regional legislation around data usage and sharing"). Quantified scale: 86.5% of US digital display advertising spend was programmatic as of 2021 (86% cited again in the Discussion section as of publication).

### Algorithms are "blind without data objects"

A citable, sharply-formulated claim: "Algorithms are powerful computational devices but essentially blind without data and data objects... Algorithms do not act upon, count, order or cluster real-life entities... but their datafied version." Directly relevant vocabulary for any future PRIS argument (P2 or otherwise) about the gap between a governed "real" entity (a citizen, a data subject) and its datafied representation within a governance system — the same representation-vs-referent distinction underlying P2's records-rules asymmetry, though this paper does not itself address citizen/state governance.

### GDPR embedded as object specification, not external obligation

Figure 1's rendition of a "user object" in digital advertising explicitly embeds "GDPR, data regulations, etc." as fields within the object's technical specification itself, alongside implementer obligations phrased in mixed deontic strength ("should ensure compliance," "must be stable long enough," "strongly recommended"). This is a concrete empirical instance of regulatory obligation being encoded directly into a technical artefact's schema — structurally comparable to [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F105_Goldkuhl_Egov_Design_Research_Policy_Ingrained_Artifact_2016|F105]]'s "policy-ingrained IT artifact" construct, though from a private-sector adtech rather than e-government context.
