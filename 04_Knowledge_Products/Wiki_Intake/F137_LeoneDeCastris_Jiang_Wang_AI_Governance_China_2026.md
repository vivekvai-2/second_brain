## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Departmental-rule-first regulatory sequencing** (feeds S3): China regulates AI primarily through application-specific departmental rules and interim measures (algorithmic recommendation 2022, deep synthesis 2023, generative AI 2023, AI-generated content labelling 2025, anthropomorphic interaction 2026), issued ahead of a comprehensive national AI law — prioritising regulatory agility over legislative comprehensiveness.

2. **Legal-hierarchy ceiling on departmental rules** (feeds S3): Departmental rules sit below national laws and administrative regulations in China's legal hierarchy and cannot independently expand departmental powers or reduce statutory rights — a structural constraint on how far agile administrative rulemaking can substitute for primary legislation. Directly comparable to India's Rules-under-Act structure (DPDP Rules 2025 vs DPDP Act).

3. **Centralised standardisation as a competitiveness lever** (feeds S3): AI standard-setting is centrally coordinated by the Standardisation Administration of China (SAC) and explicitly positioned by the state as a tool for international competitiveness, not solely domestic technical harmonisation.

4. **2017 AIDP three-step national roadmap** (feeds S3): China's foundational AI strategy (New Generation AI Development Plan, 2017) set time-bound national milestones (2020 / 2025 / 2030) tying AI governance directly to industrial-policy targets — a useful timeline-discipline comparator for NITI Aayog's #AIforAll strategy.

5. **Anthropomorphic-interaction services regulated as a relational/psychological risk category** (feeds S3/S5): The Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interaction Services (effective 15 July 2026) target AI companionship, emotional-care, and continuous-emotional-interaction services specifically — imposing obligations to prevent content that encourages self-harm/suicide, induces emotional dependence or addiction, damages users' real interpersonal relationships, or manipulates users into unreasonable decisions, plus embedded risk warnings and mental-health/emotional-boundary guidance. This is a distinct instrument from the March 2025 AI-generated-content labelling measures (which target provenance/labelling of synthetic media, not relational risk) — see VERIFY #294 resolution below.

## ⚠ CORRECTION 2026-08-04 — the "single-coordinator" contrast is overstated

This note originally characterised China as a **"single-coordinator departmental-rule model"** contrasting with India's multi-regulator parallelism. [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F139_ConcordiaAI_State_of_AI_Safety_China_2026]] does not support that framing at the standards layer.

Three Chinese standards bodies were **simultaneously drafting overlapping agent-security standards** as of June 2026 — TC260/WG9, MIIT/TC1/WG8, and SAC/TC28/SC42. Concordia states directly: *"There is some uncertainty about how TC260 will coordinate these efforts with other standard-setting bodies. For example, MIIT/TC1 is also working on multiple related standards."* Separately, the relationship between MIIT's new ethics-review regime and the CAC algorithm registry is unresolved (Art. 26 hints at exemption; implementation unclear pending the ten-province pilot) — an [[Assurance_Reuse_Gap]] instance.

**What survives:** China's *rulemaking* layer is genuinely more centralised than India's — the CAC leads, ministries co-sign, and the algorithm registry is a single enforcement spine. The contrast is real at instrument level.

**What does not:** the implication that China avoids parallelism. It does not. Parallelism appears at the standards and review layers, inside a unitary state with centralised standards coordination.

**Why this matters beyond China.** If [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] occurs in a centralised unitary system, it is a property of **mandate architecture**, not of federal or multi-regulator political structure. That strengthens the construct's generalisability and weakens any framing of India's parallelism as a distinctively Indian institutional failure. Use the corrected contrast as *degree*, not *kind*.
