## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Japan's "framework law" approach — innovation-first, non-punitive** (feeds S3): Japan's AI Bill (submitted to National Diet, February 28, 2025) establishes a "framework law" — it creates an Artificial Intelligence Strategy Headquarters within the Cabinet and formulates basic government plans, but imposes only a *duty to co-operate* on businesses (no penalties for non-compliance). Unlike the EU AI Act, Japan's approach explicitly prioritises innovation promotion over compliance burden — making Japan the clearest G7 non-EU counterpart to Australia's and India's advisory-only postures in the fsQCA comparative analysis.

2. **Sector-fragmented regulatory architecture — no cross-sectoral AI authority** (feeds S3/S2): Japan has no unified AI regulator. Oversight is distributed: METI (unfair competition, AI contracts), MIC (data protection, internet), MHLW (labour + healthcare AI), PMDA (medical devices), FSA (financial services AI), JFTC (competition/antitrust). The FSA governs AI in financial services under existing financial regulation, not AI-specific rules. This directly parallels India's multi-regulator (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/MeitY) architecture — both share the same structural [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] pattern without a coordinating body.

3. **Financial services AI — existing regulation applied, no AI-specific rules** (feeds S3/S6): Japan's Financial Services Agency (FSA) applies existing financial regulation (suitability, conduct of business, AML) to AI-enabled financial services. Major Japanese banks have deployed generative AI for automated reporting, personalised financial advice, and enhanced customer support — but under existing financial sector rules without AI-specific disclosure or accountability obligations. The AI Bill does not create new FSA AI obligations. Comparable to RBI's 2017 opportunity-framing posture (RBI_4) — AI in BFSI expanding rapidly, regulatory framework essentially unchanged.

4. **APPI copyright-AI interface: pro-innovation but contested** (feeds S3): Japan's Act on Protection of Personal Information (APPI) and Copyright Act (Article 30-4) are significantly more permissive for AI training data use than the EU GDPR. Article 30-4 permits use of copyrighted works for "information analysis" without rights-holder consent — one of the most AI-friendly IP regimes globally. The Agency for Cultural Affairs issued AI/copyright guidance in July 2024, but it is non-binding. This creates a meaningful regulatory divergence from the EU's AI Act + GDPR combination — Japan explicitly chose innovation-permissive IP for AI training.

5. **Judicial precedent — algorithmic transparency as court-imposed obligation** (feeds S3/S6): The Tokyo District Court's June 2022 Tabelog ruling (restaurant ratings algorithm antitrust case) established that unilateral algorithm changes to the disadvantage of users can constitute "abuse of superior bargaining position" under Japan's Anti-Monopoly Act. Notably, the operators' initial refusal to disclose the algorithm itself was raised in litigation. This is the closest Japan has to a judicially-imposed algorithmic transparency obligation — arising from competition law, not AI regulation.
