## Key Findings and Framework

### Cape Town "Day Zero" — direct positive comparator to records-rules asymmetry

Van Belle & Hlabano's (2019) documented Cape Town water-crisis OGD strategy combined four channels: (1) public broadcast of the "Day Zero" (taps-run-dry) projection date, (2) release of detailed water-usage statistics by suburb, (3) a map-based visualisation of household-level water consumption, and (4) crowdsourced real-time reporting of water losses via social media. Result: **a 50% reduction in citizen water consumption within less than a month**. This is the clearest quantified example in the corpus of an open-data system that activated citizens as participatory, behaviour-changing actors (P2's "voice"/Governance-level citizen-centricity) rather than merely as passive recipients of transparency data (the "vision"/End-result-only pattern F107 and F108 document as typical) — a valuable **positive counterfactual**: the records-rules asymmetry is not structurally inevitable, and this case shows what a design that closes it can achieve.

### Four OGD-visualization research streams

(1) description/evaluation of specific visualization examples (n=18 in the reviewed set); (2) general tools/frameworks (n=8); (3) stakeholder needs assessments (n=4); (4) usability/user-outcome evaluations (n=9). A structural map of the field, useful for positioning any future PRIS work on Indian DPI data-portal usability (e.g., India's Open Government Data platform, RBI/SEBI public data releases) against the existing research landscape.

### Munzner (2014) visualization design principles — directly reusable practitioner guidance

Six principles distilled from the review: avoid unnecessary 3D visualisation; prefer simple structures (lists) over complex ones (node-link networks) where the data allows; offer multiple coordinated views where possible; sequence presentations as overview → zoom/filter → details-on-demand; ensure colour-vision-deficiency-accessible palettes; prioritise functionality over novelty/beauty. Directly transferable to PRIS's own consulting deliverables, teaching materials, and LinkedIn carousel/content design work — not just to DPI-portal analysis.

### Rigor gap — parallel to F108's own finding

Among the studies that included any usability evaluation, less than half (3 of 8) used a **formal** usability-evaluation method (structured social-science research methods with primary or secondary data); the majority used informal/narrative description of the tool's use in practice. A second, independent instance in the corpus of an evidence-rigor gap in government-technology research, directly parallel to F108's own "8 of 75" rhetoric-vs-evidence finding for open government generally.

### Stakeholder taxonomy

Graves & Hendler's (2013) five-type OGD user-profile taxonomy — government data provider, government data consumer, researcher/journalist, civil programmer, ordinary citizens — a reusable stakeholder-mapping structure for any PRIS analysis of who actually uses a given DPI/open-data portal and for what.
