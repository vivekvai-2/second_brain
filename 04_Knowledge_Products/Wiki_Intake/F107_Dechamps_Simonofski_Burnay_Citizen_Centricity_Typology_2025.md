## Key Findings and Framework

### Four competing understandings of citizen-centricity

Derived from SLR + interview triangulation:

1. **User understanding** — detailed comprehension of users at every stage of digital-government transformation ("a culture more than a specific set of tasks... or a checklist," per one interviewee).
2. **Governance understanding** — an administrative mindset and shared organisational culture.
3. **Design understanding** — a set of processes/techniques (e.g., user-centred design, value-sensitive design) considered citizen-centric regardless of outcome.
4. **End-result understanding** — achieving determined characteristics of the digital public service itself (context-aware interface, quality, sustainability).

### Necessity/sequencing relationship — the paper's sharpest finding, direct P2 relevance

"**User and Governance understandings are necessary but not sufficient premises for Design and End-result understandings**." User and Design understandings are directly actionable by public agents; Governance and End-result understandings are only indirectly actionable. This means a DPI system can score well on End-result citizen-centricity (efficient transactional service delivery — the register/records-processing function) while remaining structurally weak on User- and Governance-level citizen-centricity (citizens as participating, rule-shaping actors) — the same underlying asymmetry P2 already documents for India's DPI architecture, now backed by a named, empirically-derived construct rather than an ad hoc observation.

### Named empirical baselines

Only 22 of 58 reviewed studies explicitly or implicitly define citizen-centricity at all — most of the literature uses the term without defining it. Terminology is inconsistent: "citizen-centricity" (25 studies), "user-centricity" (13), "human-centricity" (5), or a hybrid (10). Within the identified characteristics: "Context aware interface" is the most-cited End-result characteristic (31 of 58 studies); "User participation" is the most-cited Design characteristic (23 studies); Governance-level characteristics are comparatively under-theorised (Organisational Culture, 10 studies; Actions, 2 studies) — itself evidence that the Governance dimension is the least-developed of the four in the existing literature, mirroring P2's own finding that citizens are weakly represented as governance-level (rule-bearing) actors.

### Mixed-methods design (SLR + interviews) — third distinct GIQ design pattern in the corpus

Combines a PICOC-framed systematic literature review (300 initial results → 58 final studies via database search, an updated search wave, and snowballing) with 14 semi-structured interviews with Belgian public agents (June–August 2023), analysed through an adapted People-Technology-Process (PPT) framework. A third methodologically distinct GIQ design pattern in the corpus, after F086's pure QCA and F089's SEM+fsQCA — reinforcing GIQ's (P1/P2-adjacent target journal) methodological pluralism.
