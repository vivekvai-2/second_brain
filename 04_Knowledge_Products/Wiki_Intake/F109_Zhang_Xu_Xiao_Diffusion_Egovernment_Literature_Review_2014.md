## Key Findings and Framework

### Eight diffusion theories identified in the literature

Diffusion of Innovation theory (Rogers, 1995), group theory, public goods theory, cultural theory, model of micro-political arenas, technology acceptance model (TAM), communication model, and collaboration perspective — a compact theory-landscape taxonomy. Directly useful if P2's theoretical-framing section needs to position its own DPI diffusion analysis against the range of theories already applied to e-government diffusion generally.

### Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework for diffusion factors

Influencing factors are classified into three categories: **technological** (ICT/broadband/interoperability), **organisational** (government-agency type/size/structure/capacity, public-servant skills, executive leadership/centralisation-vs-decentralisation), and **environmental** (macro: institutional/cultural/policy environment; meso: city size; micro: organisational culture). A directly reusable three-category taxonomy for structuring an analysis of what drove — or constrained — India's DPI diffusion (e.g., UPI's rapid diffusion vs. Account Aggregator's slower uptake could be compared using the same TOE lens).

### Push/pull forces and planned-vs-spontaneous diffusion — direct India-DPI relevance

E-government diffusion dynamics are framed as a balance between **"push forces"** (social/political dynamics, institutional motivations originating in the public sector) and **"pull forces"** (the public's own adoption of e-government services). Diffusion additionally occurs as either a **"planned/formal diffusion process"** or a **"spontaneous/informal diffusion process"** (Rogers, 1995). India's DPI rollout (Aadhaar mandated enrolment, UPI's regulator-driven interoperability mandate) is analytically a strongly **push-driven, planned diffusion** case — a useful, precisely-named contrast to more organically "pulled"/spontaneous e-government diffusion patterns documented elsewhere in the literature, worth citing if P2 needs to characterise *why* India's DPI diffusion looks structurally different from the bottom-up adoption patterns much of the general e-government diffusion literature assumes.

### Documented methods gap

The paper's own future-research call — for more longitudinal studies and a "comprehensive dynamic framework for e-government diffusion" (existing literature relies heavily on cross-sectional snapshots) — is a citable methods gap, still open a decade later per this corpus's other GIQ nodes (F108's own 2021 review does not report the gap as closed).
