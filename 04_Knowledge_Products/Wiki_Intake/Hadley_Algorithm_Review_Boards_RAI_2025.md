## Summary

Hadley et al. present the first detailed empirical investigation of Algorithm Review Boards (ARBs) in practice. Based on interviews with 17 technical contributors at organisations across sectors, the paper documents ARB membership composition, scope, and measures of success. It confirms known ARB deployment in the finance sector and reveals extensive but under-documented ARB use in the health sector. Key finding: Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) alone are insufficient for algorithm governance; ARBs operate in tandem with other RAI mechanisms. Financial tensions between profit and the cost of RAI emerged as the dominant constraint across interviewees.

---

## Key Findings

### ARB Design Dimensions Observed

| Dimension | Observed Range | Best-Practice Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Membership** | 3–15 members; typically cross-functional (technical + legal + ethics + domain) | Cross-functional composition + external member(s) |
| **Scope** | Model-by-model review vs portfolio-level oversight | Both scopes present; combined approach most robust |
| **Trigger** | Voluntary submission, pre-deployment mandatory, or ad-hoc post-incident | Pre-deployment mandatory with defined criteria |
| **Integration** | Standalone body vs embedded in existing risk committee | Embedded with leadership visibility |

### Sector Findings

- **Finance sector:** ARBs confirmed and formalised, driven by regulatory and reputational pressures
- **Health sector:** ARB use widespread but under-documented — most operate outside IRB framework
- **Cross-sector:** IRBs are insufficient; ARBs handle algorithmic-fairness, model-drift, and deployment-context risks that IRBs (research-ethics focused) do not address

### Success Factors

Interviewees identified three critical success factors:
1. Integration with existing organisational processes (not siloed)
2. Leadership buy-in and resource allocation
3. Cross-functional expertise (not solely technical)

Primary constraint across all interviewees: **financial tensions between profit maximisation and RAI investment costs**.
