## Summary

Anil & Misra present the first substantive empirical study of AI adoption in India's peer-to-peer (P2P) lending market — a segment operating under RBI's NBFC-P2P Master Directions (2017 as amended) and directly implicated in the [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] Indian BFSI thesis. Using a holistic multiple-case study design across six licensed NBFC-P2P companies with 18 semi-structured interviews, the paper documents how AI has penetrated Indian P2P lending markets, the governance structures NBFC-P2P entities have (or lack), and the road ahead. The paper is the corpus's clearest single-paper empirical grounding for the RBI NBFC-P2P segment specifically — a segment often referenced in aggregate BFSI discussion but rarely studied at case-analysis depth.

## Key Findings and Framework

### Empirical Design

- **Cases:** Six licensed NBFC-P2P Indian companies (⚠VERIFY specific company names; the paper likely names them under research-ethics disclosure)
- **Interviews:** 18 total — 6 NBFC-P2P founders + 12 FinTech/P2P industry experts
- **Analytical approach:** Cross-case pattern analysis for AI penetration + governance responses

### Central Findings (from abstract; full paper reading required for detail)

- AI adoption in Indian P2P is *at operational maturity* — used for credit scoring, borrower risk assessment, and fraud detection across all six cases
- Governance structures at NBFC-P2P vary widely, ranging from ad-hoc practitioner-driven approaches to formal board-level oversight
- P2P segment operates at the intersection of RBI NBFC-P2P Directions + DPDP Act + emerging AI-specific SEBI-adjacent expectations — a specific Regulatory_Parallelism instance
- Road ahead: interviewees expect increasing AI use combined with increasing RBI supervisory attention

### Position in Indian Regulatory Landscape

NBFC-P2P is a specific sub-segment: NBFCs licensed under RBI to operate P2P lending marketplaces without balance-sheet lending. RBI's 2017 Master Directions established the NBFC-P2P category; subsequent amendments (2019, 2021, 2024) have progressively tightened conduct and prudential requirements. AI use in NBFC-P2P is thus subject to:
- RBI NBFC-P2P Master Directions (prudential + conduct)
- RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025 ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_7|RBI_7]]) — since NBFC-P2Ps use LSPs
- DPDP Act 2023 ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/MeitY/MeitY_4_DPDP_Rules_2025|MeitY_4]]) — SDF classification depending on data volumes
- Sectoral cyber requirements (CERT-In Directions 2022)

A four-regulator overlap at the NBFC-P2P segment specifically.
