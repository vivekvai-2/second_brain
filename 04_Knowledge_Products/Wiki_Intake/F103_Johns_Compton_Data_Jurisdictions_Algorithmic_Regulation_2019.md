## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Three "ideal type" data jurisdictions** (feeds S1): the paper proposes market, public science, and humanitarian jurisdiction as three distinct normative regimes that data and algorithmic operations can simultaneously invoke — market (contractual/competitive relations, freedom-as-exit), public science (epistemological/collaborative relations, objectivity, circulatory continuity), and humanitarian (relations of giving/duty, actionable data to prevent or ameliorate harm). Each authorises different claims and forms of speech/action through the same underlying algorithmic operations.

2. **Parallelism within a single system, not just across regulators** (feeds S1/S3 — [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] instance): the comparative case study of Haze Gazer (Indonesian 2015 haze-crisis monitoring) and CycloMon (Pacific cyclone monitoring) shows a single algorithmic platform simultaneously instantiating multiple, non-coordinated jurisdictional logics at once — a market-jurisdiction data-licensing relationship (UN Global Pulse's 2016 no-cost Twitter-firehose agreement, against Twitter's own 86%-advertising/14%-data-licensing revenue split), a public-science jurisdiction (NASA MODIS satellite feeds, NOAA cyclone data), and a humanitarian jurisdiction (crisis-response framing) — coexisting inside one algorithmic complex rather than across separate regulatory instruments. This is a structurally distinct parallelism variant from the corpus's usual cross-regulator pattern: the same *system*, not different regulators, carries multiple uncoordinated normative logics.

3. **Standard-setting insufficiency argument** (feeds S1 — [[Institutional_Coherence]] instance): the paper's closing argument is that formal cross-jurisdictional standard-setting (the WEF's "public-private data collaboration" model is named explicitly) has limited effect if the underlying data formats and infrastructures in which people engage day-to-day encode divergent normative commitments — "those instruments may have little effect." This independently arrives, via governance theory rather than empirical survey, at the same coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance distinction [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]] documents empirically across 84 AI ethics-guideline issuers.

4. **Quantified humanitarian-tech case anchor** (feeds S3): the 2015 Indonesian haze crisis is precisely quantified — an estimated 100,000 premature deaths, ~475 trillion rupiah (over US$34 billion) in costs, and over 5 million children's schooling disrupted — as the empirical stakes underlying the jurisdiction comparison. A citable real-world humanitarian-tech example distinct from the corpus's largely BFSI-weighted case material.

5. **Target-journal precedent for P1** (meta-finding, not a substantive-content finding): published in *Regulation & Governance* — P1's exact target journal — this is a jurisdictional/regulatory-theory paper accepted by that journal using a comparative two-case-study design (not fsQCA), a useful methodological contrast to the corpus's growing fsQCA-in-R&G precedent cluster (F087, F088, F090). Also documents a ~3-year acceptance-to-volume-assignment lag (accepted Nov 2019, volume-dated 2022) — a data point on R&G's publication timeline relevant to P1's own submission planning.

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

**Highest-value reuse:** the three-jurisdiction ideal-type framework (market/public science/humanitarian), illustrated with the Haze Gazer case, is a vivid, non-BFSI teaching example for explaining regulatory parallelism/normative-regime conflict to non-specialist Board or policy audiences — an accessible entry point ahead of the corpus's denser BFSI regulatory-instrument material.
**Consulting connections:** None direct (no BFSI or agentic-AI-specific content); tangentially relevant to any cross-jurisdictional data-governance question AEGIS_OS or PrivacyWeave engagements might raise, but not flagged as a direct connection.
**Teaching connection:** Case study — humanitarian-tech/data-jurisdiction pluralism example (Haze Gazer/CycloMon) for teaching regulatory theory to non-technical audiences.
