## Scope Note — Why This Node Exists (Major Node)

This is the corpus's most direct and important successor study to [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]] (Jobin, Ienca & Vayena, *Nature Machine Intelligence*, 2019; 84 documents). Corrêa et al. more than double the corpus (200 vs. 84 documents), extend the time window four years further (through 2022), and — critically — perform an explicit **meta-comparison across four prior AI-ethics-guideline landscape studies** (their own, plus Jobin/Hagendorff/Fjeld), making this simultaneously a primary data source and a methodological reflection on the entire "principles landscape" research tradition that F094 founded in this corpus. It supplies the sharpest single piece of coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance evidence yet assembled — not merely convergence-with-divergent-interpretation (F094's finding), but a documented, named "Hidden costs and divergences" analysis of *why* principle convergence fails to translate into governance substance.

## Key Findings and Framework

### The 17-principle aggregated taxonomy — extends F094's 5-principle set

Corrêa et al. code 200 documents (2004–2022, expanded from earlier smaller iterations of the same authors' running catalogue) against a taxonomy of **17 ethical principles/themes**, a substantial expansion of F094's 5-principle convergence set (transparency, justice/fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility, privacy). The most frequently occurring principles remain broadly consistent with F094's finding (transparency, accountability, and privacy/data-protection cluster at the top), but the 17-principle taxonomy captures a materially wider substantive space than F094's 5, including principles largely absent from the 2019 landscape: sustainability, human oversight of automation, professional responsibility, and diversity/non-discrimination as a category distinct from general fairness.

### Four-dimension document-classification taxonomy

Documents are classified along four independent dimensions: **Nature** (the issuing sector — government, industry, civil society/academic, multistakeholder), **Form of regulation** (voluntary/soft-law vs. binding/hard-law), **Normative strength** (aspirational principle vs. operational requirement), and **Impact scope** (national, regional, or global application). This four-dimension classification is a directly reusable coding scheme for any future PRIS corpus-wide meta-analysis of the vault's own 121+ nodes, and formalises a classification logic the PRIS corpus has been applying informally (Tier A/B/C/D; Format A/B) without an explicit four-axis structure.

### The central statistic — 98% soft-law, 18.7% binding government regulation

Of the 200 documents, **98% are soft-law / non-binding** in form. Separately, only **18.7% originate from binding government regulation** (as opposed to industry self-regulation, civil-society guidance, or multistakeholder principles). Extends F094's implicit convergence-without-coordination finding into an explicit, quantified statement of the soft-law dominance of the global AI-ethics-guideline landscape at more than double F094's sample size — the single most citable statistic in the corpus for characterising the *form* (not merely the content) of global AI governance circa 2022.

### Meta-comparison across four landscape studies — Jobin (F094), Hagendorff, Fjeld, Corrêa (this paper)

The paper's most methodologically distinctive contribution: it explicitly cross-tabulates its own 200-document, 17-principle findings against three prior landscape reviews — Jobin et al. 2019 (= [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]], 84 documents, 5 aggregated principles), Hagendorff 2020 (~22 documents), and Fjeld et al. 2020 (Harvard Berkman Klein, ~36 documents, 8 thematic clusters). The comparison finds broad principle-label convergence across all four studies (transparency, accountability, and fairness/non-discrimination appear near-universally) but documents that each successive, larger-sample study surfaces additional principles absent from smaller predecessor samples — an empirical demonstration that the "five/eight/seventeen principle" landscape is itself sample-size-dependent, not a stable underlying structure. This is a direct, citable methodological caution for any PRIS synthesis that treats a fixed principle-count as an established fact rather than a corpus-size artefact.

### "Hidden costs and divergences" — the sharpest coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance evidence in the corpus

The paper's named "Hidden costs and divergences" analysis goes beyond F094's "substantive divergence in interpretation" finding to document specific mechanisms by which formally convergent principles fail to constrain practice: **divergent operational definitions** of the same principle-label across issuers (e.g., "fairness" variously operationalised as demographic parity, equalised odds, or individual fairness with no cross-document reconciliation); **absent enforcement mechanisms** for the 98% soft-law majority, meaning formal principle-adoption carries no compliance cost; and **selective principle adoption** — issuers cite convergent principle labels while omitting the specific operational commitments (audit rights, redress mechanisms, liability allocation) that would make those principles enforceable. This is the corpus's most explicit, named articulation of the coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance distinction that [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F103_Johns_Compton_Data_Jurisdictions_Algorithmic_Regulation_2019|F103]] reaches independently via governance theory and F094 documents empirically at half the sample size — this paper makes the mechanism of the gap, not just its existence, the explicit object of analysis.

## Relevance to PRIS Research

### Institutional_Coherence — direct, heavy-weight instance

The 98%-soft-law/18.7%-binding-regulation statistic and the "Hidden costs and divergences" analysis together constitute the corpus's sharpest, most explicitly-argued coherence-in-form/incoherence-in-substance evidence at global scale — see Instances in Corpus edit below.

### Regulatory_Parallelism — extends F094's global-scale evidence at 2.4x sample size

Extends F094's principle-convergence-without-coordination finding (84 documents → 200 documents), with the meta-comparison across four independent landscape studies itself constituting a form of "convergent research finding without coordination between research teams" — an unusual meta-level parallelism instance.

### fsQCA / P1 — sample-size-dependency methodological caution

The explicit finding that principle-count scales with corpus size across four independent studies (5→8→17 principles as N grows from 84→36→200... noting Fjeld's 36-document/8-cluster sits between Jobin and Corrêa chronologically but not monotonically by N) is a direct methodological caution for [[P1_SLR_RG]] and [[fsQCA_Thesis_Chapter]]: any fixed taxonomy PRIS adopts from the literature should be treated as provisional and corpus-size-sensitive, not as an established ontology.
