## Summary

Martins et al. develop a DSR-methodology-based action-plan proposal for BCBS 239 compliance in banking. BCBS 239 (the Basel Committee's *Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting*, 2013) is the international standard whose G-SIB compliance failure has been repeatedly documented in the corpus — only 2 of 31 G-SIBs fully compliant after a decade of supervisory pressure (per International_8 BIS/FSI Insights No. 73). The paper's contribution is an implementation-layer action plan derived from banking + IS expertise, focusing on master data management and risk-data governance as the operational bridges between banks' compliance intentions and the substantive BCBS 239 requirements. Directly reinforces the master-data-governance dimension of the PRIS corpus.

---

## Key Findings and Framework

### DSR-Derived Action Plan for BCBS 239 Compliance

Martins et al. structure the action plan around four master-data-management pillars:
1. **Data governance architecture** — formal ownership, quality, and stewardship for risk-related master data
2. **Data lineage + traceability** — every risk-report data element must be traceable to source system + transformation logic
3. **Master data quality** — completeness, accuracy, timeliness (BCBS 239 Principles 3, 5, 6)
4. **Governance integration** — master data governance integrated into risk-management operating model, not siloed IT function

### BCBS 239 Compliance Gap Diagnosis

The paper affirms the empirical finding (echoed in International_8) that BCBS 239 compliance remains elusive because master-data-management is systemically under-invested. Banks treat BCBS 239 as a reporting exercise (compliance to output form) rather than a data-architecture exercise (compliance to substance). Martins et al.'s action plan reframes compliance around substantive master-data-governance re-architecting.

### Practical Contribution

The paper provides a *replicable action-plan template* — a rare implementation-layer artefact in the BCBS 239 literature, which is otherwise dominated by BIS/regulator supervisory reports.
