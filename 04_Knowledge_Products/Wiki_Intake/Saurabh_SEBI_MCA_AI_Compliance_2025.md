## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Documents a December 2024 SEBI consultation/board decision ("Assigning Responsibility for the Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools by Market Infrastructure Institutions, Registered Intermediaries and Other Persons Regulated by SEBI", PR No. 34/2024, 21 Dec 2024) — a recent regulatory development not yet captured in earlier corpus batches, directly relevant to AI accountability/responsibility-assignment debates (feeds S2).
2. Proposes a three-phase periodisation of AI adoption in Indian corporate compliance: (i) basic digitisation 1990s-2005 (SEBI's 1996 stock-exchange computerisation, MCA21 portal 2006), (ii) transitional data-analytics era 2006-2015 (Companies Act 2013 §447 fraud-detection mandates, SEBI Committee on Technology), (iii) acceleration 2016-present (2016 demonetisation, SEBI's 2017 Data Analytics Department, 2018 Committee on Fair Market Conduct, COVID-19-driven automation) — a citable historical-institutional timeline for [[Institutional_Coherence]]/path-dependency arguments (feeds S2/S1).
3. Core diagnostic finding: India's AI adoption in corporate compliance has been "mostly reactive, spurred by emergencies... rather than deliberate planning," producing "cumulative layers of technological development without matching changes to governance institutions" — a direct textual articulation of the [[Governance_Debt]] construct, citable verbatim (feeds S1/S2).
4. Explicitly contrasts India's absence of AI-specific corporate-compliance regulation against the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1084) and the US SEC's proposed rule on governance of predictive data analytics in broker-dealer activities (Release No. 34-99924, 2023) — a ready-made three-jurisdiction comparator table for fsQCA conditions (feeds S3).
5. Frames the core normative tension as a binary: AI in Indian corporate compliance "becomes a weapon for unrestricted government control or a tool for improved corporate governance," contingent on whether legislators "respond fast" — directly usable as a framing device for legitimacy/accountability discussions (feeds S1).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

- The three-phase adoption periodisation (digitisation → data analytics → acceleration) is a reusable slide template for explaining "how we got here" to BFSI/corporate compliance audiences before pitching AI-governance solutions.
- The "reactive not deliberate" diagnosis is a strong, quotable framing for LinkedIn thought-leadership on India's AI-governance gap.
