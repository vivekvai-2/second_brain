## Summary

Papagiannidis et al. examine how AI governance is applied to enable robust AI system deployment and mitigate AI risks, based on a comparative case analysis of three firms in the energy sector. The paper contributes to enterprise AI governance literature by identifying (a) dimensions relevant to AI governance in organisations, (b) practices that support decision-making, and (c) barriers with recommended mitigations. Frames AI governance as producing knowledge that assists decision-making while overcoming barriers — a distinctive practice-based approach to enterprise AI governance. This is the intellectual predecessor to the 2025 scoping review (F050); together they form a longitudinal pair for citing the Papagiannidis research trajectory.

## Key Findings

### AI Governance Dimensions (from 3-Firm Comparative Case Analysis)

| Dimension | Function | Best-Practice Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural** | Governance body architecture | Dedicated AI committee + cross-functional integration |
| **Procedural** | Model lifecycle processes | Standardised risk assessment; model documentation |
| **Relational** | Stakeholder engagement | Cross-departmental collaboration; external ethics input |

*Note: The 2023 paper introduces the structural/procedural/relational trichotomy that becomes the analytical spine of the 2025 scoping review (F050).*

### Barriers Identified

- Lack of clear governance responsibility allocation
- Skill gaps in cross-functional AI understanding
- Difficulty translating high-level AI ethics principles into operational practice
- Resource constraints and competing priorities
- Regulatory ambiguity

### Recommended Actions

- Establish dedicated AI governance function with clear ownership
- Invest in AI literacy across the organisation
- Develop operational bridges from principles to practice
- Integrate AI risk assessment into existing risk-management frameworks
