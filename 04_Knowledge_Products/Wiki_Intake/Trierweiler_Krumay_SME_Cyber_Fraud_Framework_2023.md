## Summary

Trierweiler & Krumay develop an integrated framework for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) to manage cybersecurity and fraud risks jointly during digital transformation. The framework recognises that SMEs face compressed governance capacity — they cannot deploy the enterprise-scale 3LoD architectures documented in F007–F010, F063 — and must therefore design fraud management programs that fold cybersecurity into a single integrated function rather than the separated first/second/third-line architecture large enterprises use. The paper extends the 3LoD-in-cybersecurity SLR (F063 Valkenburg & Bongiovanni) into the SME context, showing how the model's assumptions break down at smaller organisational scales.

---

## Key Findings and Framework

### SME Fraud + Cyber Risk Integration Framework

The paper proposes a joint management model recognising three defining constraints on SME governance:

| Constraint | SME Reality | 3LoD Assumption Breaks Down Because |
|---|---|---|
| **Staffing** | Single role covers risk + compliance + IT security | Cannot separate first from second line |
| **Governance authority** | Owner-manager typically holds all approval rights | No third-line audit independence possible |
| **Resource envelope** | Limited budget for dedicated tooling | Cannot afford enterprise SOC or dedicated fraud analytics |

### Integrated Program Design

Trierweiler & Krumay recommend four design principles for SME cyber+fraud management:
1. **Single ownership** of both cyber and fraud risk in one function
2. **Digital-first fraud detection** using low-cost SaaS tools rather than in-house infrastructure
3. **External audit substitution** — engage external auditors periodically rather than maintain internal audit function
4. **Regulatory-proportionality alignment** — SMEs should be able to demonstrate proportionate compliance, not full 3LoD replication

### Contribution

The paper is a rare formal IS-conference treatment of SME cybersecurity + fraud governance — most 3LoD scholarship (F007–F010, F063) implicitly assumes large-enterprise scale. Trierweiler & Krumay provide the SME-scale extension.
