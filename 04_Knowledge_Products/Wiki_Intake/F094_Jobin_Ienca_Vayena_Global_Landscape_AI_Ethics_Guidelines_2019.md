## Key Findings and Framework

### The Five Converging Principles (headline finding)

Systematic review of **84 documents containing AI ethics principles or guidelines** (88% released after 2016; literature monitored through 23 April 2019, PRISMA methodology, 3,457 codes → 1,180 ethics-relevant codes) found convergence around five principles, each referenced in more than half of all sources:

| Principle | Frequency (of 84) | % |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency | 73 | 87% |
| Justice, fairness & equity | 68 | 81% |
| Non-maleficence | 60 | 71% |
| Responsibility & accountability | 60 | 71% |
| Privacy | 47 | 56% |
| Beneficence | 41 | 49% |
| Freedom & autonomy | 34 | 40% |
| Trust | 28 | 33% |
| Sustainability | 14 | 17% |
| Dignity | 13 | 15% |
| Solidarity | 6 | 7% |

Headline finding (verbatim): *"Our results reveal a global convergence emerging around five ethical principles... with substantive divergence in relation to how these principles are interpreted; why they are deemed important; what issue, domain or actors they pertain to; and how they should be implemented."* — **convergence on labels, divergence on operationalisation.**

### Issuer and Geographic Distribution (fragmentation evidence)

- **Issuer types:** private companies 22.6% (n=19), governmental agencies 21.4% (n=18), academic/research institutions 10.7%, inter-governmental/supra-national organisations 9.5%, non-profits 8.3%, professional associations 8.3%, private-sector alliances 4.8%, plus smaller categories (research alliances, science foundations, worker-union federations, political parties).
- **Geographic distribution:** USA (n=20, 23.8%) and UK (n=14, 16.7%) together account for more than a third of all documents; EU institutions + member states = 19 documents total; single documents each from Canada, Iceland, Norway, UAE, **India**, Singapore, South Korea, Australia.
- **Target audience:** 32.1% multiple stakeholder groups, 28.6% self-directed (issuer applies principles to itself), 11.9% public sector, 15.5% unspecified.

This issuer/geography spread — 84 independent bodies across governments, companies, NGOs, and IGOs converging on similar labels without central coordination — is the **clearest single piece of evidence in the corpus for global-level normative fragmentation**, the international-scale analogue of the corpus's India-focused [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] findings (RBI/SEBI/CERT-In/MeitY parallelism, scaled up to a 20+-country landscape).

### Methodology (relevant to P1)

PRISMA-based systematic search (68 sources retrieved → 30 eligible → 84 final after snowballing/iterative supplementary search), followed by **code mapping** (qualitative metasynthesis) and **reflective equilibrium** (deliberative mutual adjustment between codes and categories) — a directly comparable systematic-review methodology to what P1 (AI Governance SLR) itself must execute, making this both a substantive and a methodological precedent for P1.

### Notable Definitions

- **AI:** "the theory and development of computer systems able to perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence."
- **Transparency** (per Turilli & Floridi, cited): "a proethical condition for enabling or impairing other ethical practices or principles."
- **Harm** (non-maleficence): primarily interpreted as discrimination, privacy violation, or bodily harm; less frequently as loss of trust/skills, "radical individualism," or regulation being outpaced by technology.

## Relevance to PRIS Research

### For [[P1_SLR_RG]] (primary)

Landmark predecessor SLR in the exact subject area (global AI ethics/governance principles landscape). P1 should explicitly position itself relative to this paper: Jobin et al. (2019) maps *principles* convergence/divergence globally as of April 2019; P1's own typology work extends this by (a) focusing on *regulatory instruments* rather than ethics guidelines specifically, (b) adding six years of subsequent regulatory hardening (binding rules superseding voluntary principles), and (c) applying Institutional Grammar 2.0 rule-level coding rather than principle-frequency counting. This is a citable "what has changed since 2019" framing opportunity.

### For [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] (primary — new "Instances in Corpus" entry)

The clearest global-scale (as opposed to India-scale) evidence of principle-convergence-without-coordination in the entire corpus — 84 independent issuers arriving at similar labels through parallel, uncoordinated processes.

### For [[Institutional_Coherence]] (primary — new "Instances in Corpus" entry)

Directly operationalises the convergence/divergence distinction central to Institutional_Coherence: label-level convergence (5 shared principles) without implementation-level coherence (divergent interpretation, weighting, and operationalisation) — a precise empirical instance of "coherence in form, incoherence in substance."

### For [[fsQCA_Thesis_Chapter]] (secondary)

The 11-principle taxonomy and issuer/geography coding scheme is a candidate reference point for calibrating the EA (Ethical Alignment) condition, since Jobin et al. is one of the primary empirical sources establishing which principles are globally recognised as core AI-ethics content.
