## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Four-pillar AI Governance Operating Model** (feeds S1/S6): HTI's operating model structures board oversight into strategy, governance structures, governance practices (including risk management), and organisational enablers — a directly reusable board-level AI governance checklist structure.

2. **AI oversight as an extension of existing director duties** (feeds S1): The guide frames board-level AI oversight not as a new compliance category but as an extension of existing director duties, spanning privacy, consumer protection, discrimination, work health and safety, copyright, and cyber security obligations already on boards' agendas.

3. **"Shadow AI" named as a distinct board blind spot** (feeds S1/S6): The guide explicitly flags employee use of AI tools without formal oversight ("shadow AI"), and AI embedded within third-party products, as governance blind spots requiring deliberate board attention rather than being subsumed under general IT governance.

4. **Case-study-anchored maturity benchmarking** (feeds S7): Case studies (Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Atlassian's Responsible Tech Review, Canteen) anchor the abstract governance principles in named-organisation practice — reusable illustrative material for board training or consulting decks.
