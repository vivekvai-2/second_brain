## Key Findings and Framework

### Indian IS research publication landscape — quantified, 2001–2023

Of 8,845 papers published across 186 MIS-discipline journals with at least one India-affiliated author (2001–2023), 6,416 are India-only-authored and 2,429 involve international collaboration. Only **2.4%** of India-only-authored papers reached A* journals, versus **9.8%** for internationally-collaborated papers — a roughly 4x A*-publication-rate gap favouring collaboration. **63.9%** of India-only papers land in C-category journals versus 43.2% for collaborated papers. In the AIS Senior Scholars' "Basket of Eleven," only 191 India-affiliated papers exist across the full 2001–2023 window, of which just 52 are India-solely-authored — and of those 52, only **11 papers** actually study India-specific data or problems. A directly citable, precisely quantified case for exactly the collaboration strategy [[01_Strategy/VV_Governance_Advisory/Consultancy-V2|Consultancy-V2]] and [[01_Strategy/VV_Governance_Advisory/VV_Integrated_Strategy_Master|VV_Integrated_Strategy_Master]] already pursue (India-based co-author identification, per P2's own Open Question OQ-02) — this paper supplies the empirical baseline for *why* that strategy matters at the level of the entire Indian IS research community, not just P2 specifically.

### Neo-institutional theory applied reflexively — coercive/mimetic/normative forces on Indian B-schools

A fourth independent corpus anchor to DiMaggio & Powell (1983) this session (after [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F122_Scott_Institutional_Theory_Research_Program_2004|F122]], [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F125_Alaimo_From_People_to_Objects_Digital_Transformation_Fields_2022|F125]], and [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F126_Butler_Gozman_Lyytinen_Conceptual_Ontology_Regulation_IT_2023|F126]]) — but applied here to a genuinely different unit of analysis: the institutional forces shaping *Indian business schools' research behaviour itself*, not a regulatory or technical domain. **Coercive forces**: NIRF rankings (post-2016), AICTE/UGC/MHRD mandates — associated with a documented 38% research-output increase (Kumar et al. 2019). **Mimetic forces**: "business school globalisation routes" (Vakkayil & Chatterjee 2016: drift/infiltration/replication/expansion) — IIMs and ISB explicitly modelled on the American business-school template. **Normative forces**: AIS India Chapter (INAIS, founded 2016, reaching 1,500+ scholars), ICIS hosted in India (2020, 2023), the American tenure-track system's diffusion into Indian institutional incentive design.

### "Theorising the local" — five attention-urgent domains for the Global South

A direct, citable prioritisation: **agriculture (and rural-centric livelihoods), healthcare, education, informal-sector, and governance** as the five domains most urgently requiring India-specific IS theorising, justified against India's approximately 70%/30% urban/rural split and 10%/90% formal/informal-sector split. **Governance is explicitly named as one of five priority domains** — a direct, citable warrant (from a JSIS guest editor's own published prioritisation) for P2's own DPI-governance research agenda, strengthening the "why IS scholars, why India, why now" case P2's Action A-14 needs to make.

### The "American buffalo" quote and the responsibility framing

A sharply quotable line capturing the data-availability bias problem in comparative/local research: "it is easier to write a paper on the American buffalo (because data is available) than Indian livestock." Paired with the paper's explicit definition of research **responsibility** as having two dimensions — "addressing problems situated in the country and developing contextual theories" — this is a directly citable methodological/motivational device for P2's Introduction, distinct from but complementary to Avgerou (2019)'s contextual-theorisation citation already in P2's scaffold.

### Collaboration schemes and the neo-colonial-replication caution

Names three formal India-international collaboration schemes (GIAN, VAJRA, SPARC) as concrete infrastructure for the "Collaboration" future-direction, alongside an explicit, citable self-caution: such collaborations "must also be cautiously approached lest the Indo-western research collaborations replicate neo-colonial relations." A nuance worth noting for any PRIS discussion of international co-authorship strategy (relevant to P2's own OQ-02 co-author question) — collaboration is recommended, but not uncritically.
