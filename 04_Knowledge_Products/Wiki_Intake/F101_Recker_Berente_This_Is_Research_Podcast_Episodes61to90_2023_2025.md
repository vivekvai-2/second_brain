## Key Findings and Framework

### The Jagged Technological Frontier (Episode 71, "Navigating the Jagged Frontier of Computing")

Dell'Acqua et al. (2023)'s metaphor: AI competence is not a smooth capability gradient but a "jagged frontier" — non-linear and contextually variable, with AI excelling unpredictably at some tasks while failing at superficially similar ones. Directly relevant to enterprise AI-governance capacity: an organisation's ability to *locate* the jagged frontier for its own use cases (rather than assume uniform AI capability) is itself a governance-capacity competency, distinct from but complementary to the executive-oversight-distinctiveness argument already captured in F098.

### GenAI-as-research-subject methodology discipline (Episodes 64–65, "Disclaimer: ChatGPT Produced This Episode" / "Jan Does Not Research ChatGPT but That Does Not Mean No One Should")

Mandatory guidance for papers studying ChatGPT/GenAI specifically: "(1) identify a specific mechanism GenAI activates or disrupts, (2) anchor it in prior theory, (3) show empirically what is new." A discipline against superficial "AI applied to X" papers — reinforces the violated-assumption contribution test (F097/F100) specifically for AI-topic submissions.

### Algorithm sensemaking — a worker-perspective governance lens (referenced across the AI-adjacent episodes)

Möhlmann et al. (2023): a sociotechnical perspective on how platform workers make sense of the algorithmic systems governing them. A bottom-up, worker/user-facing complement to the corpus's predominantly top-down (regulator/enterprise) AI-governance material — a theoretical anchor if PRIS ever examines AI governance from an affected-party rather than deployer/regulator perspective.

### AI Readiness Framework (referenced via Holmström, 2022)

An organisational-capacity deliverable emerging directly from academic research (via a named Digital Innovation Centre institutional model bridging firms, researchers, and policymakers) — a concrete example of the "engaged scholarship" thesis already captured in F099, now specifically instantiated for AI-governance capacity-building.

### Research-integrity taxonomy (referenced, elaborated further in F102)

Andrade (2021)'s questionable-research-practices taxonomy — HARKing, p-hacking, cherry-picking, data dredging, fishing expeditions — named here as a foundational reference; the theme is substantially deepened in the Episodes 91–117 batch ([[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F102_Recker_Berente_This_Is_Research_Podcast_Episodes91to117_2025_2026|F102]]), where it becomes directly relevant to LLM-assisted research practice.

### Four-test journal-fit framework and citation-category taxonomy (submission-strategy episodes)

A four-test framework (IS discourse test, IS hook test, digital artefact test, sociotechnical orientation test) for assessing manuscript-journal fit, and a three-way citation-category distinction (legitimate / obligatory / flunky citations) — both extend the journal-fit and citation-hygiene threads already running through F097/F099/F100.
