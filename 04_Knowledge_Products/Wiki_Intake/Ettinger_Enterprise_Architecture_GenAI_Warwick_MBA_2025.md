## Summary

Ettinger's Warwick MBA dissertation applies the *dynamic capabilities* lens (Teece 1997, 2007) to the enterprise-adoption challenge of generative AI, arguing that Enterprise Architecture (EA) practice can function as the organisational dynamic capability enabling scalable and sustainable GenAI deployment. The dissertation bridges innovation-adoption theory with governance-architecture practice, addressing the gap between (a) fast-moving GenAI capability curves and (b) slower-moving governance frameworks in large enterprises. Positions EA as the organisational bridge between the two — a practitioner-facing formulation useful for consulting engagements and enterprise-implementation cluster analysis, though not academically citeable at PRIS journal-track rigor.

---

## Key Findings

### Central Thesis

Enterprise Architecture (EA), historically framed as a technical-architecture discipline for IT alignment, can be repurposed as a *dynamic capability* (Teece) for GenAI adoption because it:
- Bridges innovation and governance across siloed enterprise functions
- Provides the organisational scaffolding for scalable model deployment
- Enables governance instrumentation without slowing innovation velocity

### Dynamic Capability Framework Applied

Ettinger structures the argument via Teece's three dynamic-capability components:
1. **Sensing** — EA identifies GenAI opportunities across the enterprise landscape
2. **Seizing** — EA structures the organisational response (governance + integration + risk controls)
3. **Reconfiguring** — EA continuously adapts as GenAI capabilities and regulation evolve

### Practitioner Recommendations

The dissertation is oriented toward practitioner consumption; concrete recommendations include EA-team leadership involvement in AI governance committees; explicit EA responsibility for AI model registers; and EA-driven cross-functional governance orchestration.
