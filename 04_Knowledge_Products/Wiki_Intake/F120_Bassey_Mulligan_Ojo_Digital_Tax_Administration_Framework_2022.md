## Key Findings and Framework

### Digital government project failure rates — citable caution against techno-solutionism

94% of digital tax IT projects in the United States run over budget, and 40% are never completed; in developing countries, failure rates "could be as high as 85% (35% total failures, 50% partial failures)." A worked negative-ROI example: Romania's e-tax project cost €189,500 against €95,700 in financial benefits — a **negative** three-year net present value of −€51,000. Directly citable evidence for the corpus's recurring rhetoric-vs-evidence-gap thread (cf. F108, F110, F117, F118) and for the paper's own explicit caution: "tax officials and policymakers need to resist the urge to see ICT as a 'silver bullet.'"

### Digital tax ecosystem framework — four categories, fifteen themes

**Context** (private-sector digitalisation impact, multilateral-institution influence, aid-agency influence, political environment, legal framework), **Stakeholders** (identification, adoption, equity), **Technology** (tax data, build-vs-buy decisions, technology use), **Demonstrated Results** (net benefits to taxpayers/officials/society, cost savings). A structurally similar four-category framework to the corpus's other macro-level e-government analytical structures (F108's Roberts 2020, F116's Public Service Ecosystem theory), applied specifically to tax administration.

### Digital-service inequities taxonomy (Fig. 9) — extends F116's inclusion thread

Five dimensions of inequity in digital tax service access: **Geographical** (rural vs. urban), **Economic** (poor vs. non-poor), **Cultural** (family background, ethnic/language), **Social** (young vs. old, disabled vs. non-disabled, literate vs. illiterate), **Approach** (technophobes vs. enthusiasts). A directly comparable, tax-domain-specific companion to F116's vulnerable-groups digital-inclusion framework — worth citing together if P2 needs a cross-domain (DPI-general + tax-specific) inequity evidence base.

### User-adoption theory inventory (Table 3)

Compact list of ten established technology-adoption theories applied in the digital-tax literature: TAM, Theory of Planned Behaviour, UTAUT, DeLone & McLean IS Success Model, Diffusion of Innovation, SERVQUAL, Perceived Characteristics of Innovating, Trust, Hofstede Cultural Values, Technology Readiness Index, Expectancy Confirmation Theory — a useful reference list if any future PRIS work needs to select an adoption-theory lens for citizen-facing DPI system analysis.
