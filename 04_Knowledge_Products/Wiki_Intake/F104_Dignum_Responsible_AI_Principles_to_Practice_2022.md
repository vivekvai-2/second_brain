## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Five-principle global convergence, reaffirmed via direct citation** (feeds S1): Dignum cites Jobin et al. (2019) — already in the vault as [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F094_Jobin_Ienca_Vayena_Global_Landscape_AI_Ethics_Guidelines_2019|F094]] — for the same five-principle global convergence (Transparency, Justice and Fairness, Non-Maleficence, Responsibility, Privacy) across "over 600 AI-related policy recommendations, guidelines or strategy reports," a figure updated from F094's original 84-document count as of this 2022 keynote — independent secondary corroboration that the convergence finding held (and the guideline count grew substantially) three years after F094's original publication.

2. **"Question Zero" reframing of responsibility** (feeds S1): Dignum explicitly rejects giving AI systems responsibility for their own actions, reframing Responsible AI as requiring "more responsibility and more accountability from the people and organisations involved" — both for the AI application's decisions/actions and for the organisation's own decision to deploy AI in a given context. A clean, citable one-line accountability-attribution principle distinct from the corpus's usual regulatory-instrument framing of the same idea.

3. **Design for Values — a concrete three-step design methodology** (feeds S1/S6): (i) identification of societal values, (ii) selection of a moral-deliberation approach (via algorithms, user control, or regulation), (iii) linking values to formal system requirements and concrete functionalities — paired with the named "Glass Box framework" (Aler Tubella & Dignum, 2019) as a verification method and source of high-level transparency. Distinct from the corpus's more common principle-listing frameworks in offering an actual design *process*, not just a target-state taxonomy.

4. **Data-power argument** (feeds S1): "Those that have the power to decide on data, have the power to determine how AI system will be design[ed], deployed and used" — reframes AI governance as fundamentally a question of power over data and datification, not solely algorithmic design or model behaviour; situates AI within a "socio-technical ecosystem" (Dignum, 2019) rather than treating it as a discrete technical artefact.

5. **Interdisciplinary-team mandate as a governance mechanism** (feeds S1): explicit argument that AI development requires diversifying the discipline background of contributors to include philosophy, social science, law, and economics expertise — framed as a substantive governance requirement (diversity "in all the meanings," including gender, cultural background, ethnicity), not merely a workforce-composition preference.

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

**Highest-value reuse:** the "Question Zero" framing (responsibility sits with the people/organisations deploying AI, not the AI system) is a clean one-line explainer for Board/CRO audiences on accountability allocation; the Design for Values three-step methodology is a directly usable practitioner framework for structuring an actual AI governance design engagement, rather than a principles checklist.
**Consulting connections:** AEGIS_OS — Design for Values / Glass Box framework's emphasis on pre-deployment value-to-requirement linkage and verification maps onto AEGIS_OS's pre-execution runtime governance design work.
**Teaching connection:** the algorithm-as-recipe / "baking an apple pie" explanation is a usable plain-language AI-literacy example for non-technical audiences.
