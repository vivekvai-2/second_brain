## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Three-way comparative regulatory-posture taxonomy (EU/US/India)** (feeds S3/fsQCA RL): The paper's own comparative summary (p. 643) characterises the EU as having a "strong focus on transparency" via comprehensive, proactive AI Act regulation; the US as "market-driven, with some sector-specific oversight" (decentralised); and India as having an "emphasis on consumer protection and transparency, with developing oversight mechanisms." This is a secondary-source (non-primary) articulation of a three-configuration comparative posture already evidenced primary-source in the corpus (EU: [[06_Frameworks/EU_AI_Act]], [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_22_EU_AI_Act_Conformity_Assessment_Notified_Bodies]]; India: developing/advisory posture across RBI/SEBI/MeitY) — useful as an independent, non-corpus-derived confirmation of the same fsQCA RL-condition ordering.

2. **"Consent Managers" as a DPDPA-specific AI-governance mechanism** (feeds S5): Identifies "Consent Managers" as a concept the DPDPA introduces specifically "to ensure AI systems manage consumer data responsibly" (p. 639) — a named institutional/technical mechanism not previously surfaced by name in the corpus's existing DPDPA coverage ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/MeitY/MeitY_4_DPDP_Rules_2025]]). Candidate for a targeted follow-up check on whether MeitY_4 already covers Consent Managers in the Rules text itself.

3. **Pre/post RBI FREE-AI "call-and-response" evidence** (feeds Regulatory_Parallelism/Governance_Capacity): Writing in 2024 — roughly a year before RBI FREE-AI (November 2025) — the paper's Research Problem section calls for "regulatory bodies to establish oversight mechanisms that ensure the protection of consumer rights and maintain market integrity" (p. 638) as an "urgent need." This is a dated, externally-authored (non-RBI) articulation of the exact gap RBI FREE-AI was subsequently issued to address — a useful independent evidentiary anchor for arguing RBI FREE-AI was responsive to an already-visible practitioner/academic gap rather than issued in a vacuum.

4. **Regulatory sandbox expansion recommendation, pre-dating SEBI's 2025 sandbox** (feeds S1/P3): Suggestion 5 recommends "expanding the use of sandboxes" to "identify potential risks early on" (p. 644) — written before [[07_Institutions/Corpus/SEBI/SEBI_13_Regulatory_Sandbox_Framework_2021]] (SEBI's 2025 Regulatory Sandbox framework), offering the same call-and-response evidentiary pattern as Finding 3, this time on the SEBI side.

5. **Data-quality/citation-integrity teaching example** (feeds S1, methods): The comparative table's US regulatory-approach cell is a verbatim copy of the EU cell ("Comprehensive, proactive regulation with a strong emphasis on data protection and AI ethics (EU AI Act)"), directly contradicting the paper's own preceding paragraph describing the US as "decentralized and sector-specific." Combined with the paper's reliance on press/corporate-blog sources (WEF, NVIDIA, New Indian Express, The Recursive) for its empirical claims about AI's financial-system impact, this is a concrete, citable example of secondary-literature quality variance — useful as a practitioner/teaching illustration of why comparative tables in secondary sources should be checked against primary regulatory text before reuse (directly relevant to this vault's own "never replicate primary-source text, verify before citing" discipline).
