## 3–5 Reusable Findings

> ⚠ Each finding below is characterisation, not quotation. Page anchors required before use.

1. **The three-model taxonomy** (feeds S3): Bradford partitions global digital regulation into an **American market-driven** model (innovation-first, minimal ex-ante intervention), a **Chinese state-driven** model (state direction of technology development, surveillance and social oversight), and a **European rights-driven** model (comprehensive ex-ante frameworks protecting privacy, consumers and speech). This taxonomy is the default organising frame in comparative AI-regulation scholarship and is the frame P1 must either adopt or explicitly displace.

2. **Regulatory competition as the unit of analysis** (feeds S3 / P4): the book's structure — introduce the empires, then their horizontal conflicts (US–China, US–EU), then their vertical expansion into third countries — treats regulatory models as **exportable and competing**, extending the diffusion mechanism of Bradford's earlier *Brussels Effect* (2020) argument. India is not one of the three empires; on this frame it is a jurisdiction over which the empires compete.

3. **The frame's blind spot, and why it matters here** (feeds P1 positioning): a taxonomy built on three exporting jurisdictions has no native category for a **large jurisdiction that is a rule-taker in some domains and a rule-maker in others** — which is India's actual position (DPI as an *export*, AI governance as an *import*). This is the same asymmetry recorded independently at International_38 as the **deployment-versus-governance** finding: WDR 2026 cites India ~25 times as an AI-deployment exemplar and never as an AI regulatory-governance case. Two independent sources, one structural observation.

4. **Contested by WDR 2026 Box 9.4** (feeds S3 / P1): the World Bank frames **California and China as parallel leaders in both innovation and binding regulation**, explicitly titled *"Beyond narratives of regulatory competition,"* and concludes that developing countries should extract common principles rather than copy a model. This is a **flagship multilateral institution declining Bradford's frame** — a positioning asset available to P1 that did not exist before 2026.

5. **Relationship to [[Regulatory_Parallelism]]** (feeds P3 / P4): Bradford theorises divergence **between** jurisdictions competing to export models. This vault's construct theorises divergence **within** a jurisdiction between co-equal regulators with no export ambition. The constructs are not rivals and the second is not reducible to the first — which is a contribution-framing point worth stating explicitly rather than leaving implicit. See also the 2026-08-15 **substantive-vs-floor divergence recode** in Regulatory_Parallelism: Bradford's models diverge substantively at the floor level by design, whereas Indian intra-jurisdictional floor divergence is unintended.
