## Key Findings and Framework

### "Multiple QCAs, Different Time Periods" — the distinctive methodological contribution

Unlike F087's single-panel sub-period consistency check, Gong & Yang run **five fully separate fsQCAs**, one per year (2018–2022), on the same 31-case sample, then compare which solution configurations recur across years. Recurring ("equivalent") configurations across adjacent periods are interpreted as evidence of **institutional stability and maturity** (their Proposition 5) — a genuinely different temporal-robustness technique from F087's "consistency of pathways through time," and a second, distinct candidate for [[fsQCA_Thesis_Chapter]] if a longitudinal design is ever considered. The paper also explicitly recommends "dynamic QCA methods" for future longitudinal work.

### Institutional Logics Taxonomy

- **State logic:** social goals — improving society/community well-being, redistribution, citizenship.
- **Market logic:** economic goals — share price/profit growth, transaction efficiency, self-interest.
- **Corporation logic:** the corporation as a legal, commerce-associated institution.
- **Core conditions:** essential/prerequisite conditions for successful system function.
- **Peripheral conditions:** conditions that enhance performance when present but whose absence doesn't preclude success (same core/periphery distinction as [[02_Projects/Shared_Anchors/Fiss_2011|Fiss (2011)]], applied here to a different empirical domain).

**Named exogenous shock:** the 2020 COVID-19 public health crisis is used as a natural experiment — it "strongly activated the public service market," temporarily shifting institutional-logic dominance within the panel. A methodologically clean example of using an exogenous shock as a robustness/validity check on a configurational model.

### Light Conceptual Connection to P4 (not evidentiary)

Gong & Yang's state/market/corporation logics taxonomy is structurally similar in kind (though substantively unrelated) to [[P4_Doctrinal_IJLIT]]'s existing framing of RBI's "lending prudence" logic vs. SEBI's "market conduct" logic as divergent institutional logics driving regulatory non-coordination (see P4_Doctrinal_IJLIT.md, Institutional Theory row). Noted as a possible citable methodological parallel for how to formally operationalise "divergent institutional logics" as an fsQCA condition, should P4 or the fsQCA chapter ever need to — **not added as P4 evidence**, since the substantive domains are unrelated and P4 already has DiMaggio & Powell (1983) as its institutional-theory anchor.
