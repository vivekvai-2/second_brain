## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. Quantifies the AI-in-finance literature's growth trajectory: 607 WoS-indexed papers (1993-2024), with only 139 published in 1993-2017 (~5.56/year) versus an average of 121.6/year in 2018-2023 — a citable "field maturation" timeline directly useful for framing the novelty/timeliness of P1's SLR (feeds S1/S7).
2. Confirms via TCCM analysis that ethical AI, governance, and regulatory oversight remain *emerging* (not yet mature/saturated) themes within the AI-in-finance literature — corroborates the SLR's premise that a regulatory-governance-focused review fills a live gap (feeds S1).
3. Identifies persistent gaps in interdisciplinary methods and methodological rigor as a structural weakness of the field — supports positioning fsQCA/Institutional Grammar approaches as methodologically novel contributions (feeds S7).
4. Core thematic clusters identified: algorithmic trading, fraud detection, credit scoring, risk management, customer service/NLP chatbots, and (as an emerging cluster) ethical/regulatory AI — useful as a ready-made thematic taxonomy for classifying corpus nodes by application domain (feeds S1).
5. Explicitly calls for future research on "equity in algorithmic decision-making and transparency in the AI model" (citing Kurshan et al., 2021 — cf. F013) — a direct literature-anchored research-gap citation linking this bibliometric review to the corpus's model-governance cluster (feeds S1/S6).
