## Summary

Turk offers a doctrinal analysis of *rule overlap* in US financial regulation, framed against post-Dodd-Frank reform debates. The paper argues that overlap between agencies (SEC/CFTC/OCC/Fed/CFPB) is often mischaracterised as redundancy or inefficiency — Turk develops an administrative-law framework that distinguishes constructive overlap (peer-review-generating) from destructive overlap (compliance-cost-generating without incremental risk mitigation). This is the corpus's clearest doctrinal precursor to the [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] construct developed for the Indian BFSI context.

---

## Key Findings

### Turk's Two Types of Rule Overlap

| Type | Definition | Governance Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Constructive overlap** | Multiple regulators produce comparable-but-not-identical rules on same activity; the divergence forces cross-regulator learning + iterative refinement | Generates regulatory improvement over time |
| **Destructive overlap** | Multiple regulators impose *identical or contradictory* rules on same activity without mutual reference | Generates compliance cost without corresponding risk reduction — "administrative burden without benefit" |

### Doctrinal Framework

Turk grounds the constructive/destructive distinction in administrative-law scholarship (Freeman & Rossi 2012; Marisam 2011). Key doctrinal claims:
1. Overlap without coordination mechanism → destructive
2. Overlap with cross-referencing + reciprocal recognition → constructive
3. Overlap without preemption authority + without coordination → worst case (post-DF characterisation)

### Empirical Application

Turk applies the framework to specific overlapping-rule pairs in post-Dodd-Frank US financial regulation, showing which fit constructive vs destructive categorisation. The framework generalises: any polycentric regulatory system can be evaluated using it.
