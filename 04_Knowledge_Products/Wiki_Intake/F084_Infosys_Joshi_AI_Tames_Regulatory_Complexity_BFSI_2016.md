## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Three-source regulatory-complexity taxonomy** (feeds Regulatory_Complexity_Handbook): Data Quality, Regulatory Overload, and Product Data Silos — a simple, citable three-category taxonomy for regulatory complexity that predates (by roughly a decade) the corpus's much more granular P3/fsQCA-era complexity analysis (Regulatory_Parallelism, Governance_Debt). Useful as a plain-language framing device for executive audiences before introducing the corpus's more precise signature constructs.

2. **Pre-AI-governance-era compliance-cost baseline** (feeds S1, historical comparator): US$270bn annual compliance spend (~10% of big-bank operational spending); ~15% of workforce occupied by compliance; 20,000 new banking regulatory requirements added in 2015 alone; a forecast of 300 million+ pages of regulation "by 2020." This is a ~2016 baseline predating the corpus's 2023–2026 figures (e.g., [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/EY_IIF_Global_Bank_Risk_Survey_2026]]), useful for a before/after regulatory-burden-growth comparison.

3. **JPMorgan Chase commercial-loan-review automation case** (feeds S6, teaching): Manual review "used to occupy staff for 360,000 hours every year" before machine-learning-based review reduced manual effort and error rates. The extraction does not name the specific platform, but this is very likely an early public reference to what JPMorgan later branded **COIN (Contract Intelligence)** — worth confirming the platform name independently before citing by name (⚠ not asserted in-node as confirmed).

4. **Early (pre-2020) articulation of human-oversight-retention concerns** (feeds Accountability/Governance_Capacity): "Banks have to be careful not to allow machine learning and other AI systems to develop biases" and "should also make sure that the compliance team doesn't lose its grip on things once machines start taking decisions" — a vendor-side, practitioner-facing articulation of bias risk and human-oversight retention roughly 6–8 years before the corpus's formal AI-governance literature (EU AI Act, RBI FREE-AI) codifies the same concerns. Also notes financial institutions "would have to convince regulators that their intelligent machines are doing at least as much due diligence... as the human beings before them" — an early statement of the AI-explainability-to-regulator burden.

5. **Compliance-workforce-shrinkage forecast, testable against later corpus data** (feeds Governance_Capacity, forecast-accuracy check): Predicts "the average compliance workforce will shrink by half in the next three to five years" (i.e., by c. 2019–2022), with 84% of a 1,600-leader survey planning AI-benefit training and 80% planning to retrain/redeploy (not simply displace) affected staff. This forecast is directly checkable against the corpus's later empirical workforce data ([[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/NASSCOM_RAI_India_2023]], EY IIF 2026) — a candidate forecast-accuracy case study for a teaching or thought-leadership piece on AI-adoption-prediction reliability in BFSI.
