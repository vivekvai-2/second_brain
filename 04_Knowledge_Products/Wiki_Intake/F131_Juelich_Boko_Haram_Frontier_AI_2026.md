## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Institutionalised frontier-AI misuse, not ad hoc use** (feeds S5): Semi-structured interviews with 27 former Boko Haram members (fieldwork 2025–2026, northeast Nigeria) found both factions use frontier consumer AI models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Meta AI, DeepSeek) in an institutionalised way — through specialised units and internal training, not individual improvisation.

2. **Documented safeguard circumvention** (feeds S5): Respondents reported successfully circumventing some frontier-model safety guardrails to obtain assistance with attack planning, weapons troubleshooting, and explosive-device design.

3. **Transnational tradecraft diffusion** (feeds S5): AI-use know-how spread through transnational jihadist networks, with Islamic State operatives delivering in-person training on AI tool use to Boko Haram members — direct evidence of cross-group diffusion of frontier-AI-misuse tradecraft.

4. **Enthusiasm outpaces documented severity** (feeds S5): Respondents expressed strong enthusiasm and, in some cases, openness toward mass-casualty (WMD) applications, but documented use to date remained conventional — a distinction relevant for calibrating proportionate AI-safety policy response versus alarmist framing.
