## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **TrustScore Readiness five-level maturity ladder** (feeds S6): The brief defines a five-stage AI governance maturity model — Unverified → Documented → Auditable → Certified → Trusted — culminating in a "demonstrable, portable compliance" state described as an "AI trade passport." This is a governance-capacity calibration instrument comparable to ISACA's CMMI-based model (F038) and WEF/Capgemini's four-stage framework (F037), but framed around third-party certification portability rather than internal process maturity.

2. **Three converging principles vs. three structural divergences across global AI regulation** (feeds S3): The report identifies risk management, security-by-design, and transparency as converging organising principles across surveyed frameworks, set against three persistent divergences — an "assurance & enforcement" (proof-and-penalty) gap, inconsistent clarity/precision in innovation-vs-control balancing, and fragmented incident-reporting thresholds, formats, and timelines. This divergence triad is direct practitioner-level evidence of cross-jurisdictional Regulatory_Parallelism and Governance_Debt.

3. **"Portable AI governance file" artifact checklist** (feeds S6): A concrete minimum-documentation package — risk register, model evaluations, incident log, AI-BOM (AI Bill of Materials), and provenance documentation — proposed as the practical unit organisations should build to move up the TrustScore ladder and operate across multiple regulatory regimes simultaneously.

4. **ISO/IEC 42001 portability limit** (feeds S3): Direct quote — "For global operators, 42001 offers portability, but must be bolted onto local filings and data residency duties" — states explicitly that a global voluntary certification standard does not substitute for local mandatory compliance obligations, reinforcing that certification-based governance strategies cannot fully resolve jurisdictional fragmentation.

5. **Global regulatory timeline periodisation (1975–2040+)** (feeds S3): The brief periodises the trajectory of AI/cybersecurity governance into "Implementation Wave" (2025–2030), "Sectoral Consolidation" (2030–2040), and "Global Harmonisation" (2040+) — a forward-looking convergence forecast that can be cross-referenced against the corpus's other timeline/maturity instruments (F048 FLI Safety Index; International_13 BIS/IMF framework) for fsQCA temporal calibration of the Regulatory_Parallelism → Institutional_Coherence trajectory.
