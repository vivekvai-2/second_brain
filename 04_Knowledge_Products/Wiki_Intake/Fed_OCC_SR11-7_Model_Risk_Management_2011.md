>
> **SR 11-7 was superseded and replaced on 17 April 2026 by SR 26-2, *Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management*** (Federal Reserve, jointly with OCC and FDIC; companions OCC Bulletin 2026-13 and FDIC FIL-15-2026). https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm
>
> SR 26-2 also supersedes SR 21-8 and rescinds OCC Bulletins 2011-12, 2021-19 and 1997-24, the Model Risk Management booklet of the Comptroller's Handbook, and FDIC FIL-22-2017.
>
> **Do not cite SR 11-7 as current guidance.** Retain this node for historical and citation purposes — SR 11-7 remains the foundational text and the reference point most of the literature was written against. Successor changes: applicability narrowed to banks above $30bn; model definition tightened to three cumulative criteria; new third-party/vendor-model section; **generative and agentic AI explicitly carved out of scope**, with non-generative/non-agentic ML retained.
>

## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Tripartite scope of model risk management** (feeds S6): SR 11-7 defines effective model risk management as encompassing three interlocking components — (i) robust model development, implementation, and use, (ii) an effective independent validation framework, and (iii) sound governance, policies, and controls — establishing the foundational US precedent that AI/ML model governance regimes (including RBI's 2024 draft circular on model risk) trace back to.

2. **Validation as independent and ongoing, not one-time** (feeds S6): The guidance frames model validation as a continuous, independent function rather than a pre-deployment gate — directly relevant to AI governance debates on whether "validation" obligations under RBI_3 (Draft Circular — Model Risk in Credit) should be structured as continuous monitoring vs. point-in-time sign-off.

3. **Governance as encompassing incentive and organisational structure** (feeds S6): SR 11-7 explicitly includes "an appropriate incentive and organizational structure" within model-risk governance — broadening the governance frame beyond technical controls to organisational design, a useful anchor for [[Institutional_Coherence]] arguments about AI governance requiring structural (not just procedural) reform.

4. **2011 vintage as the "year zero" of model risk management doctrine** (feeds S6): As the earliest document in this batch's 3LoD/model-risk cluster (2011), SR 11-7 provides the doctrinal baseline against which the IIA (2024), AFM (2021), FCA (2018) and Schuett (2023) developments can be sequenced for a regulatory-evolution timeline analogous to the existing RBI_4→SEBI_12→FEAT→RBI_3→FREE-AI sequence.
