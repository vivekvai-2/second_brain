## Key Findings and Framework

### Contribution framing — the "violated assumption" test (Episode 9)

Direct methodological guidance: an interesting, citable contribution is one that explicitly frames the paper around a violated assumption — "if a reader with domain knowledge would predict your findings before reading them, your contribution is probably not interesting." This operationalises the same craft failure diagnosed in the GIQ rejection and is directly reusable as a pre-submission contribution self-test in the Drafting Discipline Protocol.

### Narrative sequencing — "show why the answer matters before showing the answer" (Episode 1)

Editorial gatekeeping advice: motivate the phenomenon's importance before presenting findings, rather than leading with results. This is the same failure mode named in the GIQ Editor-in-Chief's rejection language ("difficult to follow... insufficient connection to an overall narrative").

### Theory as activity, not label — the "theory fetish" critique (Episode 1)

Distinguishes "having a theory" (a citational label) from "doing theoretical work" (a cognitive activity), and cautions against reflexively reaching for a named theoretical lens (Structuration Theory, Institutional Theory, Actor-Network Theory, etc.) if it does not fit the phenomenon. A useful discipline check against decorative rather than earned theory application — relevant given PRIS's own use of DiMaggio & Powell (1983) institutional theory across P3/P4/fsQCA.

### Configurational and computational methods named as an "emerging" cluster (Episode 5, "Method-ism")

fsQCA is named explicitly as an "emerging methodological innovation for configurational phenomena," alongside process mining (van der Aalst) and computationally-intensive theory development (Berente, Seidel & Safadi, 2019) using ML/NLP to generate theory from large datasets. This corroborates but does not add new technical detail beyond [[08_Methods/fsQCA]]'s existing Ragin/Fiss/Dul/Andrews anchor set — no edit made to that file on this basis, per the Bidirectional Linking Convention's instruction not to edit an already-accurate bullet.

### Authorship governance — CRediT framework and gift/ghost authorship warnings (Episode 8)

Concrete process recommendation for multi-author papers: establish contribution agreements early, use the CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy) framework, and document division of labour before submission — with an explicit warning against "gift authorship" and "ghost authorship," and a note on doctoral students' rights against supervisors who attach their names without substantive contribution. Directly actionable for any future PRIS co-authored submissions.

### Career and publication strategy — the 10-year citation horizon and community fit (Episodes 4, 9, 10)

Recurring advice cluster: lead with the research question, then select the method — not the reverse; work within methodologically-aligned sub-communities rather than trying to satisfy every IS subcommunity in one paper; treat citation-based "impact" as a lagging indicator and write for a 10-year horizon rather than the next review cycle; manage (rather than resolve) imposter syndrome through regular writing and wide, undisciplined reading outside one's specialisation. Consistent with, and reinforcing, [[01_Strategy/VV_Publication_Pipeline_v3]] AP-02 ("optimise for earliest acceptance probability, not journal prestige ranking").
