## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **"Fantasia" hybrid-attack scenario + four real precedents** (feeds S5, teaching): The authors construct a hypothetical coordinated hybrid attack (social-media smear campaign against a finance minister, a social-media-fuelled bank run, an electricity blackout, a national internet slowdown) explicitly modelled on real precedents they name: Estonia (2007, cyberattack), Bulgaria (2014, social-media-triggered bank run), the Kiev power-grid attack (December 2015), and blackouts across Argentina/Uruguay/Paraguay. The direct quote — "players in the corporate and political worlds would find it difficult to see the whole picture" — is a strong articulation of the cross-domain-attribution problem in hybrid-threat response, complementary to but predating the corpus's AI-specific threat-actor taxonomies ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_2]]'s offensive-use taxonomy, [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F073_Google_Cloud_Mandiant_Cybersecurity_Forecast_2026]]).

2. **Five reasons traditional operational risk management fails for cyber/systemic risk** (feeds S1, Governance_Capacity): ECB's four reasons (persistent motivated-attacker campaigns; interconnection uncorrelated with business relevance; business-continuity-defeating attacks; stealth/rapid propagation) plus the authors' fifth — "cyber attacks can be systemic if they exploit shared vulnerabilities" — is a pre-AI, general-cybersecurity articulation of exactly the risk-framework-inadequacy argument the corpus's AI-systemic-risk literature makes independently ([[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Danielsson_Macrae_Uthemann_AI_Systemic_Risk_2022]]). Useful as a 2020, non-AI-specific precedent showing the "traditional ORM doesn't capture this risk class" argument predates and generalises beyond AI.

3. **"Asymmetry of governance" — the corpus's sharpest single-sentence Institutional_Coherence articulation for the EU** (feeds Institutional_Coherence): "In the EU, security questions are dealt with by and large by national authorities, while the single market is a true EU endeavour. This asymmetry of governance is becoming problematic as the global security environment becomes less benign." This is a direct, quotable statement of a structural coherence gap — national security competence vs. EU-level market competence — that sits alongside but is analytically distinct from the corpus's existing EU coherence evidence (lex specialis coordination in [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/International_12_HCJP_Legal_AI_BFSI_2025]]; conformity-assessment infrastructure in [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_22_EU_AI_Act_Conformity_Assessment_Notified_Bodies]]).

4. **EU three-part cyber-resilience architecture + CROE** (feeds S3): Names the EU's approach as (i) regulations/standards, (ii) testing/preparedness, (iii) governance — with the Cyber-resilience Oversight Expectations (CROE) for the Eurosystem (2018) as the ECB's governance/testing/response instrument, and the Network and Information Security Directive (2016) as the mandatory cross-sectoral (energy/transport/water/health/finance) supervisory baseline. A primary-adjacent EU financial-cybersecurity regulatory architecture predating and structurally comparable to the EU AI Act's own three/four-layer design ([[06_Frameworks/EU_AI_Act]], [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_22_EU_AI_Act_Conformity_Assessment_Notified_Bodies]]) — worth a future side-by-side "EU sectoral cyber-resilience architecture vs. EU AI Act architecture" comparison.

5. **2018–2020 baseline cyber-cost/frequency statistics** (feeds S5, historical baseline): Hiscox — 61% of surveyed firms reported a cyber event in 2018 (up from 45% in 2017), average loss $369,000 (+61% YoY), ~75% failed a cyber-readiness test (5,400 firms across US/UK/Belgium/France/Germany/Spain/Netherlands); Verizon DBIR 2019 — 25% of breaches espionage-motivated; BITKOM — German companies incurred ~€43bn in data-espionage/sabotage damage (2016–17), 7/10 manufacturing firms attacked; UK DCMS 2019 — 32% of businesses identified an attack in the prior 12 months (down from 43%). This is a pre-AI-boom (2018–2019) cyber-cost baseline the corpus otherwise lacks, useful for before/after comparison against the corpus's more recent AI-era figures (IBM Cost of Data Breach 2025 in [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F042_IBM_Cost_Data_Breach_Report_2025]]; Google Cloud/Mandiant 2026 forecast in F073).

## Consulting / Teaching Reuse

**Highest-value reuse:** The "Fantasia" scenario is a ready-made board-level/executive tabletop-exercise narrative for hybrid-threat awareness training — directly usable for a board cyber-risk briefing without adaptation.
**Consulting connections:** Indirect — general EU financial-system cyber-resilience framing relevant to any cross-border BFSI advisory conversation involving EU-domiciled entities.
**Teaching connection:** The five-reasons-ORM-fails argument (Finding 2) is a strong classroom bridge between traditional operational-risk-management teaching and AI/systemic-risk material, since it makes the systemic-risk case without invoking AI at all.
