### Mandate Scope
Per the article, the committee was tasked with:
1. Developing **responsible AI principles** for the state
2. Establishing an **AI risk classification system**
3. Identifying and defining **prohibited AI practices**, including:
   - Social scoring
   - Disproportionate surveillance
   - Discriminatory profiling
   - Automated decision-making in high-stakes domains without human oversight
4. Addressing **generative AI implications** specifically
5. Providing guidance on **vendor due diligence** for AI procurement
6. Recommending **implementation roadmap** for the policy

## Analytical Significance

### Regulatory Parallelism — Third sub-national governance track (2026)
Karnataka_Govt_1 documents the emergence of a **third Indian state-level AI governance initiative** (after TN 2020 and Telangana 2020), six years later and with a substantively different governance philosophy:
- TN 2020: state-bureaucracy-led, ethics-mandatory, scorecard-based
- Telangana 2020: industry-co-designed (NASSCOM), growth-first, ethics-advisory
- Karnataka 2026: industry-figure-led (Kris Gopalakrishnan), risk-classification-first, prohibition-oriented

The 2026 timing is notable: it post-dates the EU AI Act (adopted 2024) and its prohibited practices list mirrors EU AI Act Article 5 categories (social scoring, surveillance, profiling, automated high-stakes decisions). This suggests a **regulatory diffusion dynamic** — Indian state-level governance adapting EU risk-classification architecture rather than originating indigenously. (**⚠VERIFY** whether the mandate text explicitly references EU AI Act.)

### Governance_Capacity — Industry leadership model as institutional innovation
Appointing an industry co-founder (Kris Gopalakrishnan) as committee chair — rather than a bureaucrat or academic — represents a distinct **governance capacity model** for state AI policy. This positions the committee as industry-expertise-led rather than bureaucracy-led (TN) or industry-partnership-led (Telangana/NASSCOM). The 60/90-day timeline is unusually fast for an Indian policy committee. (**⚠VERIFY** whether the timeline was met.)
