### Definitions and Taxonomy

- **Institutional autonomy:** the university's level of substantial and procedural freedom in deciding matters of interest (Berdahl 1990; Verhoest et al. 2004).
- **Severe evaluation:** the increase in the evaluative state — research/teaching evaluation, quality assurance, performance/target funding, contracts — through which institutional autonomy is "redesigned, addressed, and practically bordered."
- **Continental governance mode:** hierarchical, state-centered coordination; low institutional autonomy; powerful academic-guild authority; faculties as "confederations of chair-holders" (Clark 1983) — a useful comparator concept for centralised-vs-devolved regulatory design questions generally.
- **Index of Institutional Autonomy** and **Index of Evaluation**: both 0–1 composite indices built from three equally-weighted subdimensions (financial/organizational/teaching autonomy; presence and funding-impact of evaluation tools respectively).

### Headline Finding (counter-intuitive; the paper's central contribution)

Institutional autonomy is **neither necessary nor sufficient** for good teaching performance, and can even be a necessary condition for the *absence* of good performance. Generous public funding, verticalized internal governance, and severe evaluation are more strongly associated with good performance than autonomy alone. Direct quote: *"giving more institutional autonomy to universities is not the panacea that many scholars and policymakers believe it to be."* This is a structurally interesting analogue for AI-governance debates about deregulation/institutional-autonomy-as-default-good-governance assumptions, though PRIS makes no substantive claim transferring this finding to the AI domain — noted here only as a possible framing analogy for P1's introduction, not as evidence.
