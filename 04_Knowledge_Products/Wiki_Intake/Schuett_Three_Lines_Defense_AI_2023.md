## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Direct application of 3LoD to AI companies** (feeds S4): Schuett maps the classical three-lines-of-defence model onto AI developer organisations: the first line (teams building/deploying AI systems) manages risk directly; the second line (e.g., dedicated risk/safety functions) provides expertise, monitoring, and challenge; the third line (internal audit) provides independent assurance on whether AI risk objectives are met — the most direct academic precedent for mapping 3LoD onto [[Agentic_AI_Governance]] structures.

2. **Articulated 3LoD criticisms transferable to AI governance** (feeds S6): The paper catalogues four standing criticisms of 3LoD — (i) second-line functions often lack organisational independence from profit-seeking units, (ii) this proximity encourages risk-taking, (iii) second-line functions often lack the skills/expertise to challenge first-line practices, and (iv) third-line effectiveness depends on individual auditor competence — each directly applicable as a critique checklist for evaluating proposed AI safety/risk functions inside AI labs.

3. **"Information asymmetry" critique of second-line oversight** (feeds S6): Citing Zhivitskaya (2015), Schuett notes the second line "may not recognise that they only see what the first line chooses to show them" — a transferable critique of internal AI-safety-team oversight where the safety function depends on disclosures from the development team it is meant to monitor.

4. **Proposed AI-company org chart with equivalent three-line roles** (feeds S4): The paper proposes a sample organisational chart for an AI company assigning shareholders/board, first-line (product) teams, second-line (safety/risk) functions, third-line (internal audit), and external assurance roles — a ready-made diagram template for practitioner decks on AI governance org design.

5. **Empirical gap: no studies test 3LoD effectiveness for AI** (feeds S4): Schuett notes that while studies link first/second-line practice adherence to financial performance (Andersen et al. 2022) and study internal audit effects generally (Lenz & Hahn 2015; Eulerich & Eulerich 2020; Jiang et al. 2020), none examine the 3LoD model specifically — identifying an explicit empirical gap that P3/P4 BFSI AI governance research could address.
