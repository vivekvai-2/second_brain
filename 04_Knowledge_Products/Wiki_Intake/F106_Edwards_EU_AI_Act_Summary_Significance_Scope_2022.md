## Critical Framing Note — Proposal-Stage, Not Final Text

**This document describes the EU AI Act as proposed by the European Commission in April 2021 and as it stood during 2022 Parliament/Council negotiation, not the final enacted Regulation (EU) 2024/1689** already represented in the vault via [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_22_EU_AI_Act_Conformity_Assessment_Notified_Bodies]] and the [[06_Frameworks/EU_AI_Act]] framework note. Substantial changes occurred during the 2022–2024 trilogue negotiations — most significantly the addition of dedicated general-purpose-AI (GPAI) provisions after ChatGPT's November 2022 public release (this document, dated 2022, appears to predate or be contemporaneous with that shift and treats GPAI as an emerging, secondary category rather than the central negotiating battleground it became). **Do not cite this document's specific figures (fine percentages, Annex III category list, Article numbers) as current law without checking against the final Regulation** — its enduring value is as an evolution-baseline / drafting-history source, not a current-law reference.

## 3–5 Reusable Findings

1. **Original four-tier risk framework, as proposed** (feeds S3): unacceptable risk (prohibited) / high risk (conformity assessment) / limited risk (transparency) / minimal risk (voluntary code of conduct) — structurally the same four-tier architecture that survived into the final AI Act, making this a useful evolution-baseline citation for tracking what changed vs. what was stable across the legislative process.

2. **GDPR-modelled extraterritorial scope, articulated at proposal stage** (feeds S3): "governing not just users of AI in the EU, but providers who place on the market or into service AI systems in the EU" — an explicit design choice modelled on GDPR's own extraterritorial reach, articulated here before the Act's final passage; useful for P4 doctrinal comparison of the AI Act's Brussels-effect design against India's DPDP Act (which has its own, differently-scoped extraterritorial provisions).

3. **Original fine ceiling: flat 6% global annual turnover** (feeds S3/P4, ⚠ superseded): the 2021 proposal specified a single flat maximum fine of 6% of global annual turnover; the final Regulation replaced this with a tiered structure (higher for prohibited-practice violations, lower for other high-risk violations) — a citable, dated data point for a P4 drafting-history analysis of how the Act's enforcement architecture evolved, but not itself a current-law figure.

4. **Original eight-category Annex III high-risk list, as proposed** (feeds S3): critical infrastructure; biometric ID systems; educational/vocational training; employment/workers management; essential private and public services; law enforcement; migration/asylum/border control; administration of justice and democratic processes — a proposal-stage baseline against which the final Annex III's category changes can be tracked.

5. **GPAI as an emerging, not-yet-central category** (feeds S3, historically significant): GPAI is here defined narrowly via a "new recital 70a" as systems performing "generally applicable functions such as image/speech recognition, audio/video generation, pattern detection, question answering, translation" — explicitly distinguished from AGI. This 2022 framing, which treats GPAI as one classificatory question among several rather than the negotiation's central axis, is itself evidence of how significantly the Act's centre of gravity shifted after generative AI's mainstream emergence — useful for a P1 argument about how AI governance instruments can be overtaken by the technology they regulate mid-drafting.
