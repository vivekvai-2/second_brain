## Key Findings and Framework

### Public Service Ecosystem (PSE) theory — third independent macro/meso/micro instance in the corpus

Macro level (societal values/institutional norms shaping public service provision; main stakeholder: governments) → meso level (public service organisations/systems; main stakeholder: service organisations/systems) → micro level (individual user/employee actions; main stakeholders: users and employees). A third independently-sourced macro/meso/micro analytical structure in the corpus, alongside Roberts' (2020) framework via [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/F108_Tai_Open_Government_Research_Decade_SLR_2021|F108]] — reinforcing macro/meso/micro as a robust, recurring structure worth formally adopting for P2's own multi-level DPI governance analysis.

### Goal-action framework — directly actionable for DPI equity analysis

**Macro-governance:** policy inclusion (vulnerable groups' needs as guiding design principle). **Meso-service:** infrastructure inclusion (network/social infrastructure coverage), channel inclusion (freedom to choose digital or non-digital access channels), design inclusion (system design aligned with vulnerable groups' behavioural habits/abilities), data inclusion (vulnerable groups visible in data collection, not discriminated against in analysis). **Micro-support:** individual development via digital competence, digital confidence, and digital accountability. A directly citable, three-tier action framework for assessing whether India's DPI systems (Aadhaar, UPI, DBT) actively design for vulnerable-group inclusion or merely assume uniform digital capability.

### "Warm experts" — directly resonant with observed Indian DPI intermediary patterns

"Warm experts" are "informal helpers, such as family members or friends, who provide guidance out of their own experience" — a named construct for the informal digital-access-intermediary phenomenon widely observed in India's actual DPI usage (family members, Common Service Centre operators, or local shopkeepers assisting with Aadhaar/UPI transactions on behalf of less digitally-capable citizens). Directly citable vocabulary for a P2 discussion of informal intermediation as an (unacknowledged, unregulated) inclusion mechanism operating alongside India's formal DPI architecture.

### "Automated systems" — a clean ADM definition, cross-links to P4

"Automated systems" are defined as "algorithmic decision tools designed to assess particular cases or to make an administrative decision in lieu of a civil servant" — situated within a meso-level discussion of "street-level intermediaries" (public, non-profit, and private entities), implicitly extending Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy theory to algorithmic decision-making. Directly cross-links to [[04_Knowledge_Products/Wiki_Intake/Sejwal_Gupta_ADM_Indian_Credit_Scoring_2026]]'s existing ADM/automated-decision-making treatment in the corpus — this paper supplies the general public-administration-theory framing (algorithmic street-level bureaucracy) that Sejwal & Gupta's credit-scoring-specific analysis instantiates in a BFSI context.

### Research-attention imbalance (parallels the cluster's recurring pattern)

89% of micro-level studies focus on vulnerable groups' usage behaviour; only 11% focus on channel selection — a research-attention skew toward measuring *whether* vulnerable citizens use digital services rather than *what alternatives* they have when they don't — the same "vision without voice"-style imbalance the P2 cluster documents elsewhere (measuring outcomes/behaviour rather than structural/channel-level determinants).
