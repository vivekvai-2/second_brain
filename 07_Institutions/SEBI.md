# SEBI

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-06-12

---

## Core Synthesis

The Securities and Exchange Board of India is the second anchor regulatory actor in the PRIS research portfolio — the issuer of the AI governance instruments that form the capital-markets pole of the Regulatory Parallelism construct in P3, and the institutional logic foil to RBI in P4's fragmentation diagnosis. SEBI's AI governance trajectory is characterised by earlier entry (2019 vs. RBI's 2025), narrower risk architecture, but sharper accountability assignment: Regulation 16C's sole-accountability principle is more explicit on liability attribution than anything in FREE-AI, making SEBI's instruments analytically significant despite their lesser architectural depth. The zero cross-references between RBI and SEBI across 40 coordination pairs — despite three SEBI→CERT-In references confirming cross-referencing is institutionally possible — is the empirical anchor for the Regulatory Parallelism construct.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Statutory market regulator
- **Established:** 1988 (statutory authority 1992)
- **Governing statute:** SEBI Act 1992; Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act 1956; Depositories Act 1996
- **Primary mandate:** Investor protection, market development, securities market regulation
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Brokers, dealers, investment advisors, stock exchanges, depositories, clearing corporations, portfolio managers
- **Enforcement mechanism:** Compliance inspections, trading suspensions, administrative penalties, adjudication orders

## Institutional Logic (P3/P4 Analytical Relevance)

SEBI's governing logic is **market conduct and investor protection** — its primary concern is transparency, fair markets, and the prevention of market manipulation. This produces an AI governance orientation focused on algorithmic trading accountability, disclosure of AI use to investors, and liability for AI-driven market conduct. This logic is structurally different from RBI's prudential risk-management orientation — and the divergence generates the Regulatory Parallelism finding: both regulators govern the same dual-regulated entities (major Indian banks operating as SEBI intermediaries) without cross-referencing each other's AI obligations. The vendor-accountability gap in Regulation 16C — intermediaries are solely liable for AI vendor outputs but vendors have no parallel duties — is an unresolved structural gap pending a 2026 SEBI consultation paper.
