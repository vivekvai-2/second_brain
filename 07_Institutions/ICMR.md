# ICMR

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-07-14

---

## Core Synthesis

The Indian Council of Medical Research is the healthcare-sector complement to the BFSI regulators (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI), the telecom silo (TRAI), and the executive-branch AI stack (MeitY/NCAIC) in the PRIS Regulatory Parallelism diagnosis. ICMR's *Ethical Guidelines for Application of Artificial Intelligence in Biomedical Research and Healthcare* (ICMR_1, 2023) provides the corpus's most detailed stakeholder-obligation mapping in the domestic Indian AI-governance space — seven stakeholder categories with differentiated obligations across development, deployment, and use — but with **zero enforcement mechanism attached to any category**. ICMR thus exemplifies the *architecture-without-enforcement* governance-capacity pattern: form exists, function is absent. In the fsQCA calibration, this places ICMR at Maturity Level 2–3 (Managed/Defined: obligations documented, no enforcement) — a governance-capacity gap distinct from CERT-In's operative jurisdictional expansion and RBI/SEBI's binding sectoral instruments.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Autonomous national biomedical research council under the Department of Health Research, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW)
- **Established:** 1911 (as Indian Research Fund Association); reconstituted as ICMR in 1949
- **Governing statute:** None specific (societal registration; MoHFW administrative oversight); ethical guidance-issuing authority derived from national biomedical research coordination mandate
- **Primary mandate:** Biomedical research coordination, funding, and ethical oversight; policy guidance for clinical research and public health research
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Biomedical researchers, healthcare AI developers (research context), ethics committees (Institutional Ethics Committees / IECs), data providers, healthcare institutions engaged in AI research
- **Enforcement mechanism (AI-relevant):** **None binding** — ICMR guidelines are advisory. Indirect enforcement pathways: (a) research funding conditionality (grants may require adherence); (b) journal publication requirements (some Indian and international biomedical journals require ICMR-consistent ethical approvals); (c) IEC review at institutional level. No statutory sanction, no regulatory fine, no licence-linked consequence.

## Institutional Logic (P3/P4 Analytical Relevance)

ICMR's governing logic is **research ethics and clinical safety in biomedical AI** — an orientation focused on informed consent, data protection for research subjects, minimisation of algorithmic bias in health datasets, and safety of AI-assisted clinical decision-support. This produces an AI governance posture emphasising ethics-committee review, developer data-minimisation obligations, funder conflict-of-interest disclosures, and researcher-participant relationships. Structurally distinct from BFSI regulatory logic: ICMR is not a prudential or market regulator; its instruments do not attach to registration/licence status of the regulated entity in the way RBI/SEBI/IRDAI instruments do. This produces a **healthcare sector incoherence** finding: ICMR_1 has null cross-references to DPDP Act 2023 (which governs personal health data as a distinct category), MeitY AI advisories, and any RBI/SEBI/IRDAI provision. ICMR_1 also has a null cross-reference to MoHFW_1 (SAHI Healthcare AI Strategy, March 2026) — despite ICMR being formally under MoHFW's Department of Health Research — an intra-MoHFW coherence gap the corpus documents at the strongest possible institutional-proximity level.
