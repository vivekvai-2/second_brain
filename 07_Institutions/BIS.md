# BIS — Bureau of Indian Standards

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Created:** 2026-08-02 (ISO/IEC 42001 sourcing pass)

---

## Institutional Identity

India's national standards body. Within the AI governance space BIS operates through **LITD 30 — Artificial Intelligence Sectional Committee**, under the Electronics and Information Technology Division Council (LITDC).

- **LITD 30 scope (expanded 2026-08-02):** "Standardization in the area of Artificial Intelligence and Big Data" — covering common concepts and terminology, meaningful insight from large datasets, data-quality characteristics, **governance issues**, **trustworthiness (security, privacy, ethical and societal considerations)**, sustainability/environmental aspects, and **conformity (management systems, testing, validation, benchmarking)**. The conformity limb is directly relevant to the accreditation gap below
- **Parent council:** Electronics and Information Technology Division Council (LITDC), which oversees 35 sectional committees, 1,637 sub-committees/panels/working groups, and 1,600+ published standards across LITD as a whole
- **Statutory basis:** Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 2016
- **International liaison:** **P (Participating) Member** of ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 — India has a vote on the committee that writes the world's AI standards
- **Activity:** confirmed active — 46th Sectional Committee Meeting scheduled **5 August 2026**
- **Hosted** the ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 15th Plenary, New Delhi, April 2025

## Why This Institution Matters to PRIS

BIS was entirely absent from this vault before 2026-08-02 — zero mentions of "Bureau of Indian Standards" across 480+ notes — while ISO/IEC 42001 was referenced in 32. The corpus was tracking the standard and not the body that adopts it in India. That is a systematic blind spot worth naming: PRIS has strong coverage of **regulators** and near-zero coverage of **standards bodies**, and standardisation is where a great deal of AI governance is actually being settled.

**BIS is where India's AI governance is least visible and arguably most consequential.** LITD 30 holds a P-membership vote in SC 42, which has published 35 AI standards with 47 more in development. India is participating in global AI standard-setting through a channel the corpus had not been observing at all.

Two **potential proposals** appear in a BIS presentation (May 2024) — "Reliability of AI systems" and "Guidance for Generative AI Applications" — both described as potential, neither a published standard nor a draft in consultation.

**Pattern:** BIS adopts and trains; it does not yet author. For [[Governance_Capacity]] this is capacity *deployed on transposition* rather than on domestic standard-setting, and it parallels the NCAIC finding (an India framework whose risk taxonomy substantially mirrors EU AI Act Art. 5).

## The Accreditation Gap

BIS adopts the standard. **NABCB** — India's National Accreditation Board for Certification Bodies — is the body that would accredit certification bodies to issue IS/ISO/IEC 42001 certificates. **No NABCB accreditation programme, scheme document, or auditor-competence guidance for ISO/IEC 42001 was located.** Confirmed gap, pending direct verification against NABCB's own site.

The result is a three-layer institutional build with the middle layer missing: standard adopted ✅, lead auditors trained ✅, accreditation scheme for their employers ❌, regulatory recognition ❌. See [[ISO_IEC_42001]] for the full argument.
