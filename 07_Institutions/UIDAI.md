# UIDAI

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-06-12

---

## Core Synthesis

The Unique Identification Authority of India is the statutory body responsible for the Aadhaar biometric identity system — the foundational infrastructure layer of India's DPI stack and the primary empirical case in P2's rule-allocation analysis. Within PRIS, UIDAI is significant not as a regulator of AI but as the governance actor whose rule architecture P2 diagnoses: UIDAI's governance instruments for Aadhaar's AI-driven face authentication and identity verification are rich in system-facing obligations (technical standards, accuracy benchmarks, liveness detection requirements) and structurally thin in citizen-facing fallback obligations. This asymmetry — technical rules dense, citizen-accountability rules sparse — is what P2 constructs as the technical–citizen rule allocation asymmetry construct.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Statutory authority under MeitY
- **Established:** 2009 (statutory authority under Aadhaar Act 2016)
- **Governing statute:** Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act 2016; Aadhaar and Other Laws (Amendment) Act 2019
- **Primary mandate:** Enrolment and maintenance of the biometric identity database; authentication services; regulation of Authentication User Agencies (AUAs) and e-KYC User Agencies (KUAs)
- **AI-relevant function:** Governance of Aadhaar Face Authentication API (AI-driven liveness detection, face-match scoring); governance of AI-enabled identity verification across welfare delivery systems
- **Key governance documents:** Aadhaar Authentication Regulations; AUA/KUA agreements; Circular on Face Authentication (2022 onwards)

## Institutional Logic (P2 Research Relevance)

UIDAI's governing logic is **scale, speed, and fraud prevention** — its design priorities are the ability to authenticate at population scale with minimal latency and maximum accuracy. This produces a governance architecture that specifies AI system performance parameters (liveness detection thresholds, match score cut-offs, API response times) in detail, while leaving citizen-facing failure procedures (what happens when authentication fails for a legitimate beneficiary, who is accountable, what redress exists) at the level of ministerial discretion rather than enforceable rules. In P2's IG 2.0 coding, UIDAI instruments yield a distinctive ADICO profile: high on technical Conditions (performance thresholds), low on citizen-facing Or-else (fallback consequences when the system fails the citizen). This is the empirical basis for the distributive rule density construct in P2.
