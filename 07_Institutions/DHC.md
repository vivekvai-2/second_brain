# DHC — High Court of Delhi

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Core Synthesis

The High Court of Delhi enters the PRIS corpus as the **first Indian court to adjudicate an AI governance question**, as distinct from issuing guidance about AI. Where [[07_Institutions/SCI]] contributes a white paper on how the judiciary should itself use AI, the Delhi High Court in *ANI Media Pvt. Ltd. v. OpenAI OpCo LLC* determines how existing Indian statute applies to the training and operation of a large language model. This is a categorical difference in institutional function: guidance versus binding adjudication of a live dispute between a rights-holder and an AI developer.

Its analytical significance for PRIS is threefold. First, it makes the judiciary an **instrument-issuing institution of a different kind** — one that resolves questions the executive and sectoral regulators have left open, on its own timetable and without reference to their consultations. Second, it supplies the corpus's first judicial reasoning on **evidentiary reconstruction of AI system behaviour**, decided without any retained system artefacts. Third, as the principal commercial IP forum in India, its intellectual property jurisprudence carries persuasive weight nationally and is the natural venue for future AI-related IP litigation.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Constitutional court of record; High Court for the National Capital Territory of Delhi
- **Established:** 1966 (Delhi High Court Act, 1966)
- **Jurisdiction:** Original civil jurisdiction (commercial suits above the pecuniary threshold), appellate, writ jurisdiction under Article 226
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Commercial IP disputes involving AI systems; jurisdiction over foreign AI developers established under s.62(2) Copyright Act 1957 and s.20 CPC notwithstanding offshore server location
- **Specialist infrastructure:** Intellectual Property Division (IPD), constituted 2021 — India's principal IP forum, with dedicated IPD Rules
- **Enforcement mechanism:** Injunctive relief, damages, costs; orders binding on parties, judgments persuasive across High Courts and before the Supreme Court

## Institutional Logic (P4 Analytical Relevance)

The Court's governing logic is **adjudicative and gap-filling** rather than supervisory. Para 4 of the judgment states the posture directly: "the Courts are required to step in to fill the gap between advancing technologies and existing laws." This is structurally distinct from every executive-regulatory institution in the corpus. RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and CERT-In act prospectively, addressing classes of regulated entity through instruments of their own design; the Court acts retrospectively on a concrete dispute, is bound by the statute as enacted, and cannot decline to decide.

Two consequences follow for P4. First, the Court reasons from **statutory text and legislative history** — it traces the 1957, 1994 and 2012 amendments to s.52(1)(a) and reads meaning from where the legislature did and did not exclude commercial use. That is a different mode of governance production from the principles-based drafting seen across the regulatory corpus, and it produces a more textually anchored and more contestable output. Second, the Court's answer arrived **while the executive was still consulting** on the same question — see [[07_Institutions/Corpus/DPIIT/DPIIT_1]] on the proposed text-and-data-mining exception. Judicial resolution and executive consultation ran in parallel on one question, neither deferring to the other.

## Consulting & Outreach Relevance

- Practitioner demand is immediate: every Indian enterprise training or deploying an LLM on scraped content now has a first-instance authority to work from, and a set of operational implications (opt-out signals, paywalls, crawler blocking, licence negotiation posture).
- The judgment's opt-out reasoning is directly actionable — the Court weighed against ANI that it had never exercised available blocking, making crawler-control configuration a governance control with litigation consequences.
- Engagement pathway: IP and technology bars, in-house counsel forums, publisher and news-industry associations, AI developer compliance functions.
