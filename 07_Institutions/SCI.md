# SCI

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-07-14

---

## Core Synthesis

The Supreme Court of India, acting through its Centre for Research and Planning (CRP), enters the PRIS corpus as the **first judicial-branch AI governance issuer** — a fourth branch beyond the executive-regulatory (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/TRAI/ICMR/MoHFW), the ministerial (MeitY/DPIIT), and the advisory-planning (NITI Aayog) tracks previously documented. The Supreme Court's *White Paper on Artificial Intelligence and Judiciary* (SCI_1, November 2025) establishes the judiciary's own governance posture for AI in judicial functioning — the corpus's clearest instance of a co-equal branch of government issuing AI governance guidance rather than being governed by executive/regulatory AI instruments. Analytically, SCI_1 provides three distinct PRIS contributions: (1) a role-differentiated accountability model (institutions / lawyers / law clerks) that comparators with CERT_In_1's maker-checker chain and NCAIC_1's RACI-based governance model; (2) the corpus's strongest evidentiary basis for human-in-the-loop mandates via three documented Indian judicial AI hallucination incidents; and (3) a 13-jurisdiction comparative survey that anchors India within an international judicial-AI governance landscape.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Apex judicial body of the Republic of India
- **Established:** 1950 (Constitution of India, Article 124)
- **Governing statute:** Constitution of India (Articles 124–147); Supreme Court Rules 2013; Advocates Act 1961 (bar governance dimension)
- **Primary mandate:** Constitutional interpretation, adjudication of civil and criminal appeals, writ jurisdiction under Articles 32/136/142
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Judicial functioning of the Supreme Court itself; guidance for High Courts, subordinate judiciary, and legal practitioners on AI use in litigation, judicial research, and case management. Persuasive authority for tribunals and quasi-judicial bodies.
- **Enforcement mechanism (AI-relevant):** Judicial discipline (recall of orders, cost sanctions, contempt proceedings for AI-hallucinated citations); professional discipline referral to bar councils; internal e-Committee guidance for court staff and Registrars

## Institutional Logic (P3/P4 Analytical Relevance)

The Supreme Court's governing logic is **procedural fairness, judicial integrity, and access to justice** — orientations translating into an AI governance posture emphasising human oversight (SCI_1 mandates human-in-the-loop for AI-assisted judicial work), disclosure (citation of AI tool/version/prompt as user-facing transparency for judicial AI use), and role-differentiated accountability (courts, lawyers, and law clerks each carry distinct disciplinary consequences for AI misuse). Structurally distinct from executive-regulatory logic: SCI is not concerned with prudential risk (RBI), market conduct (SEBI), or sector supervision (IRDAI/TRAI) — its AI governance is grounded in the internal integrity of adjudication and the professional responsibilities of legal practitioners. This makes SCI_1 the corpus's clearest exemplar of **branch-specific AI governance logic** and a key P4 doctrinal comparator: judicial-branch AI governance issued by the judiciary itself, contrasted with executive-branch AI governance issued for regulated entities.
