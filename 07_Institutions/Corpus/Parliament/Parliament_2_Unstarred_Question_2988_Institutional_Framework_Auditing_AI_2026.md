## Substantive Content

### Question asked
Four sub-parts, addressed to the MeitY Minister: (a) whether an institutional framework exists to audit AI algorithms used in governance and citizen-facing digital services; (b) what guidelines/protocols/standards exist to assess accuracy, bias, fairness, transparency, reliability and accountability of government-used AI; (c) whether certification/validation/testing/compliance mechanisms are being developed for AI procured or deployed in the public sector; and (d) whether Government is collaborating with academia, technology experts, standards bodies and ethics committees on algorithmic transparency and responsible AI.

### Answer — key institutional facts (verbatim/near-verbatim)

**IndiaAI Governance Guidelines — date and structure.** "The IndiaAI Governance Guidelines were released on 5th November 2025, providing a comprehensive national framework for the safe, responsible and inclusive development and deployment of Artificial Intelligence in the country, anchored around seven core principles ('Sutras'): Trust, People First, Innovation over Restraint, Fairness & Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design, and Safety, Resilience & Sustainability." This is the first vault source to state the release date on the record from the Minister directly; it should now be treated as the canonical date for [[07_Institutions/Corpus/MeitY/MeitY_2]] (confirm MeitY_2's own date field against this — see ⚠ VERIFY below).

**Safe & Trusted AI Pillar — named projects and institutions.** Thirteen projects addressing bias mitigation, machine unlearning, privacy-preserving AI, algorithm auditing, explainability and deepfake detection, "in partnership with leading academic and research institutions." Four are named explicitly:

| Project | Institution |
|---|---|
| Responsible AI for Bias Mitigation in Health Care Systems | NIT Raipur |
| Robust Privacy Preserving ML Models | IIT Delhi, with IIIT Delhi and IIT Dharwad |
| Enabling Explainable & Privacy Preserving AI for Security | Defence Institute of Advanced Technology (DIAT), Pune |
| Machine Unlearning in Generative Foundation Models | IIT Jodhpur |

**Certification and accreditation architecture — the node's most reusable finding.** "National Accreditation Board for Certification Bodies (NABCB) provides accreditation to the Certification Bodies for Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) as per ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015 and ISO/IEC 42006:2025 for certifying the AI Developers / AI Producers / AI Users as per ISO/IEC 42001:2023." NABCB "has developed accreditation framework for Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) certification as per ISO/IEC 42001:2023." This establishes that India has stood up a functioning **accreditation-of-certifiers layer** for ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS certification — NABCB accredits the bodies that then certify organisations, mirroring the existing ISO 27001/QCI architecture rather than creating something novel.

**Benchmarking.** "A Benchmarking Committee has been constituted to recommend suitable benchmarks and evaluation protocols for India's foundational AI models," and its evaluation protocols "apply to foundation models supported under the IndiaAI Mission" — scoped to IndiaAI-Mission-funded models, not a general public-sector AI procurement gate.

**International collaboration.** India as founding Chair of the Global Partnership on Artificial Intelligence (GPAI); adoption of UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (194 member states, November 2021); at the India-AI Impact Summit 2026, the New Delhi Frontier AI Impact Commitments signed by 13 global/Indian frontier model developers, and the Trusted AI Commons announced with 23 partner countries.

**What the answer does NOT confirm.** No standalone, cross-sectoral statutory audit mandate for government-used AI algorithms is stated to exist — the answer routes every sub-question back to the IndiaAI Governance Guidelines' voluntary/principles-based architecture, the Safe & Trusted AI Pillar's project-level work, and the NABCB/ISO 42001 voluntary certification track. Part (a) of the question — a dedicated institutional audit framework for citizen-facing government AI — is not answered with a named audit body or statutory audit requirement; the response substitutes the general Sutras framework and named research projects. This non-answer is itself analytically significant (see below).

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For P1 (AI Governance SLR) / [[04_Knowledge_Products/AI_Governance_Wiki]]
This is the vault's most authoritative single source for India's AI-institutional-architecture facts (Guidelines date, Sutras list, NABCB accreditation chain, named Safe & Trusted AI projects) and should be the citation of record for those facts going forward, ahead of secondary summaries.

### For P4 (Doctrinal, IJLIT) — the accountability-gap finding
The Government's own answer to a direct parliamentary question about a "framework for auditing AI algorithms deployed in governance processes and citizen-facing digital services" does not identify a dedicated audit mechanism, statutory or otherwise, for that specific concern. It substitutes principles (the Sutras), R&D projects (Safe & Trusted AI Pillar), and a voluntary certification-accreditation regime (NABCB/ISO 42001) that governs *any* AI system, public or private, rather than one targeted at government use of AI on citizens. This is a citable, on-the-record instance of a **governance-capacity gap between the accountability question asked and the institutional apparatus answered with** — directly usable for P4's doctrinal argument about the distance between India's AI-governance rhetoric and its enforceable audit infrastructure for state use of AI.

### For [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Standard/Governance_Capacity]]
A textbook instance of principles-and-projects substituting for a named audit institution when Parliament asks a pointed institutional-design question — useful as a diagnostic example of the gap between *stated* governance capacity (seven Sutras, Benchmarking Committee, GPAI chairmanship) and *demonstrated* institutional capacity (no statutory algorithmic-audit body identified for citizen-facing state AI).

### Coordination / Parallelism pattern
[[07_Institutions/Corpus/MeitY/MeitY_2]] states the Guidelines' principles; this node supplies the operational layer beneath them (NABCB accreditation chain, named project institutions) that MeitY_2 does not cover. Read together they show **institutional coherence at the principles-to-certification level** (Sutras → Accountability/Understandable-by-Design principles → NABCB/ISO 42001 certification track) but a gap at the **audit-of-government-AI level**, which neither document closes.

- **⚠ #304 (HIGH — direct contradiction with an existing vault finding).** [[06_Frameworks/ISO_IEC_42001]] (created 2026-08-02, three days before this answer) states as a **confirmed gap**, sourced independently: "❌ No NABCB published accreditation programme, scheme document, or auditor-competence guidance located," and separately that ISO/IEC 42006 (the scheme-specific auditor-competence standard) "is **not published**" per UKAS. This Lok Sabha answer (5 August 2026) states the opposite as government fact: NABCB "has developed accreditation framework for Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) certification as per ISO/IEC 42001:2023," accrediting bodies "as per ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015 and ISO/IEC 42006:2025" — implying 42006 is now published. **This is an unresolved contradiction between two primary-ish sources three days apart, not a resolved fact.** Possible explanations: (a) the Minister's answer describes a framework "developed" but not yet publicly published/operational, which would not contradict "no published programme located"; (b) NABCB moved between 2 and 5 August 2026; (c) the ministerial answer overstates operational status for political effect, a known risk class for parliamentary answers. **Do not resolve this in either direction without checking NABCB's own website/gazette for a dated, named AIMS accreditation scheme document.** Addendum added to [[06_Frameworks/ISO_IEC_42001]] flagging the same tension from that side — see its Log.
- **⚠ #304b (LOW-MEDIUM).** Independent of the contradiction above: even if NABCB's accreditation framework is confirmed operational, that is an accreditation-of-certifiers layer, not a regulatory mandate that ISO/IEC 42001 certification constitutes compliance with any specific Indian AI-governance obligation. Do not overstate this as "India recognises ISO 42001 as compliance evidence" in publication-track material.
