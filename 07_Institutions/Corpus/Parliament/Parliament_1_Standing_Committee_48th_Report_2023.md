## Substantive Content

### Critical scope limitation (verify before citing)
This report examined the **draft Personal Data Protection Bill/2022 framework**, not the DPDP Act, 2023 as finally enacted and gazetted. A confirmed source states the DPDP Bill, 2023 was **not referred to this Committee for examination** before passage — it was adopted directly by Parliament without committee review of the final text. **No parliamentary committee report exists specifically reviewing the enacted DPDP Act, 2023.** Any use of this node must preserve that distinction; it cannot be cited as "the parliamentary review of the DPDP Act."

### Value for PRIS despite the limitation
Notwithstanding the scope gap, the report is the only available window into legislative reasoning during the run-up to the 2023 Act — useful for institutional-design analysis of *why* certain provisions (Board structure, consent architecture, cross-border transfer approach) took their eventual shape, and for documenting the unusual path-to-enactment where the final Bill bypassed committee scrutiny entirely. That bypass is itself a governance-process data point: a foundational data-protection statute was enacted without a dedicated committee review stage — relevant to any argument about legislative-capacity or process-legitimacy in Indian technology-regulation lawmaking.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For P2 (DPI Governance) / P4 (Doctrinal)
Documents a process-legitimacy gap in the DPDP Act's legislative history: the final enacted text received no committee-stage scrutiny, in contrast to standard legislative practice. This is a citable process point for any argument in P2/P4 about the institutional-design quality or legitimacy-by-process of India's central data-protection statute, independent of the Act's substantive content.

### For Legitimacy (concept)
A useful empirical instance for the [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Standard/Legitimacy]] concept note (currently thin — see 2026-08-02 thin-node audit): process-based legitimacy (was the instrument properly scrutinised before enactment?) as distinct from outcome-based legitimacy (is the resulting law good policy?). The DPDP case shows these can diverge — bypassing committee review does not necessarily produce a substantively deficient statute, but it is a documented legitimacy-of-process gap worth coding.
