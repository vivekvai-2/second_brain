## S3. Mandate

As reported, the WG-AI is to:

1. Guide regulated entities on **adoption, governance and oversight** of AI technologies.
2. Provide direction on **governance frameworks, best practices and safeguards** so that innovation is "fair, transparent and resilient," with policyholder data protected at all times.
3. Assess **adoption, usage, maturity and deployment** of AI, and the **existing level of AI governance** in the sector.
4. Carry out a **structural assessment of the inventory of AI systems** used by entities.
5. Review **global regulatory and supervisory approaches** to AI with an insurance focus.
6. Suggest an **AI governance framework for ethical, transparent and explainable adoption** of AI tools and models, **including for claims and fraud prevention**.
7. Assess the **impact of frontier AI tools**.
8. Examine the need for **sector-wide stress testing**.
9. Suggest an **AI audit framework addressing pre-deployment and post-deployment audit requirements**.
10. Identify **skill gaps and capacity-building needs** for stakeholders.
11. Examine any other AI-related matter in the sector.

The group "shall meet as frequently as necessary" and may invite subject experts.

## S4. Analytical Notes

### S4.1 — The corpus's first formal cross-regulator personnel coordination on AI governance

This is the finding of record. IRDAI's working group seats **a serving CERT-In scientist** and **the former CEO of ReBIT** (the RBI's wholly-owned technology subsidiary) alongside industry CISOs and an academic chair.

Every prior coordination finding in this corpus is negative. [[06_Frameworks/Cross_Regulator_Coordination_Pattern_Matrix]] records zero cross-reference between RBI and SEBI, between SEBI and CERT-In, and between RBI and MeitY; the sole positive instance was a *mandated downstream reporting obligation* (RBI→CERT-In incident reporting), not upstream rule-design collaboration.

IRDAI_2 is different in kind. It is **upstream, deliberate, personnel-level coordination at the framework-design stage** — CERT-In and RBI-adjacent expertise embedded in the body that will draft insurance-sector AI governance. On the current corpus this is the **strongest positive [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Signature/Institutional_Coherence]] instance in India**, and it comes from the smallest and least-represented of the four financial regulators.

⚠ Two cautions. First, individual participation is not institutional commitment — Saravade is *former* ReBIT, and Bahuguna sits as a scientist, not necessarily as CERT-In's institutional representative. Second, a working group is not an instrument; whether this coordination survives into the recommendations, and thence into a regulation, is unknown. **Characterise as coordination in body composition, not in regulatory output.**

### S4.2 — An AI audit framework covering pre- *and* post-deployment

Mandate item 9 is the most consequential for [[05_Concepts/05_Concepts_Signature/Evidentiary_Governance]]. An **AI audit framework addressing pre-deployment and post-deployment audit requirements** is precisely the artefact-generating machinery the construct concerns: post-deployment audit is impossible without retained inference-time records.

No Indian regulator has yet issued such a framework. If IRDAI delivers one by ~18 September 2026, it would be the corpus's first Indian instrument to specify AI audit artefacts across the lifecycle — and the first natural place for a retention horizon to be stated, addressing the abdication pattern recorded at RBI_8 para 97 and CERT_In_3 §12.7. **This is a live watch item, not a finding.**

### S4.3 — AI system inventory, again

Mandate item 4 requires "a structural assessment of the inventory of AI systems used by entities." AI asset inventory now appears across [[07_Institutions/Corpus/SEBI/SEBI_9]]/[[07_Institutions/Corpus/SEBI/SEBI_10]] (2019 AI/ML reporting), [[07_Institutions/Corpus/CERT_In/CERT_In_3_Blueprint_AI_Assisted_Exploitation_2026]] §12.2 (AI asset inventories, shadow-AI detection), [[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_9_AI_Accelerated_Cyber_Threats_Advisory_2026]] (classification, ownership and lifecycle management) and now IRDAI_2.

**Four regulators independently converging on AI inventory as the foundational governance primitive, none citing another.** This is a cleaner convergence-without-citation instance than the 2025 accountability convergence at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_19_IOSCO_AI_Capital_Markets_CR_2025]], because the object (an inventory) is more concrete than a principle (accountability) and the convergence therefore harder to attribute to shared drafting conventions.

### S4.4 — Claims and fraud prevention named explicitly

Mandate item 6 names **claims and fraud prevention** as in-scope use cases. These are the insurance sector's highest-stakes automated decisions for the policyholder, and the closest insurance analogue to the credit-decision territory governed by [[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_7]]. Any resulting framework will be directly comparable to RBI's digital-lending accountability architecture — a natural comparative pairing for [[02_Projects/P3_BFSI_JEIM]].

### S4.5 — IRDAI's corpus position improves

[[07_Institutions/Corpus/IRDAI/IRDAI_1_Maintenance_and_Sharing_of_Information_Regulations_2025]] is a Tier C node built on secondary commentary with four open VERIFY flags, and IRDAI has been the corpus's thinnest institutional track. IRDAI_2 is Tier B, better sourced, and AI-explicit. The institution moves from "AI-adjacent via general IS guidelines" to "actively constructing an AI governance framework with an audit component."
