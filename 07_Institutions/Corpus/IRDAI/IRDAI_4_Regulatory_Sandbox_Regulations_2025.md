**Type:** Binding regulation governing experimental/InsurTech product testing
**Target entities:** Insurers, insurtech intermediaries, and other applicants seeking to test innovative products/processes under regulatory relaxation

## Substantive Content

### Scale confirmed
More than 350 applications received across the first two cohorts (per Legal 500/Tuli & Co analysis) — the clearest available quantitative signal of InsurTech experimentation volume in India, though not AI-specific.

### Process requirements
Applicants must submit a report within 30 days of experiment-period completion detailing how the proposal met its stated objectives, plus policyholder feedback. The 2025 update shifts customer/business risk during the experimentation window fully onto the applicant.

### Confirmed gap
**No IRDAI-published cohort-level outcomes report, lessons-learned document, or approved-applicant list disaggregating AI-based products specifically was located.** The 350+ application figure covers all InsurTech categories broadly — whether AI-based products form a large or small share of that pipeline is currently unknown from any public IRDAI source. This is a direct data gap for anyone wanting to assess IRDAI's actual AI-product experimentation volume rather than inferring it from the general sandbox total.

### For a new Insurance_AI_Governance domain
Together with [[07_Institutions/Corpus/IRDAI/IRDAI_2_Working_Group_AI_Governance_2026]], this gives the domain a **two-track picture** of IRDAI's AI posture: a live but AI-non-disaggregated experimentation pathway (this node) running in parallel with a not-yet-reported study process for AI-specific governance rules (IRDAI_2). Neither track currently produces a binding AI-specific obligation — consistent with IRDAI's self-described "fourth silo" status relative to RBI/SEBI.
