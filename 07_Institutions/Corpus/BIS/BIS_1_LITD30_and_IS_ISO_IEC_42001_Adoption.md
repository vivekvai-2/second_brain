## Verification Status

⚠ **The adoption of IS/ISO/IEC 42001 is confirmed only indirectly** — from a BIS-hosted training brochure that names the standard, not from a gazette notification or a Manakonline catalogue entry. The **exact adoption date is not established** (the brochure was hosted October 2025; the course ran December 2025). Verify against `manakonline.in` before citing any date. The *fact* of adoption is well supported; the *date* is not.

⚠ **NABCB accreditation scheme: confirmed absent by search, not by direct verification.** No NABCB ISO/IEC 42001 accreditation programme, scheme document, or auditor-competence guidance was located; NABCB's own site returned nothing in this search round. Treat as a strong but not conclusive negative.

## Analytical Significance

**Standard before scheme before recognition.** BIS has adopted the standard and trains lead auditors against it. India has no confirmed national accreditation scheme for the certification bodies those auditors would work for, and no Indian regulator treats a resulting certificate as compliance evidence. Three layers, first and (partly) second built, third and fourth absent.

This is a **fourth instance of the "institution before regulation" sequencing pattern** in the corpus — after CSIRT-Power (operational Sept 2024, formalising regulation still draft), the Account Aggregator regime (Master Directions 2016 preceding the DEPA architecture by four years), and [[Sahamati]] (voluntary alliance preceding SRO recognition by years). Here the form is *capability before scheme*: India can audit against an AI management standard before it can accredit the auditing.

**Adoption without authorship.** BIS transposes; it has published no originally-authored Indian AI standard. For [[Governance_Capacity]] this is capacity deployed on transposition rather than domestic standard-setting, and it parallels the NCAIC finding recorded at [[07_Institutions/Corpus/NCAIC/NCAIC_1_AI_Governance_Framework_for_India_2025_26]], where the risk taxonomy substantially mirrors EU AI Act Art. 5.

**A blind spot in the corpus, now named.** PRIS tracked ISO/IEC 42001 across 32 notes and had never recorded the Indian body that adopts it. The corpus is regulator-dense and standards-body-blank. Standardisation is a governance venue the research programme has not been observing — and India holds a P-membership vote in SC 42, which means there is a stream of revealed Indian positions on AI standards that has never been examined (OQ-BIS-03).
