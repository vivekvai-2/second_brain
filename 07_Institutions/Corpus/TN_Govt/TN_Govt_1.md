## S2. Governance Context

Tamil Nadu's policy is the first state-level AI ethics framework in India — predating NITI Aayog's Responsible AI reports and all central regulatory instruments (**⚠VERIFY** primacy claim against any pre-2020 state document). It operates exclusively within state government procurement and deployment: all GoTN bodies, PSUs, and JVs must comply; private sector entities operating independently are not bound.

The policy sits structurally above central NITI Aayog guidance (which is advisory) but below central sectoral regulators (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI), with no explicit coordination mechanism linking it to any central framework. This is a horizontal governance gap: a sub-national ethics regime operating independently of, and contemporaneously with, national economic policy frameworks.

The oversight apex is the **Safe & Ethical AI Monitoring Committee**, chaired by the Chief Secretary, GoTN — the most senior IAS officer in the state. This gives the oversight structure unusually high institutional seniority for a technology policy document.

## S3. Core Framework

### TAM-DEF Framework
Six-dimensional evaluation framework for AI systems deployed by GoTN:

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| **T** — Transparency & Audit | AI systems must be auditable; decision logic explainable |
| **A** — Accountability & Legal | Clear legal responsibility chain for AI decisions |
| **M** — Misuse Protection | Safeguards against adversarial use and data exploitation |
| **D** — Digital Divide & Data Deficit | Equity considerations; avoid excluding marginalised populations |
| **E** — Ethics | Value alignment with Indian constitutional principles |
| **F** — Fairness & Equity | Bias mitigation across caste, gender, geography dimensions |

### DEEP-MAX Scorecard
Seven-parameter scoring tool for AI system certification before public deployment:

| Parameter | Dimension |
|---|---|
| **D** | Diversity |
| **E** | Equity & Fairness |
| **E** | Ethics |
| **P** | Privacy & Data Protection |
| **M** | Misuse Protection |
| **A** | Audit & Transparency |
| **X** | Cross-Geography & Cross-Society |

All GoTN AI systems must receive a DEEP-MAX score before public deployment. Systems must be re-evaluated at least every six months (**⚠VERIFY** mandatory vs. suggested interval).

### ACTS Blockchain
Proposed **AI Certification, Transparency & Scorecard Blockchain** — a tamperproof distributed ledger to record DEEP-MAX scores and prevent post-hoc score manipulation. Governance layer: scores stored on ACTS chain; public access envisaged. Status: proposed as of 2020 (**⚠VERIFY** whether ACTS was ever implemented).

### Procurement Guidelines
- All GoTN bodies must consult CEET before any AI procurement
- CEET evaluates proposed AI systems on DEEP-MAX before deployment approval
- Vendor AI systems must satisfy DEEP-MAX threshold (**⚠VERIFY** threshold score)
- Periodic re-evaluation every ≤6 months (**⚠VERIFY** binding vs. advisory)

### Other Key Elements
- **Open Data** policy: AI dataset availability for approved researchers and vendors
- **AI Sandbox**: regulatory sandbox for AI startups testing within GoTN ecosystem
- **Data Marketplace**: structured data exchange between GoTN and private sector

## S4. Analytical Significance

### Regulatory Parallelism — Sub-national track
TN_Govt_1 establishes a **fourth governance track** running in parallel to the three central tracks already documented in PRIS (BFSI regulation via RBI/SEBI/IRDAI, data governance via MeitY/DPDP, IP governance via DPIIT). A state government adopting an AI ethics framework with mandatory procurement controls, an evaluation committee chaired by the Chief Secretary, and a blockchain-based certification system constitutes a substantive sub-national regulatory apparatus — not merely a policy aspiration. The absence of any coordination clause with central frameworks (NITI Aayog, MeitY) is a structural gap: Regulatory_Parallelism here operates *vertically* (state vs. central) rather than horizontally (RBI vs. SEBI).

### Governance_Capacity — India's earliest sub-national AI evaluation infrastructure
The DEEP-MAX scorecard + CEET advisory architecture constitutes an **operational governance capacity** for AI oversight at sub-national level — predating NITI Aayog's RAI framework (NITI_3, August 2021 ⚠VERIFY). The ACTS Blockchain (if implemented) would be the only tamperproof AI certification infrastructure in Indian state governance. Even at proposal stage, it represents a governance-capacity investment that central frameworks have not replicated.

### Governance_Debt — Implementation gap
The policy has no enforcement mechanism for non-compliant GoTN departments: no sanction clause, no penalties for bypassing CEET review, no mandatory audit rights for the Safe & Ethical AI Monitoring Committee. The high oversight seniority (Chief Secretary) is not matched by an enforcement pathway. This is a textbook **Governance Debt**: the institutional form is present but the enforcement capacity is absent (**⚠VERIFY** whether any subsequent GoTN orders added enforcement teeth).

### fsQCA Relevance
TN_Govt_1 is a critical case for the fsQCA paper: India's sub-national ethics-first approach (TN 2020) vs. growth-first approach (Telangana 2020) within the same country illustrates within-country regulatory configuration divergence, potentially challenging the fsQCA assumption that "India" is a single configuration unit.
