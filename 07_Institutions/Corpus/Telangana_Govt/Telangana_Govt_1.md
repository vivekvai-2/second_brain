## S2. Governance Context

Telangana's AI Framework is an **economic development and AI adoption strategy** — structurally different from Tamil Nadu's contemporaneous ethics-first policy (TN_Govt_1, also 2020). Where TN centred governance as the primary lens (TAM-DEF, DEEP-MAX, ACTS Blockchain), Telangana centres **economic acceleration** with governance as one of six equal pillars.

The document explicitly acknowledges central government coordination: the state "would be designing even more attractive incentive packages in collaboration with DST (Department of Science and Technology) and MeitY (Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology)." This is the only Indian state AI document in the PRIS corpus to explicitly acknowledge the central-state coordination gap and flag intent to address it — though the coordination remains aspirational rather than institutionalised.

Applicability: unlike TN_Govt_1, this framework is not confined to state government bodies — it addresses private sector, startups, SMEs, and MNCs through the incentive architecture.

## S3. Core Framework

### Six Pillars

| Pillar | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1. Data Exchange Platform & HPAIC | Open Data portal; High-Performance AI Computing shared facility (IIT Hyderabad, JNTU, CDAC discussions) |
| 2. Skilling and Education | AI workforce target (+30,000 by 2022); TASK (Telangana Academy for Skill and Knowledge) partnership; AI training centers in Warangal, Karimnagar |
| 3. Governance, Ethics and Privacy | AI Ethics Group (advisory); Government AI Procurement Guidelines; Data ethics course for IT companies; alignment with PDP Bill 2019 |
| 4. Research and Innovation | T-AIM + NASSCOM; AI innovation cohorts by sector; RICH (Research and Innovation Circle Hyderabad) partnership; 10 annual R&D fellowships |
| 5. Enabling Adoption & Community Collaboration | GovTech Catalyst; Corporate-Startup Matchmaking; SME Platform; AI Newsletter |
| 6. AI-Innovestment Fund (AIF) | ESG-compliant humanitarian fund; "AI for social good" startups; focus sectors: Agriculture, Healthcare, Education, Mobility, Law Enforcement |

### Focus Sectors
Healthcare · Agriculture · Mobility · Education · Law Enforcement

### Governance, Ethics and Privacy Pillar (Pillar 3)
The AI Ethics Group is a **working committee** (not a statutory body), constituted with representatives from government, global companies, startups, research bodies (WEF, NASSCOM). Outputs: advisory guidelines, codes of practice, governance frameworks. No binding force; compliance with Government AI Procurement Guidelines required only for companies using GoT-published data.

Key elements:
- **Government AI Procurement Guidelines**: explainable AI; interpretability; accountability and contestability
- **Data ethics course**: mandatory for IT companies using GoT datasets
- **Industry-consumer-researcher roundtable**: guidelines for ethical data usage; aligned with PDP Bill 2019
- **Training user government agencies**: capabilities/limitations + human review triggers

### Incentive Architecture (selected)
- 100% SGST reimbursement for 3 years (startups with revenue <₹5 Cr)
- 75% travel reimbursement for AI conferences (max ₹2 Lakhs/year, first 2 years)
- R&D grant: up to 10% of R&D expenses or 2% of annual turnover, whichever lower
- One-time startup grant: up to ₹10 Lakhs (max 10 startups/year, 3 years)
- 50% subsidy on patent filing costs (max ₹5 Lakhs)
- AI MakerPark: physical + HPAIC + cloud infrastructure hub

## S4. Analytical Significance

### Regulatory Parallelism — Growth-first vs. Ethics-first sub-national divergence
TN_Govt_1 and Telangana_Govt_1 are directly contemporaneous (both June 2020) and structurally divergent in governance philosophy. TN's framework is ethics-first (mandatory DEEP-MAX scoring, ACTS Blockchain, Chief Secretary oversight). Telangana's framework is growth-first (economic targets, NASSCOM co-design, SGST incentives). Neither references the other. Neither references any central Indian framework (NITI Aayog had not published its Responsible AI series in June 2020). This is **within-country horizontal parallelism** at sub-national level — a configuration divergence that complicates treating "India" as a single governance unit in fsQCA.

The document's explicit mention of DST/MeitY coordination intent (while prescribing no coordination mechanism) illustrates the **aspiration-to-mechanism gap** in Indian federal AI governance design: the state acknowledges the central layer but does not operationalise the relationship.

### Governance_Capacity — T-AIM + HPAIC as institutional infrastructure investment
Telangana's AI governance capacity investment is primarily *institutional* (T-AIM as execution body) and *infrastructural* (HPAIC shared facility). T-AIM is a unique model: a state AI mission body co-designed with an industry association (NASSCOM), giving it private-sector co-governance character. This is governance capacity innovation distinct from TN's state-bureaucracy model (CEET under TNeGA). The HPAIC proposal (shared computing facility with IIT Hyderabad, JNTU, CDAC) represents infrastructure-as-governance-capacity.

### Governance_Debt — Advisory ethics without binding mechanism
The AI Ethics Group is advisory only. No enforcement mechanism, no statutory basis, no penalties for non-compliance with procurement guidelines (applicable only to companies using GoT data — not all AI deployments). The PDP Bill 2019 alignment is forward-looking (the Bill was not enacted; it was later withdrawn and replaced by DPDP 2023). The entire Governance, Ethics and Privacy pillar is structurally a **governance form without governance force** — the institutional shell of an ethics regime with advisory outputs and no binding effect. This is Governance_Debt at the pillar level.

### fsQCA Relevance
This document, read alongside TN_Govt_1, is critical for the within-India comparative analysis. The two 2020 state frameworks represent two distinct **regulatory configurations** for AI governance:
- TN: ethics-mandatory + procurement-binding + blockchain-certification + high-seniority oversight (Condition set: ethics_mandatory=1, growth_incentives=0, central_coordination=0)
- Telangana: growth-incentive + ethics-advisory + central-coordination-aspirational (Condition set: ethics_mandatory=0, growth_incentives=1, central_coordination=0.5)

Neither achieved the fsQCA condition of "trustworthy AI governance" at the outcome level as of 2020 (both are framework documents, not implemented systems). The divergence tests whether sub-national units within a federal system should be treated as distinct configurations rather than as single-country observations.
