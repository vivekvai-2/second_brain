## Substantive Content — the four-tier grievance chain

| Tier | Mechanism | Authority | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Automated grievance ascertainment against Participant records, with compensation determination | Self-regulatory | Participation Terms Cl. 14.1 |
| 2 | Participant's Grievance Redressal Officer / Internal Ombudsman | Self-regulatory (contractual) | Cl. 14.2 |
| 3 | **Statutory regulator ombudsman** — RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2021; SEBI SCORES; IRDAI Grievance Redressal Cell; PFRDA Grievance Redressal Cell | **Statutory** | Cl. 14.2 |
| 4 | Sahamati-empanelled **ODR** institution — mediation, failing which arbitration by an independent sole arbitrator | Self-regulatory | Cl. 14.2 |

Tier 4 activates only where the statutory route "is not available, accessible or resolution by which is unsatisfactory."

## Analytical Significance

**This is the most developed contestability architecture anywhere in the PRIS corpus, and it governs data sharing rather than algorithmic decisions.** Set against the finding recorded in [[Contestability_Redress]] — DPDP 2023 contains no automated-decision-making provision, and India has no equivalent of GDPR Art. 22(3) — the comparison is stark: four tiers of redress for a consent dispute, none for an adverse automated decision. The design capacity exists; it has not been pointed at AI. This is the strongest single instance of [[Governance_Debt]] currently in the corpus, because it defeats the capability rebuttal outright.

**Four-regulator ombudsman integration** is also the clearest operational instance of [[Institutional_Coherence]] located to date: RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA grievance machinery all feed one ecosystem's redress chain without a coordinating statute. Compare [[Regulatory_Parallelism]], where the same four regulators produce non-cross-referencing AI instruments. **Same regulators, same period, coherent on consent and parallel on AI** — a within-institution control that strengthens the parallelism claim considerably, since it isolates the variable to the subject matter rather than the institutions.

**Self-regulation as governance layer.** Three of four tiers are non-statutory. An affected person's practical redress depends substantially on contractual mechanisms enforced by an industry body. For [[Legitimacy]] this raises the question the concept note flags as an open gap — no legitimacy-perception study exists for any Indian AI or DPI regulator, and this ecosystem, with its unusually explicit statutory/self-regulatory boundary, is a strong candidate case for one.

## Coding Notes (IG 2.0)

Tiers 1–2 and 4 are contractual norms (ADIC, no statutory Or-else); tier 3 inherits a statutory Or-else from the applicable ombudsman scheme. A single grievance chain spanning both deontic classes — a clean [[Deontic_Bifurcation]] instance of a kind not yet recorded there: bifurcation **across tiers of one procedure** rather than within one document.
