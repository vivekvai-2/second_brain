## Instrument Identity

**Full title:** Recommendation of the Council on Artificial Intelligence, OECD/LEGAL/0449
**Type:** OECD Council Recommendation — non-binding soft law (Adherent countries commit politically, not legally)
**Target entities:** OECD member states and non-member Adherents; addressed to governments, and — via the five values-based principles — to AI actors generally
**Legal basis:** OECD Council decision-making authority under the OECD Convention; Recommendations are not legally binding on Adherents
**Companion documents:** G20 AI Principles (Annex to the 2019 G20 Ministerial Statement on Trade and Digital Economy) — a verbatim adoption of the five OECD values-based principles, not a separate instrument; OECD's 2024 Ministerial report on implementation (below)

**Primary source link:** https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/oecd-legal-0449 (Legal Instruments Compendium — version history shows both 2019 baseline and 2024 amendment)
**2024 update announcement:** https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2024/05/oecd-updates-ai-principles-to-stay-abreast-of-rapid-technological-developments.html
**G20 AI Principles Annex:** https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/economy/g20_summit/osaka19/pdf/documents/en/annex_08.pdf (adopted at Osaka Summit, 8–9 June 2019 Ministerial Statement; reaffirmed in the Osaka Leaders' Declaration, 28–29 June 2019)

## Substantive Content

### Two-tier structure
**Five values-based principles** (addressed to AI actors generally): inclusive growth and sustainable development; respect for the rule of law, human rights, and democratic values (including fairness and privacy); transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; and accountability.

**Five recommendations to governments** (addressed to Adherent states, on policy and international co-operation): investing in AI R&D; fostering a digital ecosystem for AI; shaping an enabling policy environment for AI; building human capacity and preparing for labour-market transformation; and international co-operation for trustworthy AI.

### 2024 amendment
The Recommendation was revised at the 2024 Ministerial Council Meeting (3 May 2024) "to stay abreast of rapid technological developments" (OECD's own characterisation) — the amendment updates the instrument to address generative AI and foundation models at the principle level; it does not add an enforcement mechanism or alter the Recommendation's non-binding legal character.

### Implementation tracking — most authoritative primary document identified
**C/MIN(2024)17** — "Report assessing implementation, dissemination, and continued relevance of the OECD Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence," submitted by the OECD Secretariat to the 2024 Ministerial Council Meeting (23 April 2024). States the Recommendation "is being implemented by Adherents," with the **OECD.AI Policy Observatory** national-policy survey (updated twice yearly, 850+ policies across 60+ countries as of the most recent search) as the main tracking tool. This is a living database, not a discrete dated report — cite by access date when used.
Link: https://one.oecd.org/document/C/MIN(2024)17/en/pdf | Observatory: https://oecd.ai

**OECD Digital Economy Paper No. 311** — "State of implementation of the OECD AI Principles: Insights from national AI policies" (1 February 2021, DOI 10.1787/1cd40c44-en) — earlier implementation-insights document; full text via OECD iLibrary (RePEc mirror does not host full text directly).

### India-specific engagement — confirmed gap
India is not an OECD member. It participates in the OECD.AI Policy Observatory's general national-policy database, and OECD engaged directly with India at the **India AI Impact Summit 2026** (hosting the GPAI Council Meeting at Ministerial level within the Summit, per OECD media advisory, 15 February 2026). **No dedicated OECD.AI Policy Observatory country review or readiness report specifically assessing India's adoption of the OECD Principles was located** — distinct from UNESCO's India Readiness Assessment (see [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_18_UNESCO_Recommendation_Ethics_AI_2021]]). India appears only in the Observatory's general database, not via a standalone country-specific implementation assessment against these Principles.

### Items sourced but not independently verified — flagged, not intaken separately
No additional standalone OECD Secretariat working paper devoted specifically to cross-member "implementation gaps" as a named analytical category was identified beyond Digital Economy Paper No. 311 and C/MIN(2024)17 above; sectoral OECD AI Papers (health workforce AI risks, No. 28, Nov 2024; AI in public employment services, No. 19, June 2024) are adjacent but domain-specific rather than principle-wide, and are not coded here.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For P1 (SLR — Regulation & Governance) and the fsQCA model
This node closes a citation gap: the [[06_Frameworks/OECD_AI_Principles]] framework note's entire synthesis — including its fsQCA priors (high EA/SI, low SR/RL) — previously rested on zero primary-source corpus anchoring. C/MIN(2024)17 in particular gives a citable, dated, OECD-authored implementation assessment to support the framework note's "legitimacy without architecture" argument (principles proliferate; enforcement does not follow).

### Coordination / Parallelism Pattern
**Convergence anchor, upstream of UNESCO:** the OECD Principles (2019) and the UNESCO Recommendation (2021) were developed on an overlapping timeline with substantially overlapping principle content; the G20's verbatim adoption of the five OECD values-principles (rather than drafting its own) is itself evidence of a single normative-vocabulary source cascading through multiple intergovernmental bodies — a "principle convergence" pattern that sits upstream of the "principle convergence + implementation parallelism" pattern already documented in [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_18_UNESCO_Recommendation_Ethics_AI_2021]].
