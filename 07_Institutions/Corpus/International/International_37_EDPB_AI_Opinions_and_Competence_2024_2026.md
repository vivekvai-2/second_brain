# International_37 — EDPB Guidance on AI and the GDPR × AI Act Competence Allocation (2018–2026)

**Framework:** [[GDPR]] · [[EU_AI_Act]] | **Added:** 2026-08-02 (GDPR sourcing pass)

Consolidated node — the EU's interpretive and supervisory-competence layer for AI under data-protection law.

---

## Document Identity

**(a) WP29 Guidelines on Automated Individual Decision-Making and Profiling (WP251)**
Adopted 3 Oct 2017; revised final 6 Feb 2018; **endorsed by the EDPB 25 May 2018**.
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/automated-decision-making-and-profiling_en
Status: **current — operative by EDPB carry-over endorsement, never superseded.** No EDPB-authored replacement exists.
Two operative holdings: Art. 22 establishes a **general prohibition**, not a right requiring active invocation; "solely automated" excludes any **meaningful** human involvement — rubber-stamping does not exempt a decision from Art. 22.

**(b) EDPB Opinion 28/2024 on data protection aspects of processing personal data in the context of AI models**
Adopted 17–18 Dec 2024 under **Art. 64(2) GDPR** (matter of general application affecting more than one Member State).
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf
Three questions: (1) when an AI model can be considered **anonymous**; (2) whether and how **legitimate interest** can ground model development and deployment; (3) consequences where a model was **developed using unlawfully processed personal data**.
Status: current; the most substantive EDPB instrument on AI–GDPR interaction.

**(c) EDPB–EDPS Joint Opinion 1/2026 on the proposal for a Digital Omnibus on AI**
Adopted 19–20 Jan 2026.
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-01/edpb_edps_jointopinion_202601_proposal_ai-omnibus_en.pdf
Status: current; the most directly on-point document on supervisory-competence allocation.

**(d) Joint EDPB–Commission Guidelines on AI Act–GDPR interplay — ⚠ NOT YET PUBLISHED.**
Confirmed work in progress with the AI Office, expected 2026, modelled on the EDPB–Commission DMA–GDPR joint guidelines (draft published for consultation 9 Oct 2025). Track for retrieval.

## Competence Allocation — the operative rules

| Competence | Holder | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Personal-data processing generally, **including within AI systems** | **National DPAs retain primary supervisory competence** | AI Act **Art. 2(7)** |
| AI systems built on a **general-purpose AI model where model and system share the same provider** — extending to systems integrated into VLOPs/VLOSEs under the DSA | **AI Office** | AI Act **Art. 75** |
| AI systems of EU institutions | EDPS, exclusively | AI Act **Art. 74(9)** — ⚠ the Joint Opinion notes this sits in **recitals rather than operative text** and calls for codification |

## Analytical Significance

**This is the benchmark comparison that survives scrutiny.** Two of P4's three international benchmarks were materially weakened this week: the EU AI Act has zero cited harmonised standards, so Art. 40 presumption of conformity attaches to nothing ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_33_CEN_CENELEC_JTC21_Harmonised_Standards_2026]]); and Singapore has no binding AI instrument at all ([[Singapore_AI_Governance]]). **The competence-allocation material does not collapse.**

The EU has: a statutory allocation of supervisory competence between its data-protection and AI regulators (AI Act Arts. 2(7), 74(9), 75); a joint opinion from both supervisory bodies interpreting it; and joint interpretive guidelines with the Commission in preparation. India has **four financial regulators plus MeitY issuing AI instruments with zero cross-references** and no competence-allocation instrument of any kind. For [[Institutional_Coherence]] and [[Regulatory_Parallelism]] this is the cleanest surviving contrast, and P4 should lean on it precisely because the other two comparisons had to be softened.

**A caveat that strengthens rather than weakens it.** Even the EU's allocation is imperfect — the EDPS's exclusive competence sits in recitals rather than operative text, and the two supervisory bodies had to issue a joint opinion partly to clarify boundaries. Noting this makes the comparison credible rather than triumphalist: the EU has an *imperfect but explicit* allocation; India has none. That is a more defensible claim than asserting EU coherence.

**Opinion 28/2024 question 3 is the one with teeth.** Consequences where a model was developed on unlawfully processed data goes to remedy at the model level — a question no Indian instrument addresses and which has no Indian analogue even in principle, given DPDP's absence of ADM provisions and the lack of any model-level obligation. Connects to [[Assurance_Reuse_Gap]] and [[Evidentiary_Governance]]: if a model is tainted at training, what evidence discharges the problem?

**WP251's "meaningful human involvement" test** is the only place in the corpus where the nominal-vs-effective oversight distinction has been given operative legal content by a supervisory authority. [[Human_Oversight]] grounds that distinction on EU AI Act Art. 14(4)(b)'s automation-bias clause; WP251 supplies an older and legally tested formulation. Add to the Art. 14(4) coding scheme as an interpretive anchor.

## Coding Notes (IG 2.0)

Mixed deontic classes in one node. WP251 and Opinion 28/2024 are **non-binding guidance with high interpretive authority** — ADIC norms whose Or-else is inherited from the GDPR provisions they interpret rather than stated. The competence-allocation provisions (AI Act Arts. 2(7), 74(9), 75) are **binding constitutive rules** allocating jurisdiction rather than regulating conduct — a rule type the corpus has little of, since Indian instruments allocate obligations but almost never allocate *supervisory competence*. Worth flagging as a distinct ADICO category: **competence-allocating rules**, absent from the Indian corpus entirely.
