# International_33 — CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 Harmonised Standards Track and the Article 40 Conformity Mechanism (2026)

**Framework:** [[ISO_IEC_42001]] · [[EU_AI_Act]] | **Added:** 2026-08-02 (ISO 42001 sourcing pass)

Consolidated node covering the EU harmonised-standards pipeline and the legal mechanism that makes it the only route to presumption of conformity.

- **Issuing body:** CEN-CENELEC Joint Technical Committee 21 (JTC 21), under Commission standardisation request

## The Article 40 Mechanism

EU AI Act **Article 40** grants presumption of conformity only to AI systems conforming to **harmonised standards published and cited in the Official Journal of the EU**. Because no AI-specific harmonised standard has yet been cited, **presumption of conformity under the AI Act currently attaches to nothing**. This is the legal fact that makes the ISO/IEC 42001 recognition question answerable in the negative as a matter of structure rather than of timing.

**prEN 18286 timeline:** public enquiry 30 Oct 2025 – 22 Jan 2026; target availability Q4 2026. On publication and OJ citation it will be **the first standard anywhere to carry AI Act presumption of conformity**.

## Relationship to ISO/IEC 42001 — deliberate separation

The JTC 21 deliverables are being drafted as **distinct EU-specific instruments**, not as adoptions or endorsements of ISO/IEC 42001. The European AI Office signalled in **May 2024** that ISO/IEC 42001 was **not fully aligned with the final AI Act text**, and it is not part of the harmonisation process.

Note the structural point: prEN 18286 is itself a *quality management system* standard mapped to Art. 17. So the EU is building its own management-system standard rather than importing the international one — which indicates the objection to 42001 was not to management-system standards as such, but to that standard's fit with the Act's specific obligations.

## Analytical Significance

**Corrects an India-lags framing.** On presumption of conformity for AI, the EU is at zero, the US is at zero (NIST AI RMF is voluntary and non-regulatory by design), the UK is at zero and India is at zero. The world's most advanced binding AI regulation has, as of mid-2026, no operative conformity-assessment standard behind it. Any P4 comparison must state this or it will overstate EU maturity — see the correction recorded in [[Assurance_Reuse_Gap]].

**Sharpens the [[EU_AI_Act]] deferral picture.** The Digital Omnibus deferral of Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 is already recorded on that framework node. This node supplies the likely mechanical reason: the conformity-assessment standards the obligations depend on were not going to exist in time. The deferral is arguably a consequence of the standards pipeline, not an independent political choice — worth stating carefully, as it is an inference rather than a documented causal claim.

**fsQCA implication.** A "harmonised standards available" condition has zero variance across all cases and carries no configurational information. Do not calibrate on it. See [[ISO_IEC_42001]] for the recommended SR recalibration on national accreditation-scheme existence, which does vary.
