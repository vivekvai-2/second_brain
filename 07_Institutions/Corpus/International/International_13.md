## S2. Governance Context

International_13 addresses central banks as **AI deployers** rather than AI regulators — a distinct analytical position not covered by other corpus nodes. The existing international nodes (International_3/MAS AIRG, International_10/BoE FPC, International_11/FSB TPRM) address central banks as *regulators* of AI risk in the financial sector. International_13 addresses how central banks govern AI *within their own operations*.

This distinction is significant for the corpus:
- Central banks are simultaneously AI deployers (this node) and AI regulators (BoE FPC, MAS AIRG, FSB)
- The governance capacity required internally (International_13) is distinct from — and a prerequisite for — the supervisory capacity to regulate external AI (International_10)
- International_7 (BIS/IFC Report No. 18) provides the empirical evidence base (60 central banks survey, AE-EME gap) that this node's framework is designed to address

**India relevance:** RBI as EME central bank is in the target population of this framework. International_13's 10-action framework provides the governance architecture that RBI_FREE_AI_2025 (aspirational principles for external BFSI firms) does not yet apply to RBI's own internal AI governance.

## S3. Key Framework Elements — 10 Practical Actions

| Action | Domain | Description |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Governance structure | Establish AI governance committee with cross-functional representation (risk, technology, policy, compliance, audit) |
| A2 | Risk accountability | Define clear AI risk ownership across Three Lines of Defence; no governance gaps at line-of-business/risk/audit interfaces |
| A3 | AI risk strategy | Develop comprehensive AI risk management strategy addressing model risks, data security, cyber risks, and reputational risks |
| A4 | Data governance | Implement data governance policies covering confidentiality, access controls, and audit trails for all data used in AI systems |
| A5 | Cyber security | Establish AI-specific cyber security controls addressing AI model attacks, data leakage, and AI-assisted threat vectors |
| A6 | Information security | Address confidentiality of central bank data used in AI training and inference; prevent unauthorised disclosure via AI systems |
| A7 | Monitoring and oversight | Establish ongoing performance tracking, anomaly detection, and escalation procedures for deployed AI systems |
| A8 | Organisational readiness | Develop workforce competencies for AI governance including risk, technology, and domain expertise integration |
| A9 | Integration with existing frameworks | Adapt 3LoD model for AI-specific risk characteristics rather than creating a parallel governance structure |
| A10 | Adaptive governance | Build governance frameworks that can evolve as AI capabilities and risks change; avoid static compliance-only approaches |

## S4. Key Verbatim Quotes

The use cases for AI span a broad range of critical functions of a central bank including data analysis, research, economic forecasting, payments, supervision and banknote production." — Foreword

> "A comprehensive risk management strategy can leverage existing risk management models and processes, in particular the well established three lines of defence model." — Foreword

## S5. Analytical Notes

**3LoD for central bank AI:**
The explicit recommendation to adapt the 3LoD model (Action A9) rather than create a parallel governance structure is analytically significant. The existing international corpus (Batch 4/6: IIA_1, Schuett 2023, Davies 2018, Bantleon 2020) extensively documents 3LoD adaptation challenges for AI. International_13 adds the specific central bank context: a sovereign institution with unique confidentiality obligations, no market-competitive disclosure incentives, and reputational risk that is systemic rather than firm-specific.

**RBI gap assessment:**
Mapping International_13's 10 actions against RBI's known governance posture:

| Action | RBI status (as of Batch 11) |
|---|---|
| A1 — AI governance committee | RBI FREE-AI_2025: FREE-AI Committee (external advisory); RBI internal AI governance committee: ⚠V-I13-2 (unknown) |
| A3 — AI risk strategy | RBI_FREE_AI_2025 provides principles; operative internal strategy unknown |
| A4 — Data governance | RBI_1 (IT Governance), RBI_2 (IT Outsourcing): technology-neutral; AI training data standards not specified |
| A9 — 3LoD adaptation | Not addressed in any current RBI instrument |
| A10 — Adaptive governance | Not addressed |

RBI coverage: partial on A3 (principles only); weak/absent on A1, A4, A9, A10. The central bank's internal AI governance maturity is at Maturity Level 2 (Managed: some artefacts exist) against this framework's expectations.

**Relationship with International_7:**
International_7 (BIS/IFC Report No. 18, Apr 2025) provides the empirical demand side — 60 central banks, AE-EME gap, governance fragmentation. International_13 provides the supply side — the governance framework those central banks should adopt. Together these two nodes constitute the corpus's central bank AI governance sub-cluster.
