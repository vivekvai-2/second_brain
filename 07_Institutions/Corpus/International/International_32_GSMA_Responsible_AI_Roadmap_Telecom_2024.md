## Substantive Content

### Core function
Described as the "first sector-wide approach of its kind" — gives mobile network operators a structured way to assess responsible-AI maturity, set targets, and measure progress, drawing explicitly on OECD and UNESCO principles (the same normative-vocabulary lineage already documented in [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_23_OECD_AI_Principles_2019_2024]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_18_UNESCO_Recommendation_Ethics_AI_2021]]). Explicitly voluntary — an industry expectation-setting tool, not a regulation, with 19 operators committed at launch.

### Technical-standards layer
ETSI SAI and 3GPP AI/ML standards address a narrower, more technical question — securing AI within network infrastructure specifically (as opposed to governing AI as a business/consumer-facing decision system, which is TRAI/Ofcom/FCC's focus). This is a genuinely distinct governance layer: technical network-security standardisation rather than regulatory or industry-ethics governance.

### Confirmed absence at the ITU level
No standalone ITU-published (as opposed to GSMA-published or GSMA-submitted-to-ITU) telecom-network-specific AI governance framework was located. The GSMA Roadmap is industry self-governance feeding into ITU's regulatory dialogue (via the GSR-26 contribution), not an ITU standard in its own right — the global standard-setting body itself has not yet produced telecom-specific AI governance guidance, distinct from ITU's general AI-related work.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For a new Telecom_AI_Governance domain — the industry self-governance layer
This gives the domain a fourth governance-layer type, distinct from the three regulatory postures already mapped (TRAI's broad-aspirational, Ofcom's monitoring-only, FCC's narrow-binding): **industry self-governance operating in the absence of binding global standards.** Useful for a complete picture of how telecom-AI governance is actually being shaped — regulators, in every jurisdiction examined, are moving slower than the industry body coordinating its own members.

### Comparative note — same normative lineage as elsewhere in the corpus
The GSMA Roadmap's explicit basis in OECD and UNESCO principles is a further instance of the "single normative vocabulary cascading through multiple bodies" pattern already documented for the G20 AI Principles' verbatim adoption of OECD's five values-principles ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/International/International_23_OECD_AI_Principles_2019_2024]]) — worth noting as a recurring diffusion pattern across at least three distinct governance layers (intergovernmental, industry-association, and now telecom-specific-industry).
