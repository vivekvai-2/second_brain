## Substantive Content

### Risk taxonomy for generative AI (present-day)
- **Mis- and disinformation**: synthetic content increasingly indistinguishable from human-generated content, with material harm potential on "science-related issues, such as vaccine effectiveness and climate change," and in polarised political contexts.
- **Bias perpetuation**: generative AI risks perpetuating societal biases at scale.
- **Intellectual property**: unresolved debates on whether training generative AI on copyrighted material constitutes infringement.
- **Labour market shifts**: potential disruption from automation of creative/cognitive tasks.

### Longer-term / emergent risks
- Security, surveillance, over-reliance, academic dishonesty, and market concentration flagged as additional present-day considerations.
- **Emergent behaviours** (debated within the AI community): increased agency, power-seeking, and development of "unknown sub-goals determined by machines to achieve core objectives programmed by a human but that might not be aligned with human values and intent." The paper notes that if unaddressed, these could lead to "systemic harms" — an early (2023) statement of what later frameworks term agentic AI / misalignment risk.

### Policy framing
The paper is structured around "potential future concerns and risks" and "risk mitigation measures," positioning itself as agenda-setting input for OECD members rather than prescriptive guidance.
