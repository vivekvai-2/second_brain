## Substantive Content

### Core position — existing standards suffice, no new ones needed
The Paper **explicitly states existing Insurance Core Principles (ICPs) remain "appropriate and relevant" for managing AI-related risks — no new standards are proposed.** This is the most consequential single finding in this comparator set: the global insurance standard-setter has taken the position that AI does not require new prudential/conduct standards, only applied interpretation of existing ones.

### Five-section structure
Risk-based supervision and proportionality; governance and accountability; robustness, safety and security; transparency and explainability; fairness, ethics and redress — plus an Annex of Member examples.

### IRDAI's direct institutional link
Because IRDAI is an IAIS member, this Paper is the most likely direct input into whatever the WG-AI ([[07_Institutions/Corpus/IRDAI/IRDAI_2_Working_Group_AI_Governance_2026]]) eventually recommends — its mandate item 5 explicitly requires review of "global regulatory and supervisory approaches to AI." If IRDAI's eventual framework echoes the "existing principles suffice" posture rather than creating IRDAI-specific new rules, that would be a direct, traceable instance of international standard-diffusion into Indian regulatory design — worth checking for explicitly once the WG-AI reports (~September 2026).

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### Three distinct international postures, now fully mapped
This completes a genuine three-way comparative spread for insurance-sector AI governance design: US (NAIC) — decentralised, template-adoption, new written-program requirement; EU (EIOPA) — centralised interpretive layering atop a binding horizontal AI Act; Global (IAIS) — no new standards needed, existing principles suffice. None of the three creates a wholly new AI-specific insurance regulatory regime; all three interpret or extend existing structures. This is itself a finding: **no jurisdiction or standard-setter examined has built AI-specific insurance regulation from scratch** — every comparator extends what already existed. Worth stating explicitly in the domain synthesis as a contrast to sectors like AI-specific banking regulation (RBI FREE-AI, EU AI Act's own risk-tiering) where more purpose-built architecture exists.

### Explicit link to a live watch item
Since IAIS's "no new standards needed" position is now the clearest statement of the "moderate" pole in international insurance-AI regulatory intensity, it directly informs how to interpret whatever IRDAI eventually produces — a genuinely useful predictive benchmark rather than merely descriptive comparator material.
