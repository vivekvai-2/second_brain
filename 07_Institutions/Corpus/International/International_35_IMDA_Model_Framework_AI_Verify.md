# International_35 — IMDA/PDPC Model AI Governance Frameworks and AI Verify (2019–2026)

**Framework:** [[Singapore_AI_Governance]] | **Added:** 2026-08-02 (Singapore sourcing pass)

Consolidated node — Singapore's cross-sectoral track, four framework editions plus the testing infrastructure.

---

## Document Identity

**Model AI Governance Framework, 1st edition.** IMDA + PDPC, **23 January 2019** (launched at WEF Davos). Four areas: internal governance; decision-making models; operations management; customer relationship management. Issued "for consultation," voluntary. **Superseded.**

**Model AI Governance Framework, 2nd edition.** IMDA + PDPC, **21 January 2020** (WEF). Four areas: internal governance structures/measures; human involvement in AI-augmented decision-making; operations management; stakeholder interaction and communication. Two ethical principles: decisions should be explainable, transparent and fair; AI solutions should be human-centric. **Current, voluntary** — the operative "traditional AI" framework.
Link: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/Files/PDPC/PDF-Files/Resource-for-Organisation/AI/SGModelAIGovFramework2.pdf

**Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI.** IMDA + AI Verify Foundation, **30 May 2024**. Nine dimensions: Accountability, Data, Trusted Development and Deployment, Incident Reporting, Testing and Assurance, Security, Content Provenance, Safety and Alignment R&D, AI for Public Good. Explicitly **expands, does not replace** the traditional framework. Current, voluntary.
Link: https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Model-AI-Governance-Framework-for-Generative-AI-May-2024-1-1.pdf

**Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI.** IMDA, **~January 2026** (⚠ date not pinned — a law-firm summary says January 2026; the hosted file carries a 19 May 2026 timestamp). Four pillars: assess and bound risks upfront; make humans meaningfully accountable; implement technical controls and processes; enable end-user responsibility. Current, voluntary, additive.
Link: https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/about/emerging-tech-and-research/artificial-intelligence/mgf-for-agentic-ai.pdf

**AI Verify.** MVP launched by IMDA/PDPC **25 May 2022**; **AI Verify Foundation** launched June 2023 at Asia Tech x Singapore with seven premier founding members (IMDA, Aicadium/Temasek, IBM, Microsoft, Google, Red Hat, Salesforce); toolkit open-sourced 7 June 2023; testing framework updated **29 May 2025** to cover traditional and generative AI.
Link: https://aiverifyfoundation.sg/what-is-ai-verify/
**Governed by the independent Foundation, not by government** — IMDA is a member, not the owner.

## The Testability Finding

AI Verify operationalises **eleven principles**: Transparency, Explainability, Repeatability/Reproducibility, Safety, Security, Robustness, Fairness, Data Governance, Accountability, Human Agency and Oversight, Inclusive Growth/Social and Environmental Well-being. Explicitly aligned to EU, OECD, G7 and US frameworks.

**Technical tests run on only three: Fairness, Explainability, Robustness.** The other eight — including **Human Agency and Oversight** and Accountability — are assessed by documented **process checks**.

This is practitioner-side confirmation of a conclusion the corpus reached independently from the academic literature earlier today: [[Human_Oversight]] records that no validated psychometric instrument for human oversight exists and that it is the weakest-measured EA constituent. Singapore's testing infrastructure, built with IBM, Microsoft and Google, reaches the same conclusion by a different route — it cannot test human oversight technically, so it does not try. **Two independent routes to the same finding**, and worth stating in the methodology chapter as convergent evidence rather than as a single-source claim.

It also corroborates the ordering in [[Explainability]] and [[Fairness]] — those two are among the three the market considers technically testable, which is consistent with their being the best-instrumented constructs in the academic literature.

## Analytical Significance

**Layering rather than revision.** Singapore's cross-sectoral response to each technology shift has been to publish an *additional* voluntary framework — traditional (2020) → generative (2024) → agentic (2026) — none replacing the last. Four live documents, all voluntary, all additive. For [[Institutional_Coherence]] this is coherent-by-accretion: no contradiction, but a growing stack the deployer must reconcile unaided. Contrast [[Regulatory_Parallelism]], where the problem is multiple *regulators*; here a single body generates the layering.

**The agentic framework is the corpus's newest agentic-governance instrument** and should be read into [[Agentic_AI_Governance]]. Its second pillar — "make humans meaningfully accountable" — is a direct statement of the nominal-vs-effective oversight distinction that [[Human_Oversight]] treats as its defining boundary condition.

**Governance delegated outside government.** AI Verify sits with an independent multi-stakeholder foundation whose premier members are the firms being assessed. Structurally comparable to [[Sahamati]]'s SRO recognition in India's AA ecosystem, with a difference worth noting: Sahamati was *recognised by* the regulator; the AI Verify Foundation was *spun out with* the regulator as one member among several. Two models of delegated governance, both outside the corpus's regulator-centric default.

## Comparative Anchor

The Government of India's own **PIB comparative annex to the India AI Governance Guidelines (4 November 2025)** characterises Singapore's approach as "Voluntary, use-case based approach that emphasizes a sectoral approach based on governance frameworks," naming Veritas and AI Verify, set beside the EU's "Statutory framework" and the UK's "Context-based and cross sectoral framework."
Link: https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc2025115685601.pdf

**This is a primary Government of India document performing the exact Singapore/EU/UK triangulation P4 needs, in the same document that anchors India's own approach.** It should be cited in P4's benchmark section as evidence that the comparison set is the government's own, not the researcher's construction — which strengthens the paper's framing considerably. Cross-reference [[07_Institutions/Corpus/MeitY/MeitY_2]].

## Confirmed Literature Gap

No peer-reviewed study compares Singapore + India FREE-AI + EU AI Act + UK regulator-led model as **co-equal comparators**. The strongest located sources are Hilliard et al., *Artificial intelligence policy worldwide: a comparative analysis*, **Royal Society Open Science** (2026), which groups Singapore among jurisdictions with guidance but no binding legislation; and the PIB annex above, which is governmental rather than peer-reviewed. **P4's comparative contribution fills a genuine void.** Record in the contribution statement.
