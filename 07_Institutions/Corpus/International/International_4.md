## Substantive Content

### Core rationale
The document states that, compared to human decision-making, the nature and increasing use of AIDA "may heighten the risks of systematic [bias/error]" (text truncated at extraction boundary) — establishing the core justification for principles-based (rather than purely activity-based) AI governance: AI's systematic/scalable nature changes the risk profile relative to individual human decisions, even where the underlying activity (e.g., credit decisioning) is already regulated.

### Significance of the four-principle structure
FEAT's four named principles (Fairness, Ethics, Accountability, Transparency) form a durable taxonomy that recurs, in modified form, across later frameworks in this corpus:
- RBI FREE-AI's Seven Sutras include Fairness & Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design (≈ Transparency) — partial structural overlap with FEAT, six years later, in a different jurisdiction, with no evidence of direct citation.
- India AI Governance Guidelines' sutras likewise echo Fairness/Accountability/Transparency-type categories.

⚠ VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING — before asserting direct lineage/influence claims (FEAT → India's sutras), confirm whether any Indian framework document explicitly cites FEAT; current evidence is structural similarity only, not demonstrated citation.

### For P1 (AI Governance SLR) and Institutional Coherence / Regulatory Parallelism
FEAT (2019) vs. RBI FREE-AI Sutras (2025) vs. India AI Governance Guidelines Sutras (2025, explicitly derived from RBI FREE-AI) presents a three-way comparison of principles-based AI governance taxonomies across two jurisdictions and seven years.
