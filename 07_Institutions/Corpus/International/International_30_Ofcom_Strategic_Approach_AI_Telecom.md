## Substantive Content

### Explicitly technology-neutral, no telecom-specific AI rules
Ofcom's own framing: **"regulated companies are essentially free to deploy AI as they see fit, without needing our permission."** For telecoms specifically, Ofcom's stated activities are limited to: monitoring AI-related fraud/scams, engaging with industry on AI-related cybersecurity risks, investigating security-compromise reports under the Telecommunications Security Act (TSA), monitoring AI standards development, and monitoring AI's impact on telecom markets. None of these constitute binding AI-specific telecom rules — this is a monitoring and engagement posture, not a rule-making one.

### Consistent with the UK's cross-sector regulatory stance
This mirrors the identical "technology-neutral" posture already documented for UK financial-sector regulators (FCA/PRA, per the earlier BFSI intake) and UK insurance regulators (per [[03_Domains/Insurance_AI_Governance]]) — the UK's consistent regulatory philosophy across at least three sectors examined in this corpus (finance, insurance, telecom) is to rely on existing frameworks and monitoring rather than issue sector-specific AI rules.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For a new Telecom_AI_Governance domain — the minimal-intervention comparator pole
Set against TRAI's more developed posture (a formal 2023 Recommendations document proposing a risk-tiered cross-sector framework, now operationalised into a binding UCC/spam AI direction — see [[07_Institutions/Corpus/TRAI/TRAI_2]] and [[07_Institutions/Corpus/TRAI/TRAI_1]]), Ofcom's stance is comparatively minimal: no proposed risk-tiering, no binding telecom-specific AI rule, monitoring only. This makes UK telecom the *least* interventionist comparator in this domain's spread, a useful contrast to TRAI's more structured (if not fully implemented) approach.

### A now-repeated finding across three UK sectoral regulators
This is the **third UK regulator** in this corpus (after FCA/PRA for finance, PRA for insurance) found to explicitly favour a technology-neutral, non-rule-making AI posture — strong enough repetition to describe as a general UK regulatory-philosophy finding rather than a sector-specific coincidence, worth stating explicitly if a cross-sector UK comparator note is ever built.
