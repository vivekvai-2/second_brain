## Substantive Content

### Core requirement — a written "AIS Program"
Insurers must develop, implement, and maintain a written AI System ("AIS") Program for responsible AI use, addressing governance, risk management, and internal audit functions across underwriting, rating, claims, fraud detection, and marketing. The bulletin explicitly frames compliance obligations around existing unfair-trade-practice and unfair-discrimination law — it does not create new substantive standards so much as require insurers to demonstrate how AI use complies with law that already applies to human decision-making.

### Decentralised adoption model — a distinct comparative data point
Unlike a single binding national instrument, the NAIC Model Bulletin only takes effect where an individual state insurance commissioner adopts it. As of the most recent count, roughly half of US states (plus DC) have done so, with meaningful cross-state variation in exact adoption language. **Enforcement activity under adopted bulletins does not yet appear significant** as of the most recent secondary legal analysis (19 May 2025) — i.e., even where adopted, the instrument has generated few visible enforcement actions to date.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For a new Insurance_AI_Governance domain — comparative benchmark
This is the most substantively developed of the three international insurance-AI comparators (NAIC/EIOPA/IAIS) in terms of concrete operational requirements (a written AIS Program), but its state-by-state adoption structure means its *actual* binding reach in the US is fragmented and partial — a useful comparator for evaluating what IRDAI's WG-AI recommendations (once issued, ~September 2026) might look like if implemented via advisory template rather than binding national regulation.

### fsQCA relevance
The US decentralised-adoption model is a genuinely distinct Regulatory Logic configuration from the EU's harmonised (EIOPA-interprets-existing-law) approach and IAIS's global-standard-but-no-new-rules posture — three different institutional design choices for achieving similar substantive goals (fair, transparent, accountable AI use in insurance), useful as a three-point comparative spread if insurance is ever added to the fsQCA case set.
