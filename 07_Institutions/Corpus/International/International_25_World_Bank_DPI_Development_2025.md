## Substantive Content

### Core framing
Establishes "Legal and Regulatory Framework" as a core DPI implementation lesson, requiring data protection, privacy, and cybersecurity frameworks to be built **in parallel with** DPI rollout rather than retrofitted afterward — directly relevant to the domain's existing "records vs rules asymmetry" argument, since it implies governance design should be concurrent with infrastructure design, which the Indian cases (Aadhaar, PM-KISAN, Account Aggregator, PM-JAY) largely did not do.

### Comparative material folded in (not separately noded — below the evidence-reuse threshold as standalone items)
- **OECD DPI report (Dec 2024):** explicitly names India's IndiaStack as an example DPI strategic framework; recommends privacy-by-design and ethical standards as governance components.
- **T20 Brazil Task Force 5 brief (2024):** proposes commons-governance principles for DPI — transparency, open-source mandates, independent dispute resolution, human override of automated systems — directly framing DPI governance as an institutional-design problem, consistent with this domain's IS-theoretic framing.
- **IIM Bangalore CDPG monograph:** documents MOSIP as an Aadhaar-derived DPI stack adopted by 26 countries, discussed alongside AI-DPI convergence themes; publication date not independently confirmed — flag for verification.
- **Indian Economic Service working paper (13 Feb 2025):** discusses government's institutional role in designing, developing, and managing DPI systems — a domestic-source companion to the World Bank's global framing.
- **Vidhi Centre for Legal Policy N.U.D.G.E. Report** (7 June 2023) — "Nurturing a User-Driven Governance Entity for the Account Aggregator Ecosystem" — proposes a dedicated governance entity for the AA ecosystem specifically; directly relevant to this domain's Account Aggregator case.

### Confirmed gap
**No academic paper applying fsQCA methodology to DPI governance was located.** General institutional-design DPI literature exists (T20 Brazil, IES, World Bank), but fsQCA-specific application to DPI governance appears to be an open research niche as of the search date — a genuine white space for original PRIS contribution combining P2's DPI corpus with fsQCA's comparative-configurational method.

**No MOSIP-self-published AI-governance document was located** — MOSIP appears only as a subject within third-party publications (World Bank, IIM-B), not as the issuing body of its own AI-governance text.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For P2 (DPI Governance) — Records_Rules_Asymmetry corroboration
The World Bank's own "build legal/regulatory framework in parallel with rollout" lesson is an external, non-PRIS-authored validation of the domain's central finding: India's DPI cases were *not* built this way (governance rules concentrated on system performance, thin on citizen-facing fallback), which the domain's empirical corpus already documents. This gives the Records_Rules_Asymmetry argument an independent comparative-best-practice benchmark to cite.

### Confirmed original-contribution opportunity
The fsQCA-DPI methodological gap is directly actionable: P2's existing 12-document, 248-statement Institutional Grammar corpus could be extended into a small-N fsQCA design (using T20 Brazil's commons-governance criteria and the World Bank's legal-framework-parallelism lesson as candidate conditions) — a defensible, citable original contribution given the confirmed absence of prior fsQCA-DPI work.
