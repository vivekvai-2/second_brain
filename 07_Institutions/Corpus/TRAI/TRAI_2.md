## S2. Governance Context

TRAI_2 is the foundational policy document for AI governance in India's telecom sector. It is significant for several reasons beyond its sector-specific scope:

**Cross-sector framing**: TRAI_2 explicitly argues that a common framework must apply across **all sectors**, not telecom in isolation — making it one of the early Indian institutional voices calling for unified national AI governance (ahead of NCAIC_1 by approximately two years). This cross-sector orientation positions TRAI not merely as a telecom regulator but as a governance actor with views on the national AI framework architecture.

**International survey**: TRAI conducted a comparative survey of AI governance approaches across the EU, US (NIST), Singapore, and UK before drafting its recommendations — making TRAI_2 one of the few Indian regulatory documents with explicit comparative policy analysis embedded in its reasoning.

**Risk-based framework**: TRAI's core recommendation is a risk-based regulatory framework where **high-risk AI use cases** that directly impact humans attract legally binding obligations — directly anticipating the EU AI Act's risk-tier approach (which India has since broadly mirrored in MeitY guidance).

## S3. Key Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Adopt a national risk-based AI regulatory framework (cross-sector) | Cross-sector framing — TRAI urges Government of India to act beyond telecom |
| R2 | High-risk AI use cases (direct human impact) → legally binding obligations | Risk-tier approach; aligns with EU AI Act architecture |
| R3 | Establish a multi-stakeholder advisory body to guide responsible AI principles | Anticipates NCAIC concept by ~2 years |
| R4 | Develop a national data availability framework for AI training | Data governance as AI prerequisite |
| R5 | Responsible AI principles: privacy, bias mitigation, security, accountability, transparency | Eight principles — parallels OECD AI Principles |
| R6 | Regulatory sandboxes and testing frameworks for telecom AI applications | Innovation-governance balance |
| R7 | Cross-sector alignment: BFSI institutions using telecom AI services must align governance obligations | BFSI-telecom interdependency explicitly flagged |

### Key Provisions (verbatim)

> "For ensuring development of responsible Artificial Intelligence (AI) in India, there is an urgent need to adopt a regulatory framework by the Government that should be applicable across sectors. The regulatory framework should ensure that specific AI use cases are regulated on a risk-based framework where high risk use cases that directly impact humans are regulated through legally binding obligations." — Chapter 2, Analysis

> "Low quality data, data biases, data security, data privacy, inaccurate or biased algorithm, and unethical use of AI were some of the risks discussed in the CP." — Emerging risks from AI/ML

> "In view of the impact of AI in all sectors, the framework which has to be suggested for telecom cannot be treated in isolation and hence a common framework covering all the sectors is being proposed." — Multi-sector approach

### TRAI_2 → TRAI_1 Evolution (Policy → Operational)

| Dimension | TRAI_2 (July 2023) | TRAI_1 (Feb 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument type | Recommendations | Direction (mandatory) |
| Scope | Cross-sector AI governance (aspirational) | Telecom UCC enforcement (specific) |
| AI mandate | Proposed — risk-based framework | Imposed — mandatory AI/ML deployment |
| Addressees | Government of India | All Access Service Providers |
| Cross-sector coordination | Called for | Absent — silo implementation |

This TRAI_2 → TRAI_1 evolution reveals a pattern: TRAI's cross-sector governance vision (TRAI_2) was not adopted by Government; TRAI instead implemented what it could within its own jurisdiction (TRAI_1) — a **Governance_Debt** outcome: the broader coordination mechanism was recommended but not built.
