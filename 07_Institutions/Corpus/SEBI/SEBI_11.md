## Substantive Content

### Context and Motivation (§2)

**Evolution from 2019 reporting to 2024 accountability:**
- 2019 circulars established reporting/inventory mechanism
- Now proposing to move to responsibility assignment — structural shift from observation to accountability

**AI/ML Use Pattern (from 2019 reporting data, §2.4):**

| Regulated Entity | AI/ML Applications |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Stock Brokers & Depository Participants | Surveillance, order execution, KYC, anti-money laundering |
| MIIs (Stock Exchanges, Depositories, Clearing Corps) | Surveillance, risk management, data analysis, behaviour analysis, generative AI for reviewing documents/orders/reports, cyber security, cyber infrastructure, chatbots |
| Mutual Funds | Surveillance, customer service (email, WhatsApp, chatbots), cyber security, customer segmentation |

**Key observation (§2.5):** AI/ML tools play an increasingly important role in core operations (surveillance, risk management, KYC, cyber security) AND in operations involving investor interface (order execution, customer segmentation)

**Dual concern:**
1. Core operations (systemic risk dimension): Surveillance, risk management, cyber security
2. Investor interface (investor protection dimension): Order execution, customer segmentation, advisory services

### Justification for Regulatory Intervention (§2.7)
"While recognizing the need for market intermediaries to embrace latest technologies, it becomes equally important for SEBI to ensure the protection of investors. Hence, a need is felt to assign responsibility to those persons regulated by SEBI that use of AI/ML tools in the conduct of their securities market related activities or while servicing their clients. Mandating such responsibilities is expected to bring in more seriousness to such users while deploying AI/ML tools and at the same time ensure protection of investors."

**Regulatory logic:** Responsibility assignment → seriousness in deployment → investor protection

### Proposed Amendments (§4)

**Amendment scope:** SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations 2008, SECC Regulations 2018, DP Regulations 2018

**Proposed duty:** Impose responsibility on persons regulated by SEBI who use AI/ML tools (designed by them or procured from third-party technology service providers) to conduct securities market-related activities or service clients.

**Third-party AI governance:** Explicitly extends to AI tools "procured from third-party technology service providers" — addresses the AI vendor accountability gap

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### Critical Regulatory Development (Post-Corpus)

SEBI_11 represents the most significant AI governance development in Indian BFSI after the 2019 circulars:
- Shift from **inventory/reporting** → **responsibility assignment** = qualitative governance upgrade
- Inclusion of third-party AI tools explicitly = closes the vendor accountability gap
- Amendment to core securities regulations (not circular) = higher regulatory durability and enforcement weight

**Timeline significance:**
- 2019: SEBI reports AI/ML landscape
- 2024: SEBI proposes to assign responsibility for AI use
- 5-year gap between inventory and accountability = evidence of Governance Debt

### P3 Discussion Section Evidence

SEBI_11 should be cited in the P3 paper's discussion section (not findings) to:
1. Demonstrate that SEBI's own regulatory evolution vindicates P3's argument that the 2019 framework was inadequate
2. Show that the parallelism gap (SEBI explicit AI governance, RBI technology-neutral) is being addressed unilaterally by SEBI without cross-regulator coordination
3. Flag that no equivalent RBI "responsibility assignment" for AI use by banks has been proposed — gap persists

### Governance Architecture Evolution (AI Governance Wiki)

| Stage | Year | SEBI Instrument | Governance Logic |
|-------|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Inventory | 2019 | SEBI_9, SEBI_10 | Know what AI exists in markets |
| Accountability | 2024–25 | SEBI_11 | Assign responsibility for AI use |
| [Anticipated] | ? | Future circular | Substantive AI governance requirements |

### Third-Party AI Governance Gap

SEBI_11's explicit extension to third-party AI tools is analytically significant:
- SEBI is the first Indian securities regulator to directly address AI vendor accountability
- RBI's IT outsourcing direction (RBI_2) governs IT outsourcing generally but does not specifically address AI responsibility
- Creates an emerging parallelism: SEBI assigns AI responsibility to users of third-party AI; RBI governs IT outsourcing generically

### Legal/Constitutional Dimension (P4)

Amendment to SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, SECC Regulations, and DP Regulations creates:
- Positive legal duty to take responsibility for AI tools used in securities market activities
- Applies whether AI is internally developed or externally procured
- Potential liability exposure for intermediaries using defective third-party AI → creates doctrinal questions about AI vendor liability in Indian securities law
