## Substantive Content

### Five guiding principles (per secondary summary)
Model governance; mandatory disclosure; robust testing/monitoring; fairness/bias mitigation; and data security — with **differentiated regulatory intensity** for client-facing AI models (higher scrutiny) versus internal-use models such as surveillance systems (lighter touch).

### Significance of the differentiated-intensity design
This is the first instrument in the SEBI corpus to propose risk-tiering by *use case* (client-facing vs. internal) rather than treating all AI/ML deployment uniformly — a structural shift from the 2019 circulars' blanket quarterly-reporting requirement (SEBI_9/10/12) toward something closer to the EU AI Act's risk-based architecture, adapted to a securities-market context.

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### For P3 (BFSI JEIM) and Regulatory_Parallelism
If finalised, this consultation paper would move SEBI from the "reporting parallelism" pattern already documented (SEBI_9/10/12 alongside RBI_6, MeitY_4's Rule 13(3)) toward a genuine risk-tiered AI governance instrument — worth monitoring as a potential inflection point in the cross-regulator parallelism finding: SEBI may be the first Indian financial regulator to move beyond reporting-only AI obligations toward substantive governance principles, ahead of RBI's FREE-AI (still recommendations-stage per [[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_FREE_AI_2025]]) and IRDAI's Working Group (still study-stage per [[07_Institutions/Corpus/IRDAI/IRDAI_2_Working_Group_AI_Governance_2026]]).
