## Substantive Content

### Background and Scale
Cited user figures (2021): WhatsApp 53 crore, YouTube 44.8 crore, Facebook 41 crore, Instagram 21 crore, Twitter 1.75 crore. Framing quote: "India is the world's largest open Internet society and the Government welcomes social media companies to operate in India, do business and also earn profits. However, they will have to be accountable to the Constitution and laws of India."

### Rationale — Named Precipitating Events
- Supreme Court suo-moto writ petition (the "Prajjawala" case), order dated **11/12/2018**, prompting the Government to consider guidelines against child sexual abuse material.
- Supreme Court order, **24/09/2019**, directing MeitY to provide a timeline for notifying new rules.
- Rajya Sabha Calling Attention Motion on social media misuse/fake news, **26/07/2018**.
- Rajya Sabha ad-hoc committee report, laid **03/02/2020**, recommending enablement of first-originator identification for pornographic content.

### Consultation Record
Draft Rules published for comment **24 December 2018**; MeitY received **171 comments** and **80 counter-comments**.

### Part II — Intermediary Due Diligence (MeitY)
**Baseline obligations (all intermediaries):**
- Establish a grievance redressal mechanism; appoint a Grievance Officer (name/contact published); acknowledge complaints within **24 hours**, resolve within **15 days**.
- Remove/disable access within **24 hours** to content exposing private areas, nudity/sexual acts, or impersonation, upon complaint.

**Two-tier classification:** "social media intermediaries" vs. "**significant** social media intermediaries" (SSMIs), distinguished by user-count threshold.

**Additional SSMI due diligence (effective 3 months after publication of the Rules):**
- Appoint a **Chief Compliance Officer** (India-resident) responsible for Act/Rules compliance.
- Appoint a **Nodal Contact Person** (India-resident) for 24×7 law-enforcement coordination.
- Appoint a **Resident Grievance Officer** performing the grievance-mechanism functions.
- Publish a **monthly compliance report** (complaints received, action taken).
- **First-originator traceability**: messaging-primary SSMIs must enable identification of the first originator of information, but only for offences punishable with **≥5 years' imprisonment**; the intermediary is **not required to disclose message content** to identify the originator (a traceability-without-content-disclosure design).
- Maintain a **physical contact address in India**, published on website/app.
- Offer users a **voluntary account-verification** mechanism.
- **Prior intimation + dispute opportunity** before proactively removing user content.
- **"Actual knowledge" takedown standard**: upon court order or notification by an appropriate government agency, must not host/publish legally prohibited information.

### Part III — Digital Media Ethics Code (MIB)
Applies to online news publishers and OTT/curated-content platforms.
- **Content classification taxonomy:** U (Universal), U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+, A (Adult).
- Parental locks required for U/A 13+ and above; reliable age-verification for "A"-rated content.
- Classification rating + content descriptor must be displayed prominently at the start of every programme.
- News publishers must observe Press Council of India journalistic-conduct norms and the Programme Code under the Cable Television Networks Regulation Act.
- **Three-tier grievance redressal architecture:**
  - **Level I — Publisher self-regulation:** India-based Grievance Redressal Officer; decision within **15 days**.
  - **Level II — Self-regulatory body:** headed by a retired Supreme Court/High Court judge or independent eminent person; **≤6 members**; must register with MIB.
  - **Level III — Oversight mechanism:** government-level oversight (Inter-Departmental Committee, per MeitY_5's description of the unchanged Part III architecture).

## Analytical Significance for PRIS Research

### Parent-instrument role for existing corpus nodes
This node retroactively anchors two already-ingested downstream instruments: MeitY_5's 2025 draft SGI/deepfake-labelling amendment inserts new obligations into **Part II** specifically (not Part III, despite synthetic media typically appearing as published/curated content) — confirming these Rules' Part II/Part III split is being used as the insertion architecture for AI-specific obligations as they emerge, rather than triggering a new standalone AI-content instrument. MeitY_6's NCII takedown SOP is explicitly scoped as *implementing* Rule 3(2)(b) of this instrument and explicitly disclaims being part of the Rules themselves — a clean instrument/implementation-guidance separation worth noting as a general MeitY drafting pattern.

### Traceability-without-disclosure as a design precedent
The first-originator obligation (identify without disclosing content, offence-severity-gated at ≥5 years' imprisonment) is a notable proportionality/privacy-preserving design choice directly relevant to any AI-driven content-traceability or algorithmic-accountability discussion — the Rules deliberately narrow a broad traceability demand to a severity-gated, content-blind mechanism. Useful comparator for evaluating whether newer instruments (e.g., DPDP Rules' Significant Data Fiduciary algorithmic due-diligence, MeitY_4 Rule 13(3)) adopt similarly narrow, proportionate designs or broader ones.

### Institutional_Coherence — dual-ministry administration
The Part II/Part III split creates a dual-ministry administration structure (MeitY for intermediary due diligence; MIB for publisher/OTT content ethics) within a single instrument — an intentional coherence-by-design choice (one Rules instrument, two named administering ministries with clearly demarcated scope) contrasted with the corpus's more common pattern of entirely separate instruments per regulator (RBI/SEBI/MeitY parallelism). Worth a comparative note alongside International_21's seven-ministry single-instrument China design.

### Governance_Debt — three-tier + two-tier layered grievance architecture
Entities operating both as an SSMI and a Part III publisher (e.g., a social-media-integrated news platform) face **two separate grievance-redressal obligations simultaneously**: the SSMI Resident Grievance Officer mechanism (Part II) and the three-tier Level I/II/III mechanism (Part III) — a within-instrument (not merely cross-regulator) governance-debt instance, since both obligations originate from the same Rules but attach to different functional roles of the same entity.
