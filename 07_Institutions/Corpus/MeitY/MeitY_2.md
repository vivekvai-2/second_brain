## Substantive Content

### Structure — four parts
- **Part 1**: Seven sutras grounding India's AI governance philosophy (technology-agnostic, cross-sectoral).
- **Part 2**: Recommendations across **six pillars** within **three domains** — enablement (infrastructure, capacity building), regulation (policy & regulation, risk mitigation), and oversight (accountability, institutions). [⚠ VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING — exact names of the six pillars were not captured in extraction; do not cite specific pillar names without confirming against primary text.]
- **Part 3**: Action plan with short/medium/long-term steps, operationalised via a "whole of government" approach.
- **Part 4**: Practical implementation guidelines for industry actors and regulators.

### The Seven Sutras — Part 1 (key finding)
Trust, People First, Innovation over Restraint, Fairness & Equity, Accountability, Understandable by Design, and Safety/Resilience/Sustainability.

**Critical cross-reference**: the document explicitly states these seven guiding principles/sutras "have been adapted from the RBI's FREE-AI Committee report" and "adapted for application across sectors and aligned with national priorities." This is a **direct, named instance of inter-regulator policy diffusion** — MeitY's cross-sectoral framework is built on a sectoral (RBI/BFSI) framework's normative architecture.

### Institutional architecture (Part 3 / implementation framework)
A "whole of government" model in which:
- **AI Governance Group (AIGG)** — high-level coordinating body, supported by the **Technology & Policy Expert Committee (TPEC)**, for strategic oversight.
- **AI Safety Institute (AISI)** — provides technical expertise on trust/safety issues, conducts research, develops draft standards, and performs safety validation.
- **Sector regulators** (RBI, SEBI, TRAI, CCI, etc.) **continue to exercise enforcement powers** — this instrument does NOT centralise enforcement.
- Government agencies mapped to roles: MeitY, MHA, MEA, DoT, and others, plus sectoral regulators.

### Safe and Trusted AI pillar (foundational)
Described as the foundational layer underpinning all IndiaAI Mission pillars (infrastructure, capacity-building, etc.) — "without robust trust and safety measures... efforts in infrastructure, capacity building [are undermined]." AISI is positioned as providing the "agile and flexible policy architecture" with technical backing.

### Risk framing
Explicitly identifies deepfakes, misinformation, algorithmic bias, and threats to national security as the risk categories the framework seeks to balance against innovation.

This is evidence for a coordination mechanism (shared normative architecture via "sutras") operating alongside continued regulatory parallelism in enforcement (sector regulators retain independent enforcement powers).
