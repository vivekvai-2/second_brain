# RBI

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-06-12

---

## Core Synthesis

The Reserve Bank of India is the most consequential regulatory actor in the PRIS research portfolio — the issuer of the corpus's highest-quality AI governance instrument (FREE-AI), the primary empirical actor in P3's coordination-pair analysis, and the institutional logic anchor for the fragmentation argument in P4. RBI's AI governance posture is characterised by high architectural ambition (FREE-AI's seven sutras, six pillars, eight-category risk taxonomy) combined with sector-confinement: its writ runs to banking, NBFCs, and payment systems, but stops at the boundary with SEBI's capital markets jurisdiction — the structural source of Regulatory Parallelism in the P3 corpus.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Statutory central bank and financial sector regulator
- **Established:** 1935; nationalised 1949
- **Governing statute:** Reserve Bank of India Act 1934; Banking Regulation Act 1949
- **Primary mandate:** Monetary stability, currency issuance, banking supervision, payment systems oversight
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Banks, NBFCs, payment system operators, All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)
- **Enforcement mechanism:** Supervisory audits, license conditions, prompt corrective action (PCA) framework, moral suasion, outsourcing approval gates

## Institutional Logic (P3/P4 Analytical Relevance)

RBI's governing logic is **prudential risk management for the lending economy** — its primary concern is financial stability, credit risk, and systemic resilience. This produces an AI governance orientation focused on model risk, explainability in credit decisions, and third-party (outsourcing) risk. This logic is structurally different from SEBI's conduct/market-integrity orientation — and that difference in institutional logic is why the two regulators produce parallel AI governance obligations without cross-referencing each other. RBI does not coordinate with SEBI on AI governance because their mandates frame AI governance differently: for RBI, AI is a model risk and outsourcing problem; for SEBI, it is an accountability and market-conduct problem.
