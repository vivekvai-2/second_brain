# Sahamati — Self-Regulatory Organisation, Account Aggregator Ecosystem

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Created:** 2026-08-02 (DEPA sourcing pass)

---

## Institutional Identity

Sahamati began as a voluntary industry alliance ("collective") of participants in India's Account Aggregator ecosystem — AAs, Financial Information Providers, and Financial Information Users. On **5 June 2026** the Reserve Bank of India formally recognised it as the **Self-Regulatory Organisation for the Account Aggregator ecosystem**, converting it from a private membership body into an RBI-recognised standard-setter with delegated authority.

Recognised SRO mandate: developing operational and technical standards, facilitating dispute resolution, supporting interoperability, strengthening ecosystem discipline, and enabling coordinated engagement across participants.

## Why This Institution Matters to PRIS

**It is a novel institutional form in Indian data governance, and it has no AI-sector counterpart.** India has an RBI-recognised SRO setting binding-in-effect standards and running a dispute-resolution tier for *data sharing*. There is no equivalent body for AI in any Indian sector — not in BFSI, not cross-sectorally. For [[Governance_Capacity]] this is direct evidence that the Indian state can and does delegate governance authority to industry bodies under regulatory recognition when it chooses to; for [[Governance_Debt]] it sharpens the AI gap by removing the institutional-design excuse.

**It also complicates the standard-setting picture.** ReBIT already holds delegated technical-standard authority for the AA ecosystem under Master Direction para 9.2 (see [[07_Institutions/Corpus/RBI/RBI_12_ReBIT_AA_API_Specifications_2023]]). Sahamati's SRO mandate now also covers "operational and technical standards." **The division of labour between ReBIT and Sahamati is not documented in any located primary source.** This is an open question with real analytical value: two delegated standard-setters under one regulator, in one ecosystem, is either coordination or duplication, and which it is bears directly on [[Institutional_Coherence]].

## Status Boundary — read before citing

Sahamati's instruments split at 5 June 2026:

- **Pre-recognition** documents (Participation Terms, dispute-resolution architecture, ecosystem guidelines) were **purely voluntary industry self-regulation** with contractual force between members only
- **Post-recognition** instruments carry RBI-recognised SRO authority

Sahamati's own ODR and arbitration tiers remain subordinate to statutory ombudsman schemes and activate only where the regulator-administered route "is not available, accessible or resolution by which is unsatisfactory." Do not characterise Sahamati's grievance mechanism as statutory.
