# IRDAI

**Type:** Institution **Status:** Active **Last Updated:** 2026-07-14

---

## Core Synthesis

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India is the insurance-sector complement to RBI and SEBI in the PRIS Regulatory Parallelism diagnosis. IRDAI's AI governance posture as of Batch 15 continuation is predominantly indirect — the Maintenance and Sharing of Information Regulations, 2025 (IRDAI_1) is a data-governance/information-maintenance framework with **no explicit AI/ML provisions**, positioning IRDAI as a *fourth silo* in India's fragmented AI-governance architecture (RBI + SEBI + MeitY + IRDAI) despite substantive AI adoption in underwriting, claims, and fraud detection across the insurance sector. IRDAI's absence from AI-specific rulemaking, combined with its statutory mandate over an increasingly AI-intensive sector, generates the strongest "Regulatory Parallelism through omission" evidence in the P3 corpus.

## Institutional Profile

- **Type:** Statutory insurance regulator
- **Established:** 1999 (statutory authority under IRDA Act, 1999)
- **Governing statute:** Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act 1999; Insurance Act 1938 (sections including S.20 cited as basis for public-interest information sharing)
- **Primary mandate:** Insurance sector regulation, policyholder protection, market development
- **AI-relevant jurisdiction:** Insurance companies (life, general, health, reinsurers), brokers, agents, third-party administrators, insurtech intermediaries; Insurtech Regulatory Sandbox (IRS) operational since 2019
- **Enforcement mechanism:** Registration/renewal control, monetary penalties, prohibition orders, licence suspension/cancellation

## Institutional Logic (P3/P4 Analytical Relevance)

IRDAI's governing logic is **policyholder protection and market conduct in an information-asymmetric market** — the classical insurance-regulator orientation focused on solvency, claims-handling fairness, and mis-selling prevention. In contrast to RBI's prudential-risk orientation and SEBI's market-conduct orientation, IRDAI's AI governance would logically emphasise underwriting fairness (non-discrimination), claims decisioning transparency, and fraud detection accountability. As of July 2026, no such AI-specific instrument has been issued. This produces the P3/P4 finding that India's fourth-silo AI governance gap is at IRDAI: the insurance sector is a high-AI-adoption context with the weakest sectoral AI-governance overlay. The vendor and data-localisation provisions in IRDAI_1 create partial data-governance coverage but do not address AI-specific risks (model bias in underwriting, algorithmic claims-denial, fraud-detection false positives).
