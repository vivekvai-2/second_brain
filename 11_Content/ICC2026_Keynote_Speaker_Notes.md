# ICC'2026 Keynote — Speaker Notes

**Title:** Techno-Legal Futures: Where AI Governance Meets Career Strategy
**Speaker:** Col. Vivek Vaidyanathan
**Event:** ICC'2026, TNDALU, Chennai · 2–3 September 2026
**Duration:** 40 minutes (38 min content + 2 min transition buffer)

---

## Slide 1 — Title (1 min)

Welcome the audience. Acknowledge the Vice-Chancellor, Dean, and organising committee. Frame the address: "This keynote covers two things you care about — how AI is changing legal work, and where the jobs are."

---

## Slide 2 — The Starting Point: SC Hallucination Case (2 min)

**Open with the judgment.** Read the quote slowly:

> "Decisions built on AI-generated hallucinations are no decision in the eyes of the law."
> — *SL Infra v. Essel Infraprojects Ltd.*, Supreme Court of India, 2 July 2026

Walk through the broken citation chain: AI generated a fake citation → lawyer filed without verifying → court relied on the precedent → precedent did not exist → decision vacated.

**Pause.** Then:

"This ruling did not ban AI from legal practice. It made governance the price of admission."

**Key point:** The verification obligation now sits with the professional. This is the thesis of the entire keynote.

---

## Slide 3 — Two Pyramids: What Changed (2 min)

**Left pyramid (greyed out):** The old entry-level work — research, summarisation, DD checklists, template drafting. AI compresses this layer.

**Right pyramid (coloured):** The new entry-level work — verification, exception identification, risk classification, regulatory interpretation, evidence design.

"AI did not eliminate the junior role. It changed what the junior role produces. The base of the new pyramid is wider because the verification burden is larger, not smaller."

---

## Slide 4 — The Responsibility Chain (2 min)

Walk left to right through five steps:

1. **AI Produces** — output, prediction, recommendation
2. **Law Permits** — is processing lawful?
3. **Human Verifies** — is the output correct?
4. **Institution Authorises** — who approved deployment?
5. **Evidence Defends** — can you prove it to a regulator?

"Steps 2 through 5 require legal training. Engineers build step 1. You govern steps 2 through 5. This chain is where the new legal work sits."

---

## Slide 5 — Financial-Services AI Example (2 min)

Concrete walkthrough: A financial-services platform offering banking, investment and insurance products through an AI assistant.

That one system simultaneously sits under:
- **DPDP** — consent obligations for personal data processing
- **CERT-In** — 6-hour incident reporting for cyber breaches
- **Sector Regulators** (RBI/SEBI/IRDAI) — outsourcing governance, algorithmic audit
- **Contracts** — vendor liability with the model provider
- **Professional Duty** — the SC's verification obligation
- **Consumer Law** — fairness, transparency, redressal

"Each regime generates compliance obligations. Each obligation is a job."

---

## Slide 6 — One System, Six Governance Regimes (1 min)

Reinforce with the radial map. Introduce the Regulatory Parallelism concept briefly:

"This is the Regulatory Parallelism problem from my doctoral research. Across 18 binding instruments, I found 23 instances where regulators impose functionally equivalent mandates independently — without referencing each other. Each overlap generates compliance work."

**Data if asked:** 450 requirement units from 492 pages; 91 AI-relevant units; 48 functionally parallel across RBI-SEBI corpus.

Do not dwell — the point is made. Move to the timeline.

---

## Slide 7 — India Implementation Timeline (2 min)

Walk the timeline left to right:

- **Nov 2025:** DPDP Rules notified — this is live law, not a proposal
- **Jun 2026:** SC Draft AI Regulations — still in consultation, not binding, but signals judicial direction
- **Jul 2026:** SC hallucination judgment — already binding
- **Nov 2026:** DPDP Phase 2 — Consent Managers regime commences
- **May 2027:** DPDP Phase 3 — full enforcement, penalties up to ₹250 crore per violation category

"That is less than 12 months from today. Every organisation processing personal data in India faces full enforcement. Every compliance obligation is a governance job."

**Caveat (important):** Always frame the SC draft regulations as "draft, in consultation" — not binding or adopted.

---

## Slide 8 — Three Global Signals (2 min)

Three numbers. No elaboration needed. Read each card:

1. **92%** of legal professionals use at least one AI tool in their daily workflow *(Wolters Kluwer, March 2026)*
   - **Precise wording matters:** "at least one AI tool in their daily workflow" — do not simplify to "all legal professionals use AI daily"
2. **61%** of corporate legal departments in active AI deployment; 28% of legal work expected automatable in 2–3 years *(Deloitte, July 2026)*
3. **1M+** professional users across 107 countries on Thomson Reuters CoCounsel — rebuilt as "fiduciary-grade AI" *(Thomson Reuters, June 2026)*

**Optional reference:** A&O Shearman entered a revenue-sharing arrangement with Harvey — a law firm becoming an AI product company, not just a consumer.

Close: "This is not a future prediction. This is the profession you are entering."

---

## Slide 9 — Employment Ecosystem Overview (1 min)

"Most law students think of employment as law firms. The reality is three territories, not one."

- **Territory A:** Legal Practice — law firms and corporate legal departments
- **Territory B:** Technology & Enterprise — GCCs, legal tech, consulting
- **Territory C:** Public Institutions — courts, regulators, government

"All three now require AI governance capability. The legal employment market is no longer identical to the law-firm market."

Transition: "Let me walk you through each."

---

## Slide 10 — Territory A: Legal Practice (3 min)

**Law Firms:**
- Khaitan: established an AI Governance Committee, built ask.KAI
- Trilegal: AI is "well beyond pilot stage"
- Roles: AI-assisted research, TMT practice, data protection, legal operations, knowledge management
- **Hiring signal:** 86% of large firms increasing associate headcount — but only 35% growing first-year classes
- Translation: "They want fewer juniors who can do more, faster"

**Corporate Legal:**
- Every large organisation now needs someone to draft AI-use policies, run DPIAs, manage consent, evaluate AI vendor contracts
- Deloitte: 61% in active deployment
- "These are not law-firm jobs — they are in-house roles in banks, IT companies, manufacturing firms"

---

## Slide 11 — Territory B: Enterprise (3 min)

**GCCs — The Hidden Market:**
- ~2,100+ GCCs in India, ~2.36 million employees (FY2026)
- Nearly two-thirds of new recruitment associated with AI-related capability requirements
- Roles: global contracting, privacy operations, compliance, e-discovery, AI vendor governance
- **Chennai is a major GCC hub — these employers are in your city**

**Legal Tech & RegTech:**
- SpotDraft ($54M Series B), CaseMine, Legistify
- Market: $38B → $70–78B by 2031; AI sub-segment 27% CAGR
- "These companies need domain specialists who understand law well enough to design, test, and evaluate legal AI products. Not coding — domain expertise."

**Consulting & Assurance:**
- Deloitte and BCG now among top recruiters at NLUs
- DPDP Phase 3 enforcement (May 2027) will create a compliance consulting surge
- Roles: control frameworks, risk assessments, DPIAs, audit programmes, incident playbooks

---

## Slide 12 — Territory C: Public Institutions (2 min)

Brief — three cards:
- **Courts:** e-Courts Phase III (₹7,210 crore budget). Draft SC Regulations propose an AI Committee and Secretariat in every High Court.
- **Regulators:** Data Protection Board staffing up for Phase 3. MeitY AIGG, AISI, TPEC.
- **Government:** DPI governance (Aadhaar, UPI, Account Aggregator, ONDC). Policy analysis and regulatory drafting.

"Slower career path — but sovereign-critical work with long-term institutional impact."

---

## Slide 13 — Capability Ladder (3 min)

Walk from the bottom up:

**Tier 1 — Legal Foundation** (by graduation):
"Statutory interpretation, case analysis, legal writing. This does not disappear. It becomes the verification layer that makes you valuable in an AI-assisted workflow."

**Tier 2 — AI + Data Fluency** (Year 4–5 or first 6 months of employment):
"Not learning to code. Learning to work with AI outputs critically — prompt engineering for legal tasks, hallucination detection, data literacy. The SC's zero-tolerance ruling makes this a professional survival skill."

**Tier 3 — Governance Craft** (Years 2–5, comparative advantage zone):
"DPIA design, AI-use policy drafting, consent architecture, vendor risk assessment, regulatory mapping, control framework design. Legal education gives graduates a comparative advantage in interpreting authority, obligation, liability and evidence. That advantage becomes commercially valuable when combined with technological and organisational understanding."

**Tier 4 — Governance Leadership** (Years 5+):
"CPO, AI Ethics Lead, Regulatory Strategy, Board Advisory. Where law graduates lead, not assist."

Bottom line: "AI replaces → AI amplifies → AI cannot do."

---

## Slide 14 — Law-to-Control Translation (2 min)

Walk through four steps with the DPDP consent example:

1. **Regulatory Requirement:** DPDP — obtain informed consent
2. **Legal Obligation:** Consent must be granular, revocable
3. **Governance Control:** Consent management platform + audit log
4. **Auditable Evidence:** Timestamped record of consent + revocation

"This four-step translation — from requirement to evidence — is the work that AI governance professionals do every day."

---

## Slide 15 — Five Ps (1.5 min)

Walk through each P:

1. **Purpose** — Why does this AI system exist? → Job: AI-use policy design
2. **People** — Who is accountable when it fails? → Job: Accountability mapping
3. **Proof** — Can you show evidence to a regulator? → Job: DPIA + audit trail design
4. **Process** — What happens when it goes wrong? → Job: Incident response playbooks
5. **Partner** — Who built it? What controls exist? → Job: Vendor risk assessment

"If you can answer these five questions for any AI system in any organisation, you are employable."

---

## Slide 16 — Green–Amber–Red Triage (1.5 min)

- **Green:** All Five Ps answered, documented, reviewed → Deploy
- **Amber:** Gaps in 1–2 Ps, remediation plan exists → Deploy with conditions
- **Red:** 3+ Ps unanswered, no remediation plan → Stop deployment

"Learn to use this classification and you speak the language of every CISO, DPO and General Counsel in the country."

---

## Slide 17 — 90-Day Action Plan (4 min)

**This is the slide they will photograph. Deliver slowly and clearly.**

**Days 1–30 — Understand:**
- Complete one accessible introductory AI course
- Read the NIST AI RMF overview
- Read the DPDP Act and selected Rules
- Study the SC draft AI Regulations
- Learn how hallucination, retrieval and source verification work

**Days 31–60 — Apply:**
- Draft a mock AI-use policy
- Produce a simple DPIA
- Build a legal-AI vendor questionnaire
- Write a verified research memorandum
- Create a Green–Amber–Red AI-use classification

**Days 61–90 — Demonstrate:**
- Combine artefacts into a portfolio
- Present one project to faculty or practitioners
- Publish one well-sourced India-focused note
- Apply to law firms, GCCs, legal tech and consulting organisations

"After 90 days: one course, one mock DPIA, one AI-use policy, one portfolio, applications sent. Low-cost, largely accessible, and possible without waiting for a curriculum revision."

Return to the opening: "The Supreme Court said decisions built on AI hallucination are no decision in the eyes of the law. That ruling did not eliminate AI from legal practice. It made governance the price of admission."

**Read the closing statement slowly:**

> "The profession will need fewer professionals whose value ends with producing the first draft — and more professionals who can verify, govern and defend technology-assisted decisions."
